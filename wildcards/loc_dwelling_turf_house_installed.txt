the national vernacular of Iceland for a thousand years and one of the most intelligent uses of a nearly resourceless landscape anywhere, walls and roof of cut turf blocks laid in courses on a stone footing
in named patterns of which the herringbone is the best known
over a frame of imported or driftwood timber that was the single scarcest material in the country and was salvaged and reused across generations
roofs grassed and grazed and continuous with the ground so the building reads as part of the hill
a plan of several small gabled units joined by a passage into the mound rather than one large volume, because small spans were all the timber allowed
a *badstofa* at the back as the heated communal sleeping and working room
a hearth room with a smoke hole and no chimney in the older form
no windows worth the name
a thermal performance that is genuinely good and a damp performance that is poor
a lifespan of twenty to fifty years before the turf needed renewing which made the house a continuously rebuilt object rather than a permanent one, and an abandonment in the early twentieth century that was almost total
