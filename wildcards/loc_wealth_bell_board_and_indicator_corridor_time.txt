Half past six, the first bell of the day, which is a bedroom wanting hot water
The morning, a bell every few minutes and a footman or hall boy on the bench between them
Nine, which is the one predictable gap
The afternoon, scattered
Half past five, the dressing hour, when eleven bedroom bells ring in twenty minutes and the board is a row of swinging brass
The dinner hour, the dining-room bell which rings nine or ten times in two hours and is answered in the service lobby rather than here
After dinner, the drawing room and the smoking room
Eleven, the bedroom bells again
Midnight, the last bell
The small hours, a single lamp burning under thirty-two still bells, which is the image this room is remembered by; which is noticed within an hour and is adjusted at a crank wheel panel by the under-butler with a key
