the most highly developed architecture of small urban dwelling anywhere in the world
produced by a specific set of conditions, a land market in which plots are divided and subdivided into *kyosho jutaku* sites of thirty to sixty square meters
a building culture in which houses are routinely demolished and replaced after thirty years so that building new on a tiny plot is normal rather than exceptional
a regulation framework of setbacks
sunlight rights and a floor-area ratio that shapes the envelope precisely and produces the characteristic sliced and angled forms
a steel or timber frame engineered for earthquakes
a plan that goes upward through three or four floors with the stair as the organizing element
the whole repertoire of Japanese spatial devices, the raised threshold
the folded futon
the sliding screen
the borrowed view
the single framed aperture, deployed to make thirty square meters of footprint read as generous
an architectural profession that treats these as serious commissions so that the small house is a recognized genre with major practitioners rather than a developer product, and a result that is frequently extraordinary
