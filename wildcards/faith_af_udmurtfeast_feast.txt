at Tulys kis'ton in spring the household lays a long table with round loaves, pancakes, porridge and home-brewed beer, and the elders, a man and woman in their seventies, pour a little of each into a bowl for the dead, calling them by name
at Cheka vos' kin from many houses in their thirties to seventies gather at one table to feed all the ancestors at once, the men in white linen shirts belted over dark trousers, the women in white linen dresses embroidered in red
Udmurt women in their fifties and sixties in white linen dresses embroidered in red at the cuffs and hem, breastplates of silver coins or of linen thick with red silk embroidery on their chests and red and white headscarves, carry bowls of food to the cemetery the next morning
