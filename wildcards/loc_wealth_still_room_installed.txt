the housekeeper's own kitchen
run by a still-room maid who is not answerable to the cook, which makes it the one food room in the house outside the kitchen's authority and is a jurisdictional fact that shows in the plan, placed off the housekeeper's room at record 23 rather than off the kitchen passage, so that the two rooms form a self-contained department; a charcoal stewing stove of brick with sunken rings, which gives a low even heat that a coal range cannot and is what preserving
sugar boiling and chocolate require, and whose flue and ash door make it the one piece of cooking equipment in the house built as masonry rather than bought as ironmongery; a set copper for boiling fruit and for the house's hot water in the smaller establishments; a marble slab on brick piers for pastry
sugar and anything that must be worked cold; a cool recess with a slate shelf on an external north wall, which is the house's refrigeration; a bread oven in several examples
since the still room baked the cakes and the fine bread while the kitchen baked the household loaf; a dresser built in for jars; a stone or tiled floor with a gully; a window with a wire gauze in the lower sash against flies and wasps, which is the one room where that was done; good ventilation at high level because of the sugar steam; a coffee mill and a tea urn
since the tea
coffee and chocolate for upstairs were made here and not in the kitchen, which is the room's most surprising single function; and a bell pull that rings in the servants' hall
