In the style of Ashok Leyland, Indian trucks and buses of the 1960s to 1990s. Trucks have a boxy cab-over-engine front with a flat vertical windscreen split into two panes, a plain rectangular grille with a horizontal chrome bar, wide round headlamps and a high step to the door. The paint is solid blue, red, yellow or cream with thick hand painted decorative borders around the cab, flower and peacock scenes on the mudflap, bold lettering such as horn please or good luck, and strings of lucky charms and tassels hanging at the bumper. Wooden or steel cargo bodies are stacked with sacks, painted with chequered stripes. Buses have a rounded fibreglass or steel body in faded red and cream, sliding windows with metal bars, roof luggage racks and a destination board above the windscreen. The brand badge is a flywheel inside a circle. Mood is rugged, colourful, overloaded and gritty, on dusty highways at golden hour.
