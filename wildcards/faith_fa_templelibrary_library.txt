the library of the Ebabbar, the house of the sun god Shamash at Sippar in Babylonia about 550 BC, a small windowless room of mud brick off a temple court, three of its walls built into a honeycomb of deep niches in ten rows from knee height to above the head, each niche about as wide as a hand is long and as deep as a forearm, holding tablets standing upright on their edges in a row as books on a shelf
the tablet room of the Ezida, the house of Nabu at Kalhu, now Nimrud, in Assyria about 700 BC, a tall plastered chamber opening onto a paved court straight across from the god's shrine, its doorway flanked by stone statues of bearded attendants with clasped hands, wooden shelves along its walls and a stone well in the court outside
the tablet rooms of Nineveh in Assyria about 650 BC, in the house of Nabu beside the palace of King Ashurbanipal, high rooms with walls plastered white and painted with a band of blue and red rosettes, cedar shelves loaded with flat boxes and baskets of tablets, and by the door a stone slab carved with a winged spirit holding a bucket and a cone
the library of the Bit Resh, the house of the sky god Anu at Uruk about 200 BC, a suite of small rooms off a court of blue and yellow glazed brick, the tablets kept in clay jars and reed baskets on brick benches, with star charts drawn as circles divided into wedges on round clay disks leaning against the wall
