in the present day, a reindeer herder in his fifties with a weathered face and gray stubble, in the gákti, a knee-length tunic of royal blue wool trimmed with bands of red, yellow and green at the collar, shoulders and hem, belted with silver studs, kneels and sets a coin, a strip of dried reindeer meat and a splash of coffee from a carved birch cup at the stone
in the present day, Áile, a woman in her forties with long fair hair in a braid, in a long blue gákti dress bright with red and yellow trim and a fringed silk shawl pinned with a round silver brooch, and Ásllat, a man in his forties in the hat of the four winds, a blue cap rising in four stuffed points trimmed with red, lay a small bone and a bunch of heather at the foot of the stone
in the old days, a noaidi, a Sámi shaman, a lean man in his sixties in a tunic of gray reindeer hide, strikes an oval frame drum of pale hide painted with small red figures of people, reindeer and the sun, while men in their thirties to fifties kill a reindeer bull with a knife thrust behind the skull and smear its blood and fat over the stone
in the old days, fishermen in their thirties to sixties in reindeer-skin coats and pointed boots of fur with upturned toes kneel on the shore and rub fish oil over the stone and lay the heads of the first fish of the season, silver and pink, before it
