the car at its most sociable and least ergonomic, which is a combination that has never recurred, a front bench for three with the gear change moved to the column to free the floor
a rear bench for three with a folding center armrest
a dashboard of painted pressed metal with the instruments in a strip and a good deal of chrome
a bakelite wheel of large diameter with a horn ring under the rim
a push-button radio as the period's great optional extra
a cigarette lighter and an ashtray as standard because everybody smoked
a heater that was an option and a demister that was wishful
a vent window in each front door because that was the air conditioning
a bench whose cushion is one piece so nobody has their own seat
no head restraints
no seat belts
a rubber mat at the front and carpet at the back which tells you exactly who was expected to sit where, and a parcel shelf under the dash that everything lived on
