the English country-house stable quadrangle as the type, and architecturally the most ambitious estate building in the whole subject, with the finest examples by Carr, Wyatt, Paine and Burn treated as seriously as a principal elevation
the Scottish version, usually tighter, higher-walled and with more shelter
the Irish version, with a larger establishment relative to the house because hunting mattered more
the French château's écurie, often a single magnificent range rather than a quadrangle and sometimes the grandest building on the estate, as at Chantilly
the Austrian and German Marstall, which at palace scale is a riding school as well and belongs partly in the institutional family
the Spanish and Portuguese versions
the Russian estate stable, with a separate winter arrangement for sleighs
the American version, which from 1890 builds the same quadrangle in the Hudson Valley and on Long Island and converts it to a garage within twenty years
the Indian princely stable, with elephants as well and therefore a wholly different set of dimensions that deserves its own record; and the Arabian and Ottoman traditions, which are where much of the bloodstock and some of the practice came from
