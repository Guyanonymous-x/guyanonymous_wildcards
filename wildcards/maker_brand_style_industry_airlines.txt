In the style of Cathay Pacific, a Hong Kong airline identity in green and white. Aircraft have a clean white fuselage with a thin jade green cheatline running under the windows, a dark green tail fin and a calligraphic brushwing emblem, a single stroke like a swept bird wing curving upward in white or pale green against the dark ground. The earlier version used a lettuce green and white two-tone with a bare metal belly and a small flag on the tail. Titles appear in an upright sans serif wordmark in dark green, with Chinese characters in a small simple script beside it. Cabins are carefully quiet, with warm timber toned panels, bottle green economy seats with pale head covers, flat beds and soft cream leather in business, and thin gold accents. Crew uniforms are crisp, in dark green, with a neat scarf and a small gold wing pin. Mood is calm, urbane, polished, glassy and travelled, set against the harbour skyline and clouds at dusk.
In the style of Korean Air, a Korean airline identity of the late twentieth century. Aircraft have a white lower fuselage and a sky blue upper stripe over a wide silver grey cheatline, with a big round taegeuk emblem in swirling red and blue on the tail fin, a red upper half and blue lower half twisting around each other. The title is a bold rounded serif wordmark in light blue, in a heavy slab-like cut with a quirky character. The later redesign shifts to a dark navy outlined taegeuk and a tidy sans serif, using only the single word. Cabins feature calm light blue and grey seats with white head cloths, warm wood-toned panels, and cream carpeting. Crew uniforms are light blue and white with a neat scarf, pale gloves and a small cap. Mood is orderly, polite, spacious, sky-high and corporate, with cool blue colours and the clean white fuselage on the runway under a cloudless sky.
In the style of Pakistan International Airlines, a South Asian flag carrier identity. Aircraft have a white upper fuselage and a pale beige or off white lower belly divided by a dark green stripe edged in gold, with a large waving national flag in green and white on the tail fin. The airline initials appear in big billboard capitals in dark green on the forward body, with a flowing calligraphic script mark in gold behind the cockpit and on the engine cowls. Special tail liveries feature regional folk art such as blue and white tile floral patterns, fine mosque tile decoration, bright needlework and bold red geometric kilim motifs. Early jets had a thin red and green cheatline. Cabins use deep green seats with gold accents, patterned carpets and scarves worn by crew in green and white. Mood is dignified, national, warm and proud, with green, white and gold as the key colours against bright blue sky.
In the style of IndiGo, a modern low cost airline identity in deep blue. Aircraft have a white fuselage with a large indigo blue lower belly sweeping up toward the tail, ending in a deep indigo tail fin with a lighter sky blue accent stripe, and the small airline name in a rounded lowercase wordmark in indigo on the front body and tail. The logo is made of twenty round dots arranged in the shape of a plane, rendered in white or sky blue over the indigo. Engines and winglets are dark blue with a small white website address. Cabins are bright and simple with slim slim-line seats in blue and white, matching overhead lockers, and cheerful bright staff in navy tunics with an indigo belt. Boarding passes, tickets and ads use a clear white background, a rich blue block and playful, crisp sans serif type. Mood is clean, efficient, youthful, accessible and cool.
In the style of Garuda Indonesia, an airline identity in deep blue and aqua. Aircraft have a clean white fuselage with a swooping blue and turquoise stripe along the belly sweeping into the tail, which is a solid royal blue fin carrying a stylised bird emblem formed from five bent lines, like a spreading wing, in aqua and white. The name is set in clean blue capitals in a wide, evenly spaced sans serif. Earlier liveries have a red and white scheme with a bright orange stripe along the windows and a triangular bird on the tail. Cabins use soft blue seats with batik pattern head covers in brown and indigo, wooden panels and gentle warm light. Crew wear fitted kebaya blouses in blue or brown batik with sarongs, and a delicate flower in the hair. Mood is tropical, gracious, calm, island-like and warm, with turquoise seas and green hills.
In the style of Thai Airways, a Thai flag carrier identity in orchid purple and gold. Aircraft have a white body with a sweeping band from the nose to the tail in shades of magenta, violet and gold, merging into a curved tail fin whose logo is a tall stylised flame like roof curve, a pair of hands pressed together in a traditional greeting, built from gold and magenta. The name is set in a softly rounded italic sans serif in purple. Cabins are decked in purple and orange seats, silk-like fabrics in orchid pink, and patterned carpets, with orchids on the head rests. Crew uniforms are silk wrap skirts and jackets in orchid purple, gold or magenta, with a small orchid corsage and a neat bun. Special liveries use royal barges and classical dancers in gold. Mood is graceful, silky, ornate, warm and festive, with a rich palette of violet, magenta and gold.
In the style of Philippine Airlines, an airline identity in sail triangles. Aircraft have a clean white fuselage with the airline name in italic blue capitals on the forward body, while the tail carries a stylised flag device of a blue triangle and a red triangle, pointing like sails, with a small eight rayed sun in yellow over the blue. The belly is white with a thin red and blue stripe near the windows, and the engines are plain white. A tiny national flag sits near the rear door. Special liveries mark anniversaries with large stylised numbers. Cabins use deep blue seats with red piping and woven patterns, soft light and warm timber. Crew wear modern dresses in blue or red with traditional sleeves. Mood is bright, hospitable, tropical, crisp and patriotic, with blue, red, white and sunny yellow.
In the style of Qatar Airways, a Gulf airline identity in burgundy and grey. Aircraft have a pale grey and white fuselage with the name in large burgundy capitals on the front, the Arabic title nearby in a smaller darker grey, and a tail fin in deep burgundy carrying an oryx antelope silhouette in a pale grey ring with long curved horns. The finish is smooth and polished, with burgundy stripe accents around the engines and a thin line at the belly. Cabins are stately, in grey and burgundy leather, with lie-flat beds in a business suite with a sliding door and soft lighting. Lounges use marble floors, a huge sculpture and leather sofas. Crew wear deep maroon uniforms with a neat hat and veil, and a gold pin. Mood is luxurious, formal, quiet, upmarket and elegant, with deep maroon, warm grey and a gleam of metal.
In the style of Etihad Airways, a Gulf airline identity in sand and gold. Aircraft have a warm pale beige fuselage, almost the colour of desert sand, and on the tail and rear body a triangular tessellation in gold and silver facets, like a faceted gem, fading to the front. The name is set in a golden calligraphic logo mark above the windows, with a small national emblem and a flag near the door. Cabins are sumptuous, with beige and brown leather, gold trim, wood veneer and sliding doors, and a three room residence with a living room, bedroom and shower. Crew wear fitted dark sand-coloured suits and a hat with a flowing scarf. Terminals use pale marble, golden light and sweeping curved roofs. Mood is opulent, desert-warm, polished and glittering, with a limited palette of gold, sand, taupe and white.
In the style of El Al, an Israeli airline identity in blue and white. Aircraft have a white fuselage with a sweeping blue cheatline outlined in silver, running along the bottom, curving up over the nose and fading toward the tail, and a plain white or blue tail with a flag. The name is set in a bold blue wordmark in both Latin and Hebrew script letters across the forward fuselage, and aircraft carry names of cities near the cockpit. The older livery used a turquoise tail with the star of David flag at the top and a turquoise and navy cheatline across the windows. Cabins have blue seats with white head covers and a slim wood-look trim, with crisp blue and white carpets. Crew wear smart navy uniforms with a pale blue blouse and scarf. Mood is crisp, clean, secure, patriotic and cool, in sunlit blue sky.
In the style of Air Astana, a Kazakh airline identity of dark blue and gold. Aircraft have a white fuselage with a deep navy belly and a rich blue tail fin carrying a golden stylised ornament, which resembles a pair of wings or a nomadic motif, and gold lettering on the forward body in a clean wide sans serif. Special liveries wear a snow leopard stretched over the fuselage in grey spots, with white and blue snowy rock. Cabins have navy seats with a pattern of ornamental motifs in gold thread, grey carpets, and warm wood trims. Crew uniforms are smart navy with gold buttons and a silk scarf with a traditional pattern. Mood is steppe-wide, orderly, formal and cool, using navy, gold and white with distant mountains. Printed menus and boarding passes echo the same navy field with a thin gold ornamental border, and lounge interiors repeat the pattern as carved screens and rugs.
In the style of Kenya Airways, an African airline identity in red, white and black. Aircraft have a white fuselage with the airline name in black italic capitals along the forward body, and a bright red tail fin carrying a stylised K inside a circle, in white and black. Older aircraft had four coloured stripes of red, green, black and white running the length of the fuselage in the colours of the national flag. Engines are red or white. A small flag appears near the cockpit, along with the slogan about pride of Africa. Cabins use red and charcoal seats with white head covers, grey carpets, and patterned fabrics with woven motifs. Crew wear red uniforms with black and white scarves and neat caps. Mood is warm, hospitable, proud, bright and savannah-like, with red against clear blue sky.
In the style of EgyptAir, a national carrier identity of ancient mythology. Aircraft have a white fuselage, and a deep blue and gold tail fin carrying a golden falcon god, stylised in a flowing, wing-spread pose with a red head, blue feathers and a gold backdrop. The airline name appears in both Latin and Arabic script in dark blue on the forward body. The older livery had a warm band of red, gold and orange swirling around the window line and engines, giving a sunset glow. Mood is stately, ancient, desert-sunny and glowing, with lapis blue, gold and red.
In the style of Ethiopian Airlines, an African flag carrier identity. Aircraft have a pale grey and white fuselage, with a lower belly painted pale grey and a flowing band of green, yellow and red lines sweeping up toward the tail. The tail fin is deep green with a yellow and red stylised bird or wing emblem. The airline name is shown in Amharic script and Latin capitals in dark green on the forward body. Cabins feature green seats with woven patterned cloth in red and gold, white head covers and wooden panels. Crew uniforms are green, yellow and red with traditional white woven cotton shawls trimmed with coloured borders. Mood is highland-bright, welcoming, proud and colourful, with green, gold and red against clear blue sky. Boarding gates and posters repeat the three colour bands as flowing ribbons, and cargo aircraft carry the same bands on a simpler white body.
In the style of Royal Air Maroc, a Moroccan airline identity of red and green. Aircraft have a white fuselage with a vivid red tail fin carrying a green five pointed star inside a round white emblem, and a slim green and red stripe along the windows. The airline name appears in Latin and Arabic script, in bold dark red letters on the forward body. Cabins use deep red and green fabrics, geometric tile-inspired patterns in white and blue, and brass accents. Crew uniforms are smart dark red with a fine scarf and a decorative pin. Marketing images show blue skies over terracotta walls, tilework in blue, green and white, and palm trees. Mood is warm, hospitable, ornate and sunny, with red, green and white geometry. Brochures and lounge screens repeat the eight pointed star in carved wooden latticework, and ground vehicles at the airport carry a plain red band with the same green star.
In the style of Varig, the Brazilian airline livery of the jet age. The aircraft has a polished bare-metal lower fuselage with a white upper body separated by a thin navy-blue cheatline running along the windows, with the company name in large dark blue capital letters in a clean wide sans-serif above the windows. The tail is a vivid royal or cobalt blue field carrying a large circular compass rose emblem in white and gold with a star-like design, dark rays around a central disc, becoming a gold-yellow rose on a deep blue belly, engines and tail in the later 1990s version. A small green, yellow and blue national flag sits near the rear door, and a short word for the country appears in a thin italic script beside it. Earlier propeller planes show a winged mythical figure in a black circle on the tail and a bold black stripe on the nose. The look is clean, optimistic, glamorous and quintessentially 1960s intercontinental travel, with lots of reflective silver metal.
In the style of Olympic Airways, the Greek airline of the 1960s and 1970s. The aircraft has a gleaming white upper fuselage and polished silver belly divided by a navy blue cheatline with a thin sky blue stripe running along the windows, large dark blue capital letters in a smart modern sans-serif and a Greek script version beside them. The tail is plain white or deep blue with five interlocking rings in a ring emblem in crisp contrasting colours, sometimes stacked as a vertical sign. Cabin staff wore sleek mod uniforms by a Paris couturier, with short skirts in navy, white and sky blue and tall boots. The timetables and posters show sun-washed Aegean scenes, white temples, blue domes, fishing boats, columns, sparkling sea and bright blue sky. The mood is glamorous, sunny and jet-set.
