In the style of Barovier and Toso, Venetian hand-blown glass in lavish, light-filled forms, with delicate curved arms spreading from a central axis and bearing glass flowers, leaves and curling ribbons in clear crystal, rose pink, aqua and amber. Ornate scrolling branches and floral cups sit beside mid-century sculptural versions with cascading rods, tubes and ball drops in white and gold. Glossy, translucent, festive, ornate and glittering like a palace ballroom.
In the style of Tiffany Studios, American Art Nouveau stained glass built from hundreds of small leaded opalescent pieces in rich greens, ambers, deep blues, orange and red, shaped into dragonflies, wisteria, peonies, poppies, dogwood and geometric borders. Bronze forms cast as tree trunks, lily pads or twisting stems with dark green-brown patina, iridescent peacock blue and gold glass, glowing jewel-like light and lush naturalistic mood.
In the style of Louis Poulsen, calm, sculptural Scandinavian restraint where light is softened and glare is controlled. Layered concentric curves, overlapping discs or dozens of overlapping leaf-like plates form smooth symmetrical shapes. Materials are polished steel, chrome, brass, copper and white lacquer with soft warm reflections. Smooth, restrained, elegant and perfectly proportioned, giving a soft diffused glow and a quietly luxurious modernist calm.
In the style of Anglepoise, technical, balanced and flexible engineering shown honestly. Slender jointed members, exposed coiled springs, small pivots and a conical form poised in mid-air like a drawing compass, grounded by a heavy stepped round base. Enamel or powder-coated metal in cream, black, red, grey, racing green and polished chrome. Everything looks adjustable and functional, with a drawing office and workshop atmosphere, lean, precise and a little vintage.
