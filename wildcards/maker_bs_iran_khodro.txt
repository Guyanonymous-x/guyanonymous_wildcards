In the style of Iran Khodro, the national saloon car of Iran in the 1970s to 1990s. The car is a plain four door saloon with a boxy, upright body, a flat bonnet, a small rectangular grille with a horizontal bar, round or square chrome rimmed headlamps, and thin chrome bumpers. Paint is a solid enamel in colours such as cream white, sky blue, pale yellow, red, or green, applied thick and slightly faded, with black rubber door seals, steel wheels with simple hub caps and white-wall style discs. Taxi versions are painted bright orange or yellow with a roof sign, and pickups have a plain steel flatbed. Interiors have a flat dashboard in beige or brown, vinyl seats and a slim steering wheel. Streets in photos show a row of these cars in dusty traffic below mountains. Mood is humble, durable, nostalgic, lived-in, practical and communal.
