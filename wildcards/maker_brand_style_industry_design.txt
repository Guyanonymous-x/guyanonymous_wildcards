In the style of Bauhaus Weimar Workshops, expressionist and early constructivist objects made in wood, metal, glass and textile. Furniture is built from stacked cubes, slats and planes in primary red, yellow, blue, black and white, with bold geometric shapes such as circle, square and triangle. Tapestries are abstract compositions of rectangles and stripes in saturated, earthy reds, blues, ochres and black. Ceramics and teapots are rounded and cylindrical in nickel or plain glaze. Posters use heavy sans-serif lettering, stacked on grids with diagonal bars. The mood is utopian, experimental, geometric and artisan.
In the style of Vkhutemas Workshops, constructivist posters, furniture and architectural models in stark geometry. Colours are red, black, white and cream, with occasional yellow and grey. Compositions place diagonals, circles, wedges and sharp bars at dynamic angles, with heavy angular sans-serif lettering and photomontage. Furniture is made of thin plywood, tubing and simple planes, folded or foldable with exposed joints. Architectural models show stacked boxes, cantilevers, cylinders and glass walls. Prints are flat and high-contrast, with rough printed textures. The mood is revolutionary, industrial, energetic and abstract.
In the style of De Stijl Workshops, furniture, posters and interiors built from straight horizontal and vertical lines and rectangles. Palette is limited to pure red, yellow and blue, together with white, black and grey, set in flat colour blocks with thick black dividing lines. Chairs are assembled from slim rails and flat panels that overlap and project at their ends, painted in contrasting colours with exposed ends. Rooms show asymmetrical balance and open planes. Lettering is blocky and constructed from squares. The mood is strict, rational, abstract and playful in its colour.
In the style of Omega Workshops, hand-painted furniture, ceramics and textiles with bold, loose, post-impressionist decoration. Brush marks are visible, with abstract blobs, stripes, spirals and stylised flowers or figures in strong colours such as mustard, magenta, turquoise, orange, lilac, green and black. Fabrics and screens use irregular, asymmetrical compositions with an unfinished hand-drawn quality. Tables and chairs are plain and painted all over in dense patterns. Pottery has a thick opaque glaze with spots and swirls. The mood is cheerful, avant-garde, amateurish by design and bohemian.
