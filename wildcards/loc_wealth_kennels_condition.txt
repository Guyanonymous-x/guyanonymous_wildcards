the kennel of 1780 with a private pack of thirty or forty hounds kept by one family, a huntsman and two whippers-in, which is an establishment costing as much to run as the stable
the same kennel in 1860 at the height of the subscription era, when a great many private packs became county packs and the kennels either passed to a hunt committee or were abandoned
the kennel in 1900 with the pack kenneled here and the costs shared by subscription, which is the commonest arrangement
the kennel in 1950 with the pack gone, the lodges used for pheasant rearing or pig keeping, which is extremely common
the kennel today as a hunt kennel still in use, which a number are
the kennel as a rescue or boarding kennels, which is a direct continuation and is the commonest commercial reuse
the kennel as holiday cottages, which happens and is odd
the kennel mothballed, limewashed, benches stripped, troughs drained
the kennel open to the weather, where the lodges, the flagged court and the let-in troughs survive because they are stone, and the boiling house copper's brick surround stands like the brewhouse's
