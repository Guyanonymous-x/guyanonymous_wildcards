bare granite mountains of the Hejaz with round boulders, steep ridges of pinkish tan and gray rock rising one behind another, their slopes heaped with huge smooth boulders weathered into balls, domes and stacked piles, a few juniper trees and thorn bushes in the cracks, dry gravel beds in the valleys and a hard clear blue sky above
a road winding down the steep escarpment toward the coast, a narrow highway cut into the side of a great rock wall, looping back and forth in dozens of tight bends as it drops from the cool heights toward a hazy brown plain far below, low stone barriers along its outer edge, baboons sitting on the rocks beside it and bands of cloud drifting across the cliffs
terraced fields of fruit trees on the slopes, narrow level steps cut one above another up the mountainside and held by walls of stacked gray stone, each planted with a row of pomegranate, fig, apricot and peach trees and trellised grapevines, the leaves fresh green against the bare rock, small stone watchtowers standing among the plots
