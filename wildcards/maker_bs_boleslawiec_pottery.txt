In the style of Boleslawiec stoneware from Poland. The body is a thick, dense, glossy-glazed cream or white stoneware with a heavy hand-thrown feel. Pieces include mugs with big strap handles, teapots, pitchers, bowls, plates, jars, butter dishes and egg cups. The decoration is stamped by hand with cut sponges dipped in cobalt blue underglaze, producing clusters of tiny dots, peacock-eye circles, small florals, feathery sprigs, scallops, windmill patterns and rings of leaves in rhythmic repeats. The main colour is deep cobalt blue on a white ground, with a secondary palette of green, yellow, brown, rust and purple used for flowers and borders. The glaze is clear, smooth and shiny with a slightly uneven pooling at the foot, and the rim often has a bold stripe of blue. The look is rustic, cheerful, tidy and cottage-like, familiar from every country kitchen shelf.
