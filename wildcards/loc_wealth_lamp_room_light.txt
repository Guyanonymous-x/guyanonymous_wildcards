The room that makes the house's light and has almost none of its own, which is the irony everybody who has looked at one has noticed, one window giving a flat working daylight onto the bench, which is what the job needs since trimming a wick level to within half a millimeter is fine visual work
A bench position chosen for that window
Ninety glass chimneys on a rack catching and re-emitting whatever light there is, so the rack is the brightest object in the room from any angle
A single lamp of its own at the bench end, lit from the stock, which means this room is always lit by one of the very lamps it services; and at six in the evening in winter, the room's one spectacular condition, forty-eight lamps filled, trimmed, their chimneys and shades on, standing in four ranks on a zinc bench, and the lamp man lighting each one in turn to check it before it is carried out, so that for about four minutes there are forty-eight flames in a 15 square meter room and it is briefly the brightest interior in the building
