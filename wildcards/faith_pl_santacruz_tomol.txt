a tomol, a Chumash plank canoe of redwood boards sewn together and sealed with tar, painted red, a long slender open boat with high pointed ends at both bow and stern, its hull built of many short split planks laced edge to edge with cord, the seams filled with black tar, the whole stained deep red ocher and its tall end pieces set with small disks of shining white shell
a tomol paddled across the channel, the long red plank canoe riding low among rolling blue swells with six adult paddlers kneeling in a line, their long double-bladed paddles dipping on alternate sides and flashing wet in the sun, spray flying from the high bow and the island cliffs fading behind them
a tomol pulled up on the beach, the red plank canoe resting on smooth gray stones just above the waves, tilted a little to one side, its black tarred seams and shell-inlaid ends showing, long double-bladed paddles laid across the seats and wet drag marks leading up from the water
