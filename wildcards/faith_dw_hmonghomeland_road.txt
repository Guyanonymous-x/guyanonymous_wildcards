the soul of a man in his sixties, in a black hemp jacket with an embroidered collar and new shoes woven of pale hemp, walks back along a mountain path past the villages of Laos and Thailand where he once lived, a white rooster walking ahead of him to show the way
the soul of a woman in her seventies stops at the wooden house where she was born, and from the earth floor beside the sleeping place rises her placenta jacket, a soft pale silk coat she puts on for the journey
the dead, men and women in their fifties to their eighties in hemp shoes and dark embroidered funeral clothes, cross the caterpillar river, its banks crawling with fat green caterpillars, and climb the steep green worm mountain, its slopes ridged with grassy folds and wriggling with caterpillars as long as a forearm, the dead treading on them in their hemp sandals and flinging short sticks of hemp stalk into their open jaws to stop them up
at the near edge of the painting the body lies on a bamboo bier in the house, and a qeej player in his forties in black clothes with a red sash dances with the long curved bamboo pipes of his qeej over his shoulder, while the soul's path winds out of the doorway into the mountains
