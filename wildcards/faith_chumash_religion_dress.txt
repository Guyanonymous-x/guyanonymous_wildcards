the dance dress of the Chumash, the people of the coast and islands of southern California, adult men in their twenties to fifties dancing bare above the waist in skirts of long feathers hanging from a cord at the hips, headbands of orange and red flicker quills across the brow, faces and chests painted in red and white
feather skirts made of strings of eagle and hawk down and long wing feathers, the headbands of yellow-orange flicker feathers tied flat across the forehead, worn by men with their bodies painted in stripes and dots
the older dress of Chumash women in their twenties to fifties, bare above the waist in aprons of soft deerskin front and back with fringed edges, shell bead necklaces, the faces painted with red ochre
present-day Chumash dancers at a gathering, the men in feather skirts over shorts with painted chests and flicker-feather headbands, the women in long skirts of deerskin or cloth with shell necklaces
