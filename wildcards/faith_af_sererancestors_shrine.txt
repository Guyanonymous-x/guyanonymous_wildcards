at the foot of the baobab clay pots of millet porridge and gourds of milk are set on the ground, a stump of wood wrapped in white cloth for the spirit, the earth dark where libations have been poured
a man in his fifties cuts the throat of a white goat over the roots of the sacred tree, the blood running onto the stones of the shrine, kinsmen in their thirties to sixties kneeling around him
at the Xoy gathering in the village square the saltigue seers, men and women in their fifties to seventies in red robes and cowrie necklaces, dance and speak the words of the pangool in front of a crowd
the old burial mounds of the Sine, low rounded hills of earth among the millet fields, a clay pot and a cut white fowl laid on top of one of them for the ancestor below
