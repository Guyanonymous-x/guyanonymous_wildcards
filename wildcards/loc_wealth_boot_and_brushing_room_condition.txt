the room of 1800 with a bootboy of twelve or thirteen whose entire job this is, living in the house and paid four to six pounds a year
the same room in 1880 at its busiest, with a shooting party of twelve and forty-one pairs a night, done between nine in the evening and seven in the morning
the room in 1920 with a valet and a hall boy sharing the work and half the party arriving by motor car in shoes
the room in 1950 with the family doing their own boots and the stove cold
the room as a boot room again in a house that shoots
the room as a visitor entrance, a ticket desk or a shop in a house open to the public, which is very common because it is already the point where outdoors becomes indoors
the room in a divided house as a porch or a utility room
the room mothballed, swept, the brushes bagged
the room open to the weather, where the stone floor, the scrapers and the iron jack survive and everything leather and bristle is gone in five years
