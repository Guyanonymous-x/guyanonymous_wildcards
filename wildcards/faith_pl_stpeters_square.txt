Saint Peter's Square, a vast oval square embraced by two curving colonnades of four rows of columns topped with statues of saints, an immense open floor of dark cobblestones crossed by pale stone bands, the two great arms of plain round travertine columns sweeping around it in two wide curves, a long line of white stone figures standing along their flat roofs against the sky
the Egyptian obelisk in the middle of the square, a tall smooth four-sided needle of red granite with bare unmarked faces, standing on a high stone pedestal with four small bronze lions at its corners, topped by a bronze star and cross, with a ring of low stone posts around its base
two fountains in the square, a matching pair of tall granite fountains set to the left and right of the obelisk, each rising from a wide low basin to a stem carrying a great wide domed stone cap, water pouring over its scaled top in a glittering veil
