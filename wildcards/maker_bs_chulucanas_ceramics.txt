In the style of Chulucanas pottery from northern Peru, a pre-Columbian revival ceramic with smoky resist decoration. Pieces are smooth rounded vessels, bottle-necked jars, plump vases, owls, birds, fish, human figures and bowls, thin-walled with softly bulging bodies. The surface is polished to a glossy sheen by rubbing before firing. Decoration comes from a negative resist technique where a liquid clay slip is painted on areas that stay light, then mango-leaf smoke blackens the bare areas in a second firing, giving crisp contrasts of cream, orange-tan, chocolate brown and glossy black. Typical patterns are polka-dot spots, tight rows of dots, checkerboards, stripes, spirals, wavy lines, stepped angles and little fish or leaf motifs, often in two colours only, black on tan or tan on black. Some pieces are smoked solid black or painted in several earth colours. The look is minimalist, tactile, graphic and folk-modern, like a contemporary sculpture from an ancient workshop.
