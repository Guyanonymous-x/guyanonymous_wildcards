the great temple of Sogamoso, called Suamox, in the valley of the holy city, a vast round hall ringed by upright tree trunks each thicker than a man's body, hauled up from the hot plains below the mountains, its wall of split cane plastered with pale clay, its cone roof of layered thatch as tall as a great tree, one low door facing east and a fence of tall close-set posts around its yard
a village temple on the plateau near Bacatá, a round house of posts, cane and clay under a steep cone of gray thatch, twice the size of the dwelling houses around it, a tall red-painted pole standing before its door, a fence of canes around its swept yard and fields of corn and potatoes behind
the Cojines del Zaque at the foot of a hill at the edge of Tunja, two round flat seats of pale sandstone, each a little wider than a man's arm is long, set side by side and facing east over the valley, where the ruler of Hunza came with his priests to kneel and greet the Sun
the sun field of Saquenzipa near Villa de Leyva, two long straight rows of upright pillars of pinkish sandstone, each a rounded shaft chest high set close to the next, many with a ring cut around them, running east to west across a level field so that their shadows mark the turning of the year, and beyond them a group of far taller stone columns carved as upright phalluses, a ring cut below each rounded tip, the tallest well above a man's height, dry rounded hills around
