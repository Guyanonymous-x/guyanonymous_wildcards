the saloon used as a ballroom with the furniture carried out and stacked in the hall, which is how most houses did it and leaves no ballroom at all
the room of 1890 with gas and then electric fittings and a parquet floor laid over the sprung one, which kills the spring and is bitterly regretted
the room requisitioned in 1940 as a ward or a dormitory with forty beds down it and the chandeliers crated in the cellar
the room hired out for weddings, with a bar in the card-room door, a stacking-chair trolley where the dais was and the sprung floor failing in the center circle
the room mothballed, chandeliers bagged in calico and hauled to the cornice, the floor swept and unwaxed and going gray, the mirrors papered over
the room open to the weather, where the sprung floor's void rots from below and the floor becomes the most dangerous surface in the building
