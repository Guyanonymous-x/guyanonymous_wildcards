at Joshi, the spring festival in May, the women and men dance in linked lines on the open dancing ground under walnut trees in fresh green leaf, sprigs of green leaves and yellow flowers tucked into the women's headdresses, and pots of milk shared out, in bright spring sun
at Joshi in May, women in their twenties to sixties sprinkle fresh milk from small wooden cups over the houses and the people in the village lane, everyone wearing new clothes, the valley green with new wheat, in soft morning light
at Uchau, the harvest festival in late August, the herdsmen in their twenties to forties come down from the summer pastures carrying round white cheeses and baskets of walnuts and grapes, and the people dance among the ripe golden wheat fields, in warm summer light
at Uchau in late summer, women and men in their thirties to sixties gather at a stone altar on the hillside to offer bread and cheese, and then dance through the evening on the dancing ground, in the low gold light of a summer evening
at Chawmos in December, a man in his forties cuts the throat of a black goat at the juniper-wood altar of the god Sajigor and sprinkles its blood on a fire and on the altar, the men standing apart around him, the valley white with snow, in cold gray winter light
at Chawmos in December, at night, a long procession of women and men in their twenties to sixties winds down the snowy hillside, each holding a burning torch of pine wood, lit by the torches and a great bonfire
