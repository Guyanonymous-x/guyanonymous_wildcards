a self-contained building in the curtilage of a house
occupied by a member of the same household and legally dependent on it, which is the fastest-growing small dwelling type in several countries, a timber-framed or SIPs building of twenty to forty square meters
usually prefabricated or supplied as a kit and erected in two to six weeks
services run underground from the main house and metered through it
insulation and airtightness to a good standard because the fabric is new
a plan of bedroom
wet room and living-kitchen designed for one or two people and very often designed for age or disability with level thresholds
wider doors and reinforced walls for future grab rails
a planning position that is the governing fact, permitted development may allow the building but not its use as a separate dwelling, so consent is granted for use ancillary or incidental to the main house and frequently conditioned against an oven
a separate entrance
a letterbox or a separate council tax entry
a prohibition on letting it or selling it separately
an occupancy that is a parent
