In the style of Tenango embroidery from the Otomi communities of Hidalgo in Mexico, bold fantastical animal and plant scenes stitched on cloth. The base is usually plain white or cream cotton manta, sometimes black, red or blue, and the whole field is filled edge to edge with flat, outlined figures. Motifs are large stylised birds, deer, rabbits, dogs, horses, roosters, lizards, butterflies and fish, mixed with lush curling flowers, leaves, trees and tiny dots, arranged symmetrically or in mirrored pairs around a central tree. Colours are saturated and not realistic, with sunny yellow, grass green, cobalt blue, hot pink, orange, red and purple, often a single hue per figure or striped bands within one animal. The stitching is dense, tight satin and chain stitch with thick coloured thread, giving a slightly raised, filled-in look with bold dark outlines, as seen on cushion covers, table runners, bedspreads and wall hangings. The mood is folk, cheerful, playful and slightly naive, derived from ancient cave and bark-paper cut-out figures.
