the orangery of 1730 with the trees in, the flue lit from October and a gardener reading two thermometers twice a day
the same building after 1820 when glazed roofs and iron make it technically obsolete and it becomes a sculpture gallery or a garden room with the trees moved to a new glasshouse, which is a very common change
the building in 1900 with hot-water pipes laid along the front on cast-iron brackets and the wall flue abandoned and blocked
the building as a garden store, with the busts gone, two tubs left
the building as a tea room or a wedding venue, with a servery behind the back wall and the flue chamber used as a cable route
the building mothballed, glazing boarded, flags swept, trees sold, doors sealed
the building open to the weather, where the roof fails, the flags survive, the glazing goes within five winters and self-sown ash and buddleia root in the open joints of the floor
