the only principal room in the house tuned rather than decorated, and the one where a decorative decision was overruled by a physical measurement, a volume chosen for its reverberation rather than its proportion
commonly a double cube or close to it
because deep coffering and heavy modeling both kill the upper register; a timber floor left bare
with the carpet taken up for performance, which is the room's one habitual ritual and the reason for the unfaded perimeter strip; no textile wall hanging anywhere, so the room is painted or papered and not silked, which it shares with the dining room for an entirely different reason; a chamber organ built into the inner wall with its bellows and reservoir boxed out behind the plaster and a blowing handle at the case side
requiring a second person who stands with their back to the room for the whole piece; windows on one side only and shuttered during performance; a pair of doors
one to the enfilade and one to a small ante-room where instruments are left to come up to temperature; a chair store, because twenty-four chairs are set out eight times a year and in the way for the rest; two bell pulls; and a flue position chosen so the chimney breast does not sit behind the organ
since a warm wall behind a wind instrument puts it out of tune with itself
