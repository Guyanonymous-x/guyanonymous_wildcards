on the shore stands the dabuyaba, the ancestor temple, a long hut with walls of split palm stems and a high roof of thatched palm leaves, its floor of beaten sand, a table heaped with cassava bread, fish and rum for the ancestors
inside the dabuyaba the buyei, the healer who leads the dugu, a woman in her sixties in a white dress with a red head wrap, shakes a pair of gourd rattles, while three drummers in their forties to sixties play long wooden drums
women in their forties to their eighties in matching dresses of bright print cloth and head wraps dance in a slow shuffling circle in the dabuyaba, and one woman in her fifties, taken by an ancestor, sways with her eyes closed
