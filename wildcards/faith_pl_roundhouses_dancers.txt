Big Head dancers wearing tall headdresses of hundreds of willow sticks tipped with feathers, spreading out evenly in every direction, adult men whose heads are hidden under huge round bristling crowns of thin peeled rods, each rod ending in a tuft of white down or a red feather, with skirts of shredded bark and feathers and a split-stick clapper in each hand
dancers in feather capes and flicker-feather headbands, bands of orange woodpecker feathers, adult men with long capes of dark hawk and turkey feathers tied over their shoulders and down their backs, and long narrow bands of salmon-orange feather shafts edged in black bound across their foreheads and hanging to the waist on each side
dancers circling the fire, a line of adult men and women moving one behind another around the central fire pit with bent knees and short stamping steps, feather plumes held in their raised hands, their shadows passing huge across the roof beams and the curved wall
