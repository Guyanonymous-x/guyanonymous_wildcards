the room of 1875 newly built and in constant use, which for a male houseparty is six hours a day
the same room in 1935 with the lantern leaking and a bucket on the bench
the room with the table sold, leaving eight brick piers in the floor and a rectangle of unworn board, which is one of the most legible absences in the whole document
the room with the table in place and unplayable because the slates have moved after a roof failure and the cloth is damp
the room used as a store for forty years with the table under three sheets of hardboard and a bicycle on it
the room in a house open to the public, where this is usually the tea room because it is big, square, and has a door to the yard
the room mothballed, the cloth brushed and covered, the balls in their box in a safe, the lantern boarded
the room open to the weather, where the lantern goes first and the slates survive in the rubble indefinitely
