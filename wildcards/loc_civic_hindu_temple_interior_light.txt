Two conditions deliberately opposed within one building, and the contrast is the architecture, the hall bright, high and evenly lit from clerestory windows, roof lights or a lantern well above the standing eye line, so the columns and the people are fully visible; and the sanctum almost completely dark, with no window at all, one low door and a curtain across that, so the image stands in blackness and is seen only by flame
The lamp tree and the camphor burner doing all the lighting that matters, raised and circled before the image so that the light moves across it and the gold and silver catch in sequence rather than all at once, which is why the ornament is worked as it is and why a photograph of a lit sanctum looks nothing like the experience of it
A curtain drawn across the sanctum between services, so the building's focal point is literally hidden for most of the day and the drawing back of it is the event
Electric light in the hall, usually too much of it and frequently fluorescent in the converted buildings; and in the afternoon rest period the sanctum curtained, the lamps out and the hall lit only by its windows
