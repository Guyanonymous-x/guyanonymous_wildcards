In the style of the Angeles family workshop of San Martin Tilcajete in Oaxaca, carved and painted copal-wood alebrije animals. Light soft wood is hand-carved with machete and knife into lively mythical creatures such as jaguars, armadillos, owls, dogs, bears, dragons and winged beasts, often with a human face or braided hair on an animal body, caught mid-action while flying, scratching or leaping. Sizes range from palm-sized to over a metre. After carving, a smooth base coat is applied, then the whole body is covered in intricate fine-line ornament in vivid natural-dye and acrylic colours, magenta, turquoise, saffron yellow, orange, leaf green and black, in dense repeating patterns of dots, scrolls, flowers, plants, greek-key-like steps and geometric Zapotec motifs from ancient temple walls. Eyes are large and glossy, tongues and claws picked out in contrasting hues. The finish is a light satin, with every surface patterned and nothing left blank, giving a joyful, hallucinatory, highly saturated folk-art look.
In the style of Wixarika Huichol beadwork and yarn paintings from western Mexico, ceremonial imagery rendered in dense blazing colour. Small glass seed beads are pressed one by one into a layer of beeswax and pine resin that covers carved wooden forms such as skulls, jaguar heads, deer, eagles, snakes, masks, bowls and gourds, leaving a shining mosaic surface with a slightly bumpy glittering texture. Flat yarn paintings use thick coloured wool pressed into a wax board in tight spiralling lines that follow each shape like contour maps. Motifs are deer, maize, peyote buttons, scorpions, five-petalled flowers, sun faces, rain clouds and snake bodies with diamond backs, with zigzag lines radiating from animals. The palette is neon-bright and contrasting, fuchsia, turquoise, lime green, electric blue, orange, yellow, red and black, with white outlines, filled completely so no background shows. The overall effect is psychedelic, symmetrical, intricate and intensely handmade, like a jewelled devotional object.
In the style of the metalworkers of Croix-des-Bouquets in Haiti, flat decorative sculptures cut from recycled oil drums. Panels are made by flattening used steel drums, drawing a design with chalk and then chiselling and punching it with hammer and nail so the line work appears as a pattern of cut-through openings and raised ridges. Subjects are the tree of life, mermaids, birds, fish, sun faces, spirits with elaborate headdresses and crosses, drawn with symmetrical swirling leaves, scales, dotted outlines, hearts and curling vines. The metal is left in dark grey and rust-brown, sometimes polished to a rough silver with burn marks, or hand-painted in bright enamel colours like red, yellow and turquoise. Pieces are wall hangings in oval, round or irregular shapes with ragged cut edges, sometimes bent into three-dimensional animals and candle holders. The look is raw, intricate, spiritual and richly patterned, with a rugged industrial material turned into delicate lace.
In the style of Zhostovo painted metal trays from the Moscow region of Russia. Trays are thin sheet-metal, usually oval, rectangular or heart-shaped with a gently curving raised rim. The ground is glossy black lacquer, though some are red, blue, green or white. A lush bouquet of large garden and wild flowers sits in the middle, such as roses, peonies, tulips, dahlias, poppies, cornflowers and daisies, loosely tossed with leaves, buds and fruit and painted in a few quick full brush strokes with a soft, shaded modelling, in oil colours of deep crimson, pink, orange, yellow, blue and fresh green against the dark ground. A smaller scatter of blossoms and berries is arranged around the main bunch. The rim is edged with gold or silver decorative scrolls, dots and little leaf ornaments. The final surface is polished to a mirror shine under several layers of lacquer. The mood is lush, sumptuous, festive and warm.
In the style of Dymkovo clay toys from the Vyatka region of Russia. Figures are hand-moulded from red clay, fired, then coated in a bright milky-white chalk base that gives a smooth matte porcelain-like finish. They show plump stylised women in long bell-shaped skirts, ladies with parasols, nursemaids, water-carriers, horse riders, cockerels, rams, turkeys, deer and sleigh groups, with simple rounded bodies, tiny heads and exaggerated puffy collars and sleeves. Painting is crisp and playful in bright poster-like colours, red, orange, yellow, blue, green and black, in large circles, checks, diagonal stripes, wavy lines, dots and rhombuses on the dresses, with rosy circular cheeks and small black dot eyes. Details such as hair, hats, tails and horns are picked out in glittering gold leaf. The look is cheerful, flat, geometric and slightly naive, like a patterned doll made of ceramic icing.
In the style of Fedoskino lacquer miniatures from Russia. The objects are papier-mache boxes, caskets, brooches, egg shapes and plaques, coated in layer upon layer of glossy black lacquer, and the inside is often bright red or vermilion. The lid carries a small finely detailed oil painting in realistic style, depicting troika sleighs rushing through snow, a family drinking tea at a samovar, a village winter scene, a fairy-tale princess, a river landscape, a feast, or a bouquet of flowers. The painting uses glazing in many translucent layers so figures glow with soft light, and the backgrounds often have a shimmering gold, silver leaf or mother-of-pearl underlayer which makes the sky glow warmly. Edges may be framed with gold ornament, braids and flowers. The overall finish is mirror-smooth, deep and jewel-like in black, crimson, gold and sky blue. The mood is polished, storytelling and luxurious.
In the style of Petrykivka folk painting from central Ukraine. The decoration is a lavish flat floral ornament painted freehand onto white walls, wooden chests, plates, trays, eggs, boxes and furniture. Flowers are stylised and fantastical, such as large asters, dahlias, poppies, roses and tulips, shown flat from above or side-on, with fern-like leaves, curling tendrils, berries such as viburnum, tiny grape clusters and the occasional firebird, rooster or peacock. Brush strokes are visible and clearly shaped, a comb stroke, a seed, a nut, a teardrop, a tapering curl, and berries are made with a fingertip dab, giving a lighter centre ringed with a darker edge. Colours are strong and clear on a white, black, red, yellow or deep blue ground, with scarlet, cobalt, magenta, orange, lemon yellow and leaf green. Compositions are symmetrical and gracefully balanced, with a lively and celebratory feel.
In the style of the Dala horse of Dalarna, Sweden. The figure is a small stout wooden horse carved from pine in simple flat planes, with a thick arched neck, a short mane cut into notches, a straight stiff tail, four sturdy legs on a flat base and a bulky chest. The body is painted a glossy deep red or orange-red, though some are blue, black, white or natural. The harness, saddle and bridle are decorated by hand with freehand flower scroll patterns in white, green, yellow and blue, with brush strokes in a flower and swirl vocabulary known as kurbits, painted quickly in loops, petals and dots along the neck and flanks. Sizes range from a palm-size souvenir to a giant roadside version. The finish is smooth, shiny and lightly worn, and the look is simple, friendly, folk-rustic and unmistakably Scandinavian.
