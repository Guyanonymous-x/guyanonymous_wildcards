a method of building a wall from firewood, debarked log ends sawn to the wall thickness and laid like stones in two beads of mortar with an insulating core of sawdust and lime between them
pointed back so the end grain stands proud
roofed with a generous overhang because end grain is a straw for water
built almost exclusively by owners because no contractor will price the labor
demanding a tolerance for ongoing repointing, and producing a wall surface that is one of the most visually distinctive in all of building
