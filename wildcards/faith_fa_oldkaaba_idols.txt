around the House a crowd of idols, some three hundred and sixty, rough upright stones, carved wooden posts and small statues of stone and copper, each set on a low base of piled rocks and fixed in place with lead, each belonging to one tribe or clan
the stones of Isaf and Na'ila, two weathered man-high standing stones roughly shaped as a man and a woman, set up near the well facing the House, their bases dark with the dried blood of sacrificed camels and sheep, a camel kneeling before them as a man in his forties cuts its throat, the blood running into the sand
a row of tribal idols at the edge of the open ground, a white stone block, a black stone slab and a wooden post dressed in two cloths and a necklace of shells, heaped with barley, skin bags of milk and locks of shaved hair left by pilgrims
the well of Zamzam a few steps from the House, a round stone-lined shaft with a low rim, men in their twenties drawing water in leather buckets on palm ropes and pouring it into stone troughs and into the pilgrims' skin bags
