a working building on wheels
built so a man could live in the field with the flock for the six weeks of lambing, a timber body on an iron chassis with cast wheels
hauled by a horse and later a tractor and left in the corner of the field with the drawbar pegged
a curved corrugated or boarded roof
a stove for warmth and a kettle and for warming a chilled lamb
a bunk
a window on the field side
a slatted pen under the floor for ewes and lambs needing shelter
a design made by local wheelwrights and foundries in the nineteenth century to a county pattern
almost entirely obsolete by the 1950s
