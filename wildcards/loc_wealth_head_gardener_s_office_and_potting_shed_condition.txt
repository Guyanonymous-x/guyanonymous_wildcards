the range in 1918 with the staff conscripted, the crop book full and women working the garden, which is a documented and important condition
the range in 1930 with the staff at four and half the garden down to grass, which is the beginning of the end
the range in 1950 with the garden let for market growing or abandoned and the pots still on the shelves
the range today as a garden center's potting area, a tea room, a volunteers' room or a working potting shed again in a restored kitchen garden, all of which are common and the last of which is a genuine revival
the range as the shop in a house open to the public, which it very often is
the range mothballed, pots stacked, books to an archive
the range open to the weather, where the lean-to roof fails against the wall first, the bench rots, and the clay pots survive in their stacks indefinitely so that a ruined potting shed is typically found with 900 pots still graded in it
