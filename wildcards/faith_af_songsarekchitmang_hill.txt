Chitmang, a high forested hill in the east, its slopes covered in thick green forest and clouds lying on its crown, the spirits of the dead walking up its path in a long line
Balpakram, the land of the spirits, a high open plateau of grass and scattered trees in the south of the Garo Hills, cut by a deep forested gorge, where the spirits of the dead, men and women in their twenties to nineties in woven wraps, stay for a time before being born again
on Chitmang the dead live as in life, men and women in their twenties to nineties in woven wraps, in bamboo houses among their fields, cooking rice by their fires
a spirit of the dead in the shape of a woman in her sixties comes down from Chitmang as a soft light and passes into a lit bamboo house below, to be born again
a spirit of the dead in the shape of a man in his fifties comes down from the hill and passes as a soft light into a brown hill bull grazing at the edge of the forest
