Kings Park on Mount Eliza, a high bushland park above the river, a long steep bluff rising straight from the river road, its slopes thick with gray-green native bush of banksia, sheoak and tall eucalyptus trees, its top laid out with wide lawns, an avenue of tall gum trees with smooth white trunks, beds of red and green kangaroo paw flowers and a slender stone war memorial at the cliff edge
a lookout over the river and city, a paved terrace with a low stone wall and a metal rail at the edge of the bluff, below it the broad blue river spreading out with ferries crossing, a freeway bridge and green shoreline parks, and to the left the cluster of glass towers of the city rising above the water under a wide clear sky
a walkway through the treetops, a slender bridge of rust-colored steel and glass that curves out from the hillside high among the crowns of the eucalyptus trees, its deck level with the gray-green leaves and pale branches, glass panels along both sides, adult walkers leaning on the rail and the blue river showing between the trunks far below
