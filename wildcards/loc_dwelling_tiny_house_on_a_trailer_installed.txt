a house built to road-legal dimensions so that it is a vehicle in law and a dwelling in fact, a timber frame on a purpose-made trailer chassis
built to a maximum width of about two and a half meters and a height of four, which fixes the plan as a corridor and makes every design decision a consequence of the towing envelope
a weight that must be managed to the kilogram with lightweight claddings and no heavy finishes
insulation to a genuinely high standard because the surface-to-volume ratio is terrible
a sleeping loft because the floor area cannot hold a bed and a living space
services by hook-up
a water tank, solar panels and a composting toilet
a build cost that is far higher per square meter than a house and far lower in total
a legal status that is the central difficulty since most planning systems have no category for a dwelling on wheels and the house may be allowed as a caravan
disallowed as a dwelling or simply unmentioned, and a mobility that is real but exercised far less often than outsiders assume, because moving one requires a large truck and a flat site
