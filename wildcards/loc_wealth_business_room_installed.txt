the room where the estate meets the people it is an estate of, and the only room in the building with two completely separate publics in it at once, placed at the junction of the house and the service court with its own outer door from the back drive or the stable yard, so that a tenant farmer or a tradesman can come in
be dealt with and leave without entering the house, and an inner door to the service corridor and the library at record 7, so that the agent and the owner can reach it without going outside; a counter rail or a low screen across the room dividing it into a standing area of 6-8 square meters on the tenants' side and a working area of 20-25 square meters on the other, which is the two-route rule applied not to servants but to the whole tenantry; a fireproof safe set into a brick-lined recess with its own small relieving arch over it, because the thing in this room with the greatest value is paper; open shelving for ledgers built as fixed joinery
so that the fire warms the desk and reaches the settle barely at all, which is not an accident; a stone or deal floor rather than a carpet; a window on the agent's side giving light to the desk and a smaller window or a borrowed light on the tenants' side; a bell on a bracket inside with a pull outside the door, so that the room can be locked and still answered; a bell pull to the pantry; and a door to the muniment room at record 20, which in the better arrangements is directly off this room and is where everything in the ledgers ultimately ends up
