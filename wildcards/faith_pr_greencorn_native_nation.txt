among the Muscogee Creek of Oklahoma at the busk, their old name for the feast, on a square ground with four brush arbors facing the fire, the men drink a dark cleansing medicine of boiled roots and vomit beside the ground, in the hot sun of a July morning
among the Seminole of the Florida Everglades, at a camp in the pine and palmetto woods, men in their thirties to sixties in long patchwork shirts of bright banded stripes dance around the fire, in the yellow light of a humid summer evening
among the Haudenosaunee, the Iroquois peoples of New York and Ontario, inside a longhouse, a long low wooden hall with a stove at each end and benches along the walls, the faithkeepers give thanks for the corn and the men dance the feather dance in a ring, in the warm light of lamps and late summer windows
among the Yuchi of Oklahoma, on a square ground in the oak woods, men in their twenties to fifties are scratched on the arms with a comb of sharp needles until thin lines of blood appear, a cleansing rite of the feast, in the pale light of early morning
