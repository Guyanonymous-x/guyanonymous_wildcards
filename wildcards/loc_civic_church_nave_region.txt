the English parish church as the type, of which about 16,000 stand and the great majority are listed, which makes this the most numerous historic building type in the country and the one with the least money per square meter
the Welsh and Scottish versions, the latter reordered at the Reformation around a central pulpit with the communion table below it and the seating facing inward, which is a fundamentally different plan and a different record
the Irish versions, Church of Ireland and Catholic, built in different centuries for different congregations
the Scandinavian and north German Lutheran church, which keeps the medieval envelope and fills it with galleries and box pews
the Dutch Reformed church, whitewashed, stripped and furnished with a pulpit and a brass chandelier
the French and Italian parish church, which never had pews in the English sense and is furnished with loose chairs, so the floor reads completely differently
the Spanish and Portuguese versions, with a retable and side chapels
the New England meeting house, which is the box-pew arrangement exported and preserved
the American colonial and frontier church
the Orthodox church, which has no seating, an iconostasis in place of a screen and a wholly different relationship between congregation and sanctuary; and the nonconformist chapel, which inverts the plan around the pulpit
