In the style of the Pomaire potters of central Chile, rustic hand-thrown terracotta cookware and figures. Pieces are heavy, thick-walled and unglazed or lightly burnished, in a warm reddish-brown to dark grey-brown clay with a smooth matte surface that darkens with use. Shapes are chunky and round, with pots with two ear handles, wide shallow bowls for stews, lidded casseroles, jugs, pitchers, plates, candle holders and flower pots. The best-known figure is a plump little three-legged pig money box with a slot on its back, alongside owls, hens, bulls, mermaids and small nativity figures made by pinching and moulding. Some are painted with simple flat colours or white slip dots and stripes, others left plain with finger marks and uneven rims. Kiln smoke gives mottled black clouds and variations. The look is earthy, handmade, humble and warm, and typically seen stacked on rough wooden shelves in an adobe market.
