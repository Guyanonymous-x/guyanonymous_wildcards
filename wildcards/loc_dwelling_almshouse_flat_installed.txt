one of the oldest forms of social housing in continuous operation in Europe
founded by charitable endowment and still run by charitable trustees under the founder's terms, a range or quadrangle of small identical dwellings
historically one or two rooms and now modernized to self-contained flats with kitchen and bathroom
built round a courtyard with a chapel and often a common room
occupied by residents who meet the founder's criteria which may specify the parish
the trade
the religion
the age
the sex and the degree of need and which can be remarkably specific and occasionally obsolete
held under a license to occupy rather than a tenancy which is a weaker legal position and is a live policy question
with a weekly maintenance contribution rather than a market rent
a resident warden or a visiting scheme manager
alarm systems throughout
a strong expectation of mutual neighborliness that is both the great benefit and occasionally the burden
buildings that are very often listed and expensive to maintain from a historic endowment, and a waiting list managed by trustees rather than by a council
