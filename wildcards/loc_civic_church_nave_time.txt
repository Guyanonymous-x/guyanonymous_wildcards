Half past seven on a Sunday, a churchwarden in with a key, the heating on for two hours, the hymn board set, the flowers checked, the pew sheets out
Ten, the service, which for an hour puts between eleven and 180 people in a building sized for 400
Midday, the building empty, the heating off, the temperature falling
The weekday afternoon, the church open and unattended, with a visitors' book, a votive stand and between two and nine people a day coming in alone, which is the use most of these buildings actually get and is almost never counted
Four in the afternoon, a funeral, when the west door is opened and the bier is used
A Tuesday morning, the brass cleaned, the floor swept, the kneelers straightened by two people who have done it for thirty years
A Thursday
A wedding on a Saturday, which is the building's loudest and most crowded hour
A December evening; and the night, locked or in some parishes left open, the votive candles out, the building at outside temperature plus two
