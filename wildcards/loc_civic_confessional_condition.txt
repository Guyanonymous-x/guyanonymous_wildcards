the arrangement before about 1565, which had no box at all, confession was heard in the open church, seated on a stool or a bench with the penitent kneeling beside, in full view of anybody present, and the box is a Counter-Reformation invention introduced precisely to end that; which is where most surviving British examples come from and which was built as part of the original furnishing scheme
the box of 1930 with an electric light and a queue of forty on a Saturday afternoon, which is the type at its most used
the box of 1975 after the Second Vatican Council, when the rite was revised, the face-to-face option introduced and the first reconciliation rooms appeared, so the box stops being the only setting
the box of 1998 with the safeguarding alterations, glazing in, locks out, resited into view, which is the change described here and is the one a visitor can actually see
the box of 2026 used for two hours a week by a handful of people, with the reconciliation room next door used by most of them, and the box kept partly because removing a fitting needs permission and partly because some people prefer it
the box in a church closed and sold, removed whole and sold to a dealer, which is a real trade
the box repurposed as a store, a cleaner's cupboard or a nativity-figure store, which is extremely common; and the box in a church open to the weather, where a hollow oak box with a plush kneeler in it is destroyed faster than any other fitting in the building
