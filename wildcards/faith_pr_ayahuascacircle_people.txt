around the walls sit about a dozen adults in their twenties to sixties, local villagers and foreign visitors together, in loose pale cotton clothes, each on a thin foam mat with a blanket, a small plastic bucket and a roll of paper towel beside them
the people taking the brew are villagers of the healer's own people, men and women in their thirties to seventies with brown skin and black hair, in T-shirts, shorts and printed cotton skirts, sitting cross-legged on palm-leaf mats with their backs against the house posts
the drinkers are Huni Kuin men and women in their twenties to fifties, their faces painted with fine black genipapo lines, the women in bright printed skirts and ropes of colored beads, the men bare-chested, seated in a ring on low benches of split wood
