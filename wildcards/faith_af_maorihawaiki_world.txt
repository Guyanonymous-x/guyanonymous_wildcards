Hawaiki, the homeland of the ancestors, a green island beyond the setting sun, the ancestors in their fifties to nineties in cloaks of woven flax and feathers gathered on its beach to greet the newly dead
Rarohenga, the Māori world below, ruled by Hine-nui-te-pō, the great woman of the night, a tall woman in her forties with long black hair and a dark woven cloak, standing at the threshold of a dim land of the dead
Pulotu, the spirit world of Samoa and Tonga beneath the sea, a land of tall trees and calm water, ruled by Saveasiʻuleo, a great figure with the upper body of a man in his forties and the long lower body of an eel
