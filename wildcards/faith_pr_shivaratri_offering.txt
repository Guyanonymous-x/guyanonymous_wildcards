the linga is heaped with bilva leaves, the three-lobed leaves of the bael tree, white trumpet-shaped datura flowers and garlands of orange marigolds, milk running down its dark sides into the spouted basin
a priest in his fifties with a shaved head and a white thread over one shoulder bathes the linga in turn with milk, curd, ghee, honey and sugar water, the five nectars, and wipes it clean with a white cloth
above the linga hangs a copper pot with a tiny hole in its base, dripping water drop by drop onto the stone all through the night, small oil lamps burning around the basin
a priest in his forties smears the linga with gray ash and draws three white stripes across it, then circles a brass lamp of many flames before it, the devotees pressing forward with raised hands
