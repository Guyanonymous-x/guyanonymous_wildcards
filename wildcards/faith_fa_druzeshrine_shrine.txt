the shrine being Nabi Shu'ayb near Hittin in the Galilee, a large building of white and cream limestone with long rows of arched windows and small domes, stepped up a terraced hillside below a twin-peaked hill, looking down toward the Sea of Galilee, and inside, beside the tomb, a hollow in the bare rock held to be the prophet's footprint, into which pilgrims in their thirties to seventies pour olive oil and rub it on their hands and brows
the shrine being Nabi Ayyub, the prophet Job, above the village of Niha in the Chouf mountains of Lebanon, a low building of pale stone with an arcade of pointed arches and a small white dome, the five-colored Druze star of green, red, yellow, blue and white set on its roof, an open viewing terrace behind it, standing alone on a high rocky crest among scrub oak with the land falling away on both sides to deep valleys
the shrine being Nabi Sabalan on a mountaintop above the village of Hurfeish in the upper Galilee, a walled compound of pale gold limestone with a white dome and a wide paved terrace, ringed by thick oak and pine woods, with a long flight of stone steps climbing to its gate
the shrine being Nabi Habil, the prophet Abel, on a bare mountain ridge west of Damascus in Syria, a square building of pale stone with an arcaded porch and a dome, set on the very edge of a steep drop above a narrow green river valley, its paved court edged with a low wall
