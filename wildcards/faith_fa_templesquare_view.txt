the Salt Lake Temple seen from the east across the plaza, a tall narrow building of pale gray granite with three spires at each end, the middle spire of the east end the highest and topped by a gold-leafed statue of the angel Moroni in a long robe blowing a slim trumpet, rows of tall arched windows and round windows up its sides and its image doubled in a long reflecting pool
the Tabernacle, a long oval hall as large as a sports arena under a smooth rounded roof of dull silver metal shaped as the shell of a turtle, the roof resting on a ring of red sandstone piers with doors between them, and through an open door the gold pipes of its great organ rising behind tiers of choir seats
the Assembly Hall in the southwest corner of the square, a small gray granite church in the Gothic style with a steep roof, pointed stained glass windows and a row of slim white spires along its eaves, and in front of it the Seagull Monument, a round granite column topped by a stone ball on which two gilded bronze gulls are landing with raised wings
the whole square seen from the corner gate, the gray temple spires rising over the trees, the silver curve of the Tabernacle roof to one side, straight paths between raised flower beds and the glass and stone office towers of downtown Salt Lake City standing close behind
