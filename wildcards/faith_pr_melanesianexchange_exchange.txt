a moka of the Mount Hagen people in the highlands of Papua New Guinea, rows of live pigs tied by one leg to stakes and kina, gold-lip pearl shells cut into crescents and mounted on boards of red resin, the givers men in their twenties to sixties in tall plumes of bird of paradise feathers, faces painted red, black and yellow, and wide aprons of woven string
a Kula exchange of the Trobriand Islands, the visitors stepping ashore from big outrigger canoes with carved and painted prow boards, the gifts mwali, armbands of white cone shell hung with small shells, and soulava, long necklaces of red shell disks, held out in both hands, the people men and women in their twenties to seventies in grass skirts and short wraps
a Tolai exchange of shell money in New Britain, great wheels of tabu, strings of tiny cut shells bound in thick rolls as tall as a seated person, laid out in rows, the people women and men in their thirties to seventies in bright cotton laplaps, wraps around the hips, and printed shirts
a grade-taking feast in northern Vanuatu, rows of boars with long curling tusks tied to posts, and a man in his forties in a woven mat wrap and a cycad leaf in his armband clubs one boar on the head with a heavy wooden club as it falls, the people women and men in their twenties to seventies in mat skirts and penis wrappers of woven pandanus leaf
