the Portuguese Synagogue of Smyrna, the port city on the Turkish coast, in December 1665, a square hall with whitewashed walls, a painted wooden ceiling and bright carpets on the floor, the reader's platform ringed by a turned wooden rail, where Sabbatai Zevi, a tall full-bearded man of thirty-nine in a white robe and a white turban, reads from an open Torah scroll and calls up his brothers and then women of the congregation in turn, the hall packed with men in their twenties to seventies in long striped robes, sashes and dark turbans
the synagogue of Gaza, the town on the coast of Palestine, on the night of the feast of Shavuot in May 1665, a low vaulted stone hall lit by hanging oil lamps, where Nathan of Gaza, a thin pale man of twenty-two with a short dark beard, lies stretched on the stone floor in a trance with a white cloth laid over his face, his outer robe thrown aside, as a ring of scholars in their thirties to sixties in dark robes and white turbans bend over him
the synagogue of the Portuguese Jews of Hamburg, the German port, in 1666, a paneled upper hall with tall windows of small glass panes and brass chandeliers, where the elders of the congregation, merchants in their fifties to seventies in black coats, wide white collars and broad black hats, dance in a ring around the reader's platform with Torah scrolls in their arms, the scrolls dressed in embroidered velvet mantles and crowned with silver bells
the synagogue of the Portuguese Jews of Amsterdam on the Houtgracht canal in 1666, a long hall with wooden galleries on columns down both sides and rows of brass candle crowns, where men in their thirties to sixties in black coats and white collars, prayer shawls over their hats, hold newly printed prayer books open at a picture page showing the messiah seated crowned on a throne flanked by lions, with women in their twenties to sixties in dark gowns and white caps crowding the gallery rails above
