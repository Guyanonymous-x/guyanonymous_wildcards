the chapel of 1680 with a chaplain in the household and daily prayers attended by all forty servants, which was a condition of employment
the same chapel in 1850 with the chaplain gone, prayers once a week and the gallery curtain usually drawn
the chapel in 1900 with the family attending the parish church instead and the chapel used for christenings and funerals only
the chapel as a store, with the benches stacked against the wall, the font full of flowerpots and the gallery used to keep apples
the chapel still consecrated in a house sold, retained by deed with a right of access through somebody else's hall, which is a real and common arrangement
the chapel in a mothballed house, the linen and plate in a bank, the harmonium cased, the ledger stones under sand and boards
the chapel open to the weather, where the plaster barrel comes down onto the benches and the ledger stones outlast everything else in the building
