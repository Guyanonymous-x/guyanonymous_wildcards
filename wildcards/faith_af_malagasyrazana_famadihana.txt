at the famadihana the bodies of the dead, wrapped in old dusty shrouds, are carried out of the tomb on the shoulders of men in their twenties to sixties, the crowd dancing around them
women in their thirties to seventies sit on mats with the bodies of their dead across their knees, rewrapping each in a new lambamena, a shroud of white silk, and speaking to them
men in their twenties to fifties dance with the wrapped bodies lifted on their shoulders as a hira gasy band plays brass horns and drums, the new shrouds shining white in the sun
before the tomb a man in his forties cuts the throat of a zebu, a humped cow with long horns, its blood running into the red earth, its meat for the feast of the dead
