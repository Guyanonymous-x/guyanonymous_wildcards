the Pelourinho, the old town, cobblestone streets of colonial houses painted pink, blue, yellow and green, with baroque churches, rows of narrow two- and three-story houses with white-trimmed windows, wrought-iron balconies and red tile roofs sloping down a hill of rounded dark paving stones, and at the end of the street a church front of carved pale stone with twin bell towers
a steep street climbing between colorful houses, a narrow lane of uneven dark cobblestones rising sharply between old townhouses in turquoise, mustard yellow, coral and lilac, tall shuttered windows and small iron balconies above, strings of small colored paper flags zigzagging overhead from wall to wall
the Lacerda Elevator, a tall art deco tower linking the upper and lower city, a slim pale concrete shaft with clean vertical lines rising from the harbor streets up the green cliff face, joined at its top by a covered bridge to the high square above, with the blue bay and the red roofs spread out below
