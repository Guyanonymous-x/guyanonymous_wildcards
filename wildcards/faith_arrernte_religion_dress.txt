the ceremonial dress of the Arrernte, the Aboriginal people of the desert country around Mparntwe, Alice Springs, in central Australia, adult dancers in their twenties to sixties painted in red and yellow ochre and white pipeclay in bands, circles and dots over the chest, arms and thighs, the men bare above a hair-string belt and a small tassel at the front
bodies rubbed with red ochre and grease until the dark skin glows red-brown, then marked with white lines and rows of white down stuck on with plant resin, worn by men in their thirties to sixties bare-chested in short loincloths for the men's dances
women's ceremony dress, adult women bare above the waist with their breasts and shoulders painted in curving lines of yellow and white ochre, skirts of cloth or string at the hips, painted headbands across the brow
present-day Arrernte dancers at a public gathering, the men in red cloth wraps, the women in long skirts and painted tank tops, all with white ochre dotted across the face, arms and legs
