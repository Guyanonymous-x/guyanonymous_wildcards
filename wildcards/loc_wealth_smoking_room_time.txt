Nine in the morning, a housemaid in with the window wide and the frieze flaps open for an hour, the matting swept, the ashtrays emptied, the brass done
The rest of the day, the room shut and unused and colder than anywhere except the chapel
Six in the evening, the fire lit
Half past ten, the women gone up, the men arrive, jackets changed at the door
Eleven to one, the room at its purpose, which is conversation of a kind not had in any other room in the house
One in the morning, the fire guarded, the lamp out, the window left open two inches whatever the weather, which is standing instruction
The morning after, and a valet taking the jacket off the hook to air it
