on February 2nd, the feast of Iemanjá, crowds with flowers on the beach, thousands of adults dressed in white and pale blue packed on the sand and the seawall, holding white roses, tall flower spikes and woven baskets filled with blossoms, small mirrors and perfume bottles, while fishing boats hung with blue and white streamers carry the baskets out over the sparkling water
at night, the terreiro lit, warm yellow light pouring from the open doors and windows of the dance hall across the dark yard, bare bulbs strung between the trees, candles flickering at the doors of the small shrine houses and white-clothed figures passing in the glow
in the bright tropical sun, the houses colorful, hard midday light from a deep blue sky making the pink, blue, yellow and green walls blaze, the white window trim dazzling, short black shadows under the balconies and the cobblestones pale with glare
