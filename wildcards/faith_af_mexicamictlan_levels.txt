two great mountains of bare brown rock clash together and part again across the road, the dead man in his fifties running between them as they close
the dead climb a steep hill of obsidian, its slopes bristling with black glassy blades as long as a man's forearm, their feet and hands cut and bleeding as they climb
in the land of the obsidian wind a howling gale of black glass shards sweeps the plain, slicing the dead and tearing their cotton mantles away in strips
in a dark land jaguars with yellow spotted coats spring on the dead and tear open their chests to eat their hearts, the dead walking on afterward with open wounds
