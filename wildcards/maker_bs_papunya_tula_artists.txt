In the style of Papunya Tula Artists of Australia, acrylic dot paintings of the Western Desert. The canvas is covered by dense fields of small round dots of paint applied with sticks, creating a shimmering, optical texture. Compositions are mapped from above, as sacred sites, with concentric circles, U-shapes for seated people, curving lines for travel paths, meandering parallel lines of water, tracks of animals and spreading wavy bands. The palette is earthy ochre, with red-brown, burnt orange, yellow, ochre, charcoal, black, white and cream, and later more vivid pink and blue. The background is often dark, and dots are dense and layered to hide secret meaning, with the picture reading either as a map or as an abstract field. The painted surface is matte, with slightly raised dots. The look is hypnotic, symmetrical, rhythmic, very graphic and very earthy, resembling aerial views of red desert country.
