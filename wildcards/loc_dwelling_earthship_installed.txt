a building type invented in New Mexico in the 1970s and built by owners in some numbers since
designed to need no mains services at all, load-bearing walls of used car tires packed with rammed earth
laid like bricks and rendered in thick curved plaster
bermed on three sides so the earth holds the building at a stable temperature
a full-height glazed face to the equatorial side acting as a solar collector and a greenhouse
roof catchment to a cistern with filtration for drinking
graywater taken from sinks and showers into interior planters and then to a flush
blackwater to an exterior botanical cell
photovoltaic array with a battery bank and no grid connection
a wood stove as backup heat
ventilation by skylight and operable glazing with no mechanical system, and a performance that is excellent in a high desert and considerably more compromised in a cold wet maritime climate, which the honest owners will tell you
