below, in the courts of the underworld, the Ten Kings sit behind red-draped desks in black officials' hats with long side wings, ledgers open before them, the souls of the dead in white mourning clothes led before them in chains
Ox-Head and Horse-Face, the underworld guards, tall men with the heads of an ox and a horse, drag sinners to their punishments, souls stripped to white loincloths sawn in half between boards, thrown onto a hill of upright knives, boiled in a cauldron or having their tongues pulled out with iron pincers
at the last court the dead cross the Naihe Bridge over a river of blood, and Meng Po, a woman in her eighties in a plain blue jacket, ladles her soup of forgetting into bowls before they go on to be reborn
the city of the dead stretches out in streets of tiled houses and shops just as on earth, clerks of the underworld in their forties in black robes counting the paper money as it arrives in flames
