in the present day, men and women in their thirties to sixties with brown skin and black or graying hair, in kīhei, shoulder capes of tan kapa bark cloth knotted at one shoulder, over plain shirts and pā'ū, wrapped skirts of printed cotton, lay ho'okupu on the stones, small bundles of smooth gray beach stones and cooked food wrapped tightly in glossy green ti leaves and tied with leaf strips, and a woman in her forties sets one gently on the lele
in the present day, practitioners in their twenties to fifties, the men in the malo, a long strip of red or brown kapa cloth wrapped between the legs and around the waist, buttocks bare, and the women in long brown kapa pā'ū skirts, all with leis of twisted green ti leaves on their heads, offer whole roots of 'awa, the knobby gray-brown root of the kava plant, and coconut cups of the muddy brown 'awa drink
in the old days of the temples, kahuna, priests, men in their forties to sixties with brown skin and gray-streaked black hair tied up, in white kapa malo and white kapa capes, set a whole roasted pig, its skin dark and glossy, on the lele rack beside bunches of green bananas, coconuts and red fish laid on ti leaves
at the Makahiki, the old harvest season of the god Lono, men in their thirties to fifties in brown kapa malo, bare-chested, carry up the steps the tribute of the land, rolled bolts of pale kapa cloth, bundles of feathers, baskets of sweet potato and taro, and bowls of gray poi, the thick paste of pounded taro
