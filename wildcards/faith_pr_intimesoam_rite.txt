at Otavalo in Ecuador, on the night before the dancing, men and women in their twenties to sixties wade into the cold pool under the Peguche waterfall in the dark, dipping their heads under the falling white water to cleanse themselves, lit by flashlights and the moon
at Cotacachi in Ecuador, rival bands of men from the upper and lower villages stamp in circles into the town's main square, the toma de la plaza, the taking of the square, pushing and shouldering for space before the white church
at Tiwanaku in Bolivia, crowds of Aymara men and women in their twenties to eighties raise both open palms toward the first rays of the rising sun at the Gate of the Sun, a great carved stone gateway, at dawn on the twenty-first of June
