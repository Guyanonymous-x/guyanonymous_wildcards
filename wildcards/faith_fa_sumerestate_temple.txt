the Eanna, the house of the goddess Inanna at Uruk about 3200 BC, a wide precinct of great halls and courts on a low terrace above the city, the walls and the thick round columns of one court covered from top to bottom with a mosaic of thousands of small clay cones pushed into the plaster, their flat ends painted red, black and white to make bands of zigzags, diamonds and triangles
the Temple Oval at Tutub, now Khafajah on the Diyala River, about 2600 BC, two thick oval walls of gray-brown mud brick one inside the other, a single gate, a wide oval courtyard with a round well and stone basins in the middle, a row of small workrooms and storerooms built against the inside of the wall all the way around, and at the far end a brick platform taller than a man with a stair up to the shrine
the precinct of the moon god Nanna at Ur about 2050 BC, a great walled court at the foot of the ziggurat, a solid stepped tower of baked brick in three stages with three long straight stairways meeting at a gate tower on its front, and beside it the square storehouse block with its thick walls, a single guarded door and long narrow magazine rooms
the house of the god Ningirsu at Girsu about 2100 BC, a precinct of plastered mud brick with tall niched gate towers, the gateposts topped with standards of the lion-headed eagle with spread wings, a broad court where carts of barley are unloaded, and dark stone statues of the ruler Gudea standing with clasped hands along the walk to the shrine
