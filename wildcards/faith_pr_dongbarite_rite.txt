at a funeral, the dongba unroll the road to the gods, a very long narrow scroll of cloth painted with the hells at one end, then the earth, then the heavens and their gods at the far end, laid along the ground from the coffin draped in red cloth toward the door, to guide the soul of the dead adult home
at a healing, a sick woman in her fifties sits wrapped in a blanket on a low stool, and a dongba walks around her waving a bundle of pine and cypress twigs, while another priest stabs a small figure of dough with a knife to drive the sickness demon out
at a spring in the forest, a row of narrow wooden plaques with pointed tops stands stuck into the wet earth, each painted with a shu, a figure with a human body and a coiling green snake tail, and before them lie bowls of milk, butter, grain and burning juniper twigs
at a riverbank where trees were felled, the dongba set out small heaps of grain, eggs and white stones on fresh green leaves, and burn bundles of resinous pine and juniper as smoking incense, to apologize to the shu for the harm done
