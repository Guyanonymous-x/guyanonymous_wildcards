the garden of 1760 of half a hectare feeding a household of thirty
the same garden extended in 1820 to 1.2 hectares with a hot wall, forcing pits and a glasshouse range, which is the great expansion
the garden of 1870 at its absolute peak, with a staff of fourteen to twenty, forty frames, a glasshouse range at record 53, which is the type at its most extraordinary and most expensive, these gardens cost more to run than they saved and everybody involved knew it
the garden of 1918 feeding a hospital or a convalescent home in the house
the garden of 1930 with the staff at four and two quarters down to grass, which is the beginning
the garden of 1950 with the walls standing, the glasshouses derelict and the whole enclosure grassed or let as a market garden or a nursery, which is overwhelmingly the commonest fate
the garden of 1990 as a caravan site, a parking lot, a Christmas-tree plantation or a housing development inside the walls, all of which happened in numbers
the garden today restored and worked by volunteers with the frames glazed again and the fruit retied, which is a real and growing movement
the garden mothballed, walls repointed, quarters mown
the garden open to the weather, where the walls stand for centuries and everything inside them is gone in fifteen years, so a walled kitchen garden full of nettles and self-sown sycamore with 2,400 nail holes in one wall is one of the commonest ruins in the English landscape
