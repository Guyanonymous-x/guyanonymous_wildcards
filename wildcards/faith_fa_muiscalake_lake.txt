Lake Guatavita, a small perfectly round lake of dark green water lying deep in a steep-walled crater among high green hills northeast of Bogotá, its sides clothed in low trees and shrubs down to the water
Lake Guatavita on the day a new chief is made, the small round crater lake of dark green water among high green hills, and out on the water a raft of bundled reeds carrying the new chief, a man in his thirties, wholly nude, his penis and scrotum visible, small and relaxed, his copper-brown skin coated from head to foot with sticky resin and fine gold dust so that he shines yellow, a heap of gold and emeralds at his feet, four lesser chiefs in gold crowns, nose plates and ear discs standing around him and four clay braziers smoking at the corners
the lake of Iguaque, a small cold lake of black water high on the open moor under bare gray rock ridges
the lakes of Siecha, three dark lakes lying in a row in rocky hollows scooped out by ice, under a jagged ridge of gray cliffs, tawny moor grass and cushion plants around their shores
Lake Tota, the largest lake of the Muisca highlands, a wide sheet of deep blue water ringed by rounded green and brown mountains, with a long beach of white sand at one end and beds of tall reeds along the shore
the lake of Ubaque, a small still lake of green-brown water in a bowl of steep forested mountains east of Bogotá, reeds and water plants along its edge and cloud hanging on the slopes above
