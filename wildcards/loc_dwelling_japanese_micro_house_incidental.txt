a *genkan* entrance step with shoes left on the lower level and the floor raised at the threshold, which is a universal rule of Japanese domestic space and is the first thing the plan accommodates no matter how small the house
a futon folded into a cupboard by day so the bedroom becomes another room, which is the oldest spatial trick in Japanese housing and is what makes a tiny plan work
a deep *ofuro* soaking tub which is often the most generous single element in the whole building because the bath is culturally non-negotiable and will be fitted even when the kitchen is three meters long
a double-height void over the living space, which is the one luxury a narrow plan can afford and is almost always spent
a staircase occupying a quarter of every floor, which is the arithmetic of building on thirty square meters and is the type's defining cost
a car slotted under the ground-floor overhang because a parking space is required to register a vehicle in most Japanese cities and is therefore part of the brief
a high clerestory window because every lower window would look at a neighbor's wall a meter away
a single tree in a gravel strip at the front, which is a complete garden
