the French cabinet de toilette, which is the model and which includes the bidet as a matter of course
the English dressing room, which adopts the arrangement and the furniture and is coy about the bidet in print while buying them steadily
the Scottish version, with a smaller closet and a bigger press
the Italian version, often two small rooms rather than one
the Russian version, attached to a bathhouse rather than a hip bath, which is a materially better arrangement
the Ottoman and Indian versions, built round a hammam or a bathing court with piped or drawn water and a far higher standard of washing than anything in a British house of the same date; and the American Gilded Age dressing room, which from 1880 has plumbing and therefore never develops any of this
