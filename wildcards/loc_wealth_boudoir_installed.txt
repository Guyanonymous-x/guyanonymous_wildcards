the smallest named room in the state sequence and the only room in the house that one person controls absolutely, which is the whole point of it, placed off the drawing room or the principal bedroom
usually in a corner or a turret or over a porch, so it has two aspects and a view, and is reached through one other room rather than off a corridor, which is what makes it private; a low ceiling by this house's standards
and a small plan, because a small room can be warmed by a small fire and that is the only way one person gets to be warm in this building; a cooktop grate rather than a register
burning a fire of two or three shovels; walls papered or hung in a light printed cotton and hung with miniatures
silhouettes and drawings rather than oils, because the scale suits them and because these are personal objects rather than inherited ones; a window seat; shutters; a door that locks, which is rare in the principal rooms and is the second point of the room; a second door or a jib door to a back stair in many examples, giving access to the nursery floor or to the garden without passing through the state rooms; one bell pull hung low; and no concealed service door, because this room is cleaned by one maid at a fixed hour and otherwise not entered
