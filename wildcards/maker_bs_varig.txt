In the style of Varig, the Brazilian airline livery of the jet age. The aircraft has a polished bare-metal lower fuselage with a white upper body separated by a thin navy-blue cheatline running along the windows, with the company name in large dark blue capital letters in a clean wide sans-serif above the windows. The tail is a vivid royal or cobalt blue field carrying a large circular compass rose emblem in white and gold with a star-like design, dark rays around a central disc, becoming a gold-yellow rose on a deep blue belly, engines and tail in the later 1990s version. A small green, yellow and blue national flag sits near the rear door, and a short word for the country appears in a thin italic script beside it. Earlier propeller planes show a winged mythical figure in a black circle on the tail and a bold black stripe on the nose. The look is clean, optimistic, glamorous and quintessentially 1960s intercontinental travel, with lots of reflective silver metal.
