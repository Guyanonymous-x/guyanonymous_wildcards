Ereshkigal sits on a throne of lapis lazuli, a woman in her forties with deep brown skin and black hair in a heavy knot, wearing a crown of four pairs of horns and a pleated fleece robe, her minister Namtar, a gaunt man in his fifties, standing at her side
Ereshkigal is drawn as the goddess of the Burney Relief, a tall winged woman shown wholly nude, her skin painted red, her pubic triangle softly shaped, her wings of red, black and white feathers spread down from her shoulders, a horned crown on her head, her feet the clawed talons of an owl standing on the backs of two white-bodied lions with black manes, a rod and ring held up in each hand, an owl perched at each side
Ereshkigal sits before the seven Anunna judges, gods in their sixties in horned caps and long curled beards seated in a row, who fix their eyes of death on the newcomer, and on the wall behind them hangs the green, swollen body of the slain goddess Inanna from a hook
