the dead crowd the dusty courts in long garments of gray-brown feathers, men and women in their forties, sixties and eighties with gaunt pale faces, scooping dust and lumps of clay into their mouths from shallow bowls
in the dim streets the dead of every rank sit in rows against mud-brick walls, a king in his fleece skirt and gold helmet, a soldier with his spear, a weaver with her spindle, all in their thirties to seventies and coated in fine gray dust, feathers on their arms
near the throne lie the dead of the royal death pit of Ur, rows of attendants in their twenties to forties laid side by side in red robes and headdresses of gold leaves, the small cups they drank from at their hands, the grooms beside the oxen and a harp at their feet
