the temple of Amun-Ra at Karnak in Thebes, the largest of all, its pylon as wide as a town square and higher than a ten-story building, reached from the river landing by a paved road lined on both sides with crouching stone sphinxes with the heads of rams, each holding a small figure of the king between its forelegs, and behind the pylon the tips of two red granite obelisks sheathed in bright gold
the temple of Amun at Luxor, its pylon carved with the king in his chariot scattering his foes, two red granite obelisks standing before it with gilded tips, two colossal seated statues of King Ramesses II in the striped headcloth beside the doorway and four standing ones along the towers, and four cedar flagpoles taller than the pylon clamped in its grooves, long red and white pennants streaming from their tops
the temple of the falcon god Horus at Edfu, its pylon carved on each tower with a giant figure of the king, Ptolemy XII, grasping a bunch of kneeling enemies by the hair and raising a mace before Horus and the goddess Hathor, a winged sun disk above the doorway, and on either side of the door a statue of a falcon in dark gray granite wearing the tall double crown, as high as a man
the temple of King Ramesses III at Medinet Habu on the west bank at Thebes, its pylon behind a high gate tower built as a fortress, the pylon reliefs painted in strong red, blue and yellow, and through the doorway a court with a row of square pillars, each fronted by a standing statue of the king wrapped as the god Osiris with crossed arms
the temple of the goddess Isis on the island of Philae in the Nile at the southern border of Egypt, reached by boat, a long open court between two colonnades leading to the pylon, the columns topped with carved bunches of palm leaves, papyrus and lotus painted green, blue and red, and granite boulders and date palms along the water's edge
