the Scottish version, better charged because the lochs freeze harder
the Irish version, which struggled with mild winters and often imported
the French glacière, frequently a conical above-ground structure with a thatched roof, which is a different and in some ways better design
the Italian and Spanish neveros and pozos de nieve, which store compressed snow from mountains and supplied whole cities commercially, at a scale that dwarfs anything domestic here
the Persian yakhchāl, which is the ancestor of all of it and makes ice rather than merely storing it, using shade walls, evaporation and a radiative pond, and is a more sophisticated piece of engineering than anything in this record
the Russian and Scandinavian versions, which barely need a building
the American ice house and the New England and Hudson River ice trade, which industrialized the whole business and shipped lake ice to Calcutta; and the commercial brewery, fishmonger and dairy ice houses in every nineteenth-century town
