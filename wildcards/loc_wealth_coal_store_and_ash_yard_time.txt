Five in the morning on a delivery day, a cart in the yard, the plates lifted and four tonnes tipped down a chute in forty minutes, which is the noisiest event in the service court and is heard in the attic bedrooms at records 42 and 43 directly above
Six, the scuttles filled, and this is the day's real work, which for a winter day is between forty and ninety separate carries up a back stair
Seven, the ash from last night's fires brought down in pails, sifted, the cinders put aside and the ash tipped into the pit
Mid-morning, the yard swept and hosed
The afternoon, the second scuttle round for the evening fires
Four, the lamps' oil, the stable's coal and the laundry's coal all drawn from the same bays and tallied
The evening
A Saturday, the sweep's cart, the soot barrel filled and dated
A quarter day, the weighing machine and the coal merchant's account checked against the tally board, which is the one moment the board is read rather than written
