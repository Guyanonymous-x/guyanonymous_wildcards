the Toraja of the Sulawesi highlands in Indonesia hold a Rambu Solo funeral, a man in his thirties cutting the throat of a black water buffalo with a long machete in a field of mourners in black, tongkonan houses with boat-shaped roofs behind, painted in the red, black and white carved patterns of Toraja woodwork
on a cliff above the rice terraces of Toraja stand rows of tau tau, carved wooden figures of the dead in their sixties to nineties in real clothes, their arms held out, coffins tucked into the caves beside them, painted in rich earth colors
the Ngaju Dayak of Borneo hold a tiwah, men in their twenties to fifties in bark cloth and feather headdresses spearing a buffalo tied to a carved post, the washed bones of the dead set in a painted sandung house on stilts, painted in Dayak curling hornbill patterns
the Sora of Odisha in India kill a buffalo and plant a tall gray stone for the dead, women in their twenties to seventies in white cloth with brass jewelry dancing around it, drawn in the manner of Sora ittalan wall paintings, white stick figures in rows on red earth
the Ifugao of the Philippine mountains seat the dead man in his seventies on a wooden death chair under his house, wrapped in a woven blanket, kin in their twenties to sixties in loincloths and wrap skirts beside him, painted as a present-day Philippine painting
the Naga of the hills of northeast India and Myanmar lay the dead in their forties to seventies on a raised bamboo platform with their spears and shields, painted in red and black Naga carved patterns of hornbills, mithun heads and human figures
the people of Sumba in Indonesia drag a huge flat stone slab on wooden rollers to make a tomb, men in their twenties to fifties in ikat cloth hauling ropes, the stone tombs of the village beside high-peaked houses, painted in deep indigo and red
