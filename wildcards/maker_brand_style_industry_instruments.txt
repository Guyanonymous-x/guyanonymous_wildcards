In the style of Fender, American electric guitars and amplifiers of the 1950s and 1960s. Offset double-cutaway or single-cutaway slab bodies with sleek contours, sunburst finishes fading from yellow to red to black, or solid Surf Green, Daphne Blue, Fiesta Red and cream. White or tortoiseshell pickguards, chrome knobs and bridge plates, maple necks with black dot markers, and a bold curved script logo on the headstock. Amplifiers have tweed or black vinyl cabinets, silver grille cloth, chrome corner trim and a row of cream or black knobs. Mood is bright, twangy, sunny California.
In the style of Gibson, American electric and acoustic guitars. Carved arched-top bodies with a rich, glossy cherry red or golden sunburst finish, cream binding around the edges, and a flamed maple top. Bold mother-of-pearl block or trapezoid inlays on a dark rosewood fingerboard, gold-plated hardware, twin chrome-covered humbucking pickups, four gold barrel knobs, and a swept-back headstock with a pearl inlay logo and crown shape. Mood is rich, warm, heavy and luxurious.
In the style of Marshall Amplification, British guitar amplifiers. Large black vinyl-covered cabinets with a woven basket-weave cloth grille in tan or black, trimmed with gold or silver piping and a bold script logo in white on a gold or black plastic panel at the top. Control panel with a row of chunky black skirted knobs with white numerals, a pilot light in a red jewel, and a rocker switch. Stacks of tall rectangular speaker cabinets set in a wall on stage. Mood is loud, stacked, rock and roll and imposing.
In the style of Moog Music, analogue synthesizers. Solid wood cabinets in dark walnut or black with a slanted control panel in black or silver, packed with chunky round knobs with white pointers, patch cords and jacks, small rocker switches, and printed white labels in a clear sans-serif. A long keyboard with white and black keys and a pitch ribbon. Colour accents are red, orange and white lines grouping the sections. Mood is warm, vintage, scientific and handcrafted.
In the style of Roland, 1980s Japanese electronic instruments. Dark grey or black angular metal and plastic cases with wide control surfaces, rows of tiny coloured plastic buttons in orange, red, blue and cream, slim sliders and small red LED displays. Drum machines have rows of small rounded square pads in red, orange, yellow and white. Printed labels use fine white lettering with grouped panel lines. Synthesizers have long keyboards, wedge-like profiles and a bold stripe with the model name. Mood is techy, club-ready and boxy.
In the style of Yamaha Synthesizers, 1980s digital keyboards. Long slim cream-white or black plastic bodies with flat membrane buttons in grey and a small blue-green backlit LCD display screen, slim data-entry sliders, and a full-length velocity keyboard. Silver lettering and a minimal printed grid of function labels, rounded edge corners and a three-tuning-fork logo. Mood is sleek, digital and early-electronic with soft grey and teal tones.
In the style of Teenage Engineering, Swedish electronic music gear. Flat, thin, rectangular aluminium or white plastic slabs with exact grids of small coloured rubber buttons in orange, grey and white, tiny round knobs and bright pixel displays. Orange and neon accents, silkscreened white and black technical lettering in a small monospaced sans-serif, minimal diagrams and fine line graphics. Rounded sleeves and playful pastel-coloured carry cases. Mood is clean, geeky, graphic and playful.
In the style of Korg, analogue synthesizers and rhythm machines of the 1970s and 1980s. Instruments are long, shallow wedge shaped keyboards with a slanted metal control panel, brushed or anodised charcoal and silver, covered in rows of small round knobs, chunky slider caps and tiny coloured lever switches in orange, red, blue and white. Labels are screen printed in clean white or orange sans serif capitals with thin line diagrams of waveforms, envelopes and signal routing, and a row of patch sockets sits at the upper left. Side cheeks are dark stained wood or black vinyl clad. Keys are cream white and matte black with a firm square profile. Small red LED digits and amber or green indicator lights glow on the panel. Drum machines are compact grey boxes with rows of tiny rectangular rubber pads and a small knob bank. Mood is functional, technical, compact and precise, with a slightly futuristic hi-fi flavour of the late 1970s and the cool grey of 1980s digital workstations.
