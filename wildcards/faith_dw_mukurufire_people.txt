the fire keeper, a headman in his sixties with deep brown skin rubbed with red ocher, a cloth wrapped on his head and a short leather skirt, kneels by the fire and speaks to the ancestors, a bowl of fresh milk in his hands
Himba women in their twenties to their fifties sit near the fire, bare above the waist, their skin and long thick braids coated in red otjize paste of ocher and butterfat, leather skirts at their hips, heavy white shell pendants on their chests
Hugo, a Himba man in his forties in a wrapped cloth skirt and a headwrap, his chest bare, brings a calf to the fire to show it to the ancestors, Amara, a woman in her thirties with ocher-red braids, standing behind him
