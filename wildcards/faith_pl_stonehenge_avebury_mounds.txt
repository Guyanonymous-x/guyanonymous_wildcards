Silbury Hill, a huge round chalk mound, an enormous flat-topped cone of earth and chalk covered in smooth green grass, as tall as a ten-story building and perfectly regular in shape, rising alone from flat water meadows beside a small stream, far larger than the trees and the road at its foot
West Kennet Long Barrow, a long mound with a stone entrance, a low grassy ridge of chalk and earth stretching along a hilltop in a long smooth rounded green hump, its eastern end closed by a row of huge upright gray sarsen slabs, with a dark narrow passage between them leading into small stone-walled chambers roofed with great flat slabs
The Sanctuary on Overton Hill at the far end of the Avenue, a flat grassy hilltop where two rings of short concrete posts mark the holes of old circles of stone and timber, the downs rolling away all around
