the French boudoir, which names the type and treats it as an architectural set piece with carved paneling and a painted overdoor
the English boudoir, plainer, more of a working room and more likely to be papered
the Scottish version, in a turret
the Italian version, which is a camerino attached to a bedroom apartment
the Russian version, which is one of a suite of small heated rooms behind the state enfilade and is where the family lived all winter
the American Gilded Age morning room, which is the same room scaled up until it stops being private; and the Ottoman and Indian equivalents, which organize female privacy at the scale of a whole wing rather than a single room and deserve their own records
