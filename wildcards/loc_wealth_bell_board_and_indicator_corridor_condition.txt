the house of 1760 with no bell system at all, where a servant waits in an ante-room or outside a door and is summoned by voice or by a hand bell, which is the arrangement that makes record 10's levee and record 2's standing footman necessary and is worth understanding as the alternative
the house of 1800 with the first crank-and-wire system, which is a genuinely transformative piece of domestic technology because it is what allows servants to be moved out of the principal rooms and down into a basement, so the bell board is the precondition for the entire two-route plan being pushed to its final form
the board of 1860 at thirty-two bells and its fullest extent
the board of 1895 with an electric indicator beside it and both running
the board of 1920 with the mechanical system dead, nine wires cut and the indicator doing the work
the board of 1950 with a staff of two and a single bell in the kitchen
the board surviving complete in a house open to the public, which is very common because it is unsaleable, interesting and nobody ever needed the wall, and is now one of the two or three most-photographed objects in any country house
the board with the bells gone to a dealer and thirty-two bare spring mounts and thirty-two labels left, which is the saddest version
the board open to the weather, where the pine and the paper go and the brass is stolen within a year
