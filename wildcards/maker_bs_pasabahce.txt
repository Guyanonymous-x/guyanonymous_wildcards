In the style of Pasabahce, Turkish glassware with an Ottoman flavour. The icon is the tulip shaped tea glass, a small, slim, waisted glass with a flared lip and a gently pinched middle, clear and thin, set on a small saucer with a tiny spoon and a ruby coloured tea. Larger services include stemmed coffee cups, jugs and pitchers with cut and pressed patterns, such as diamond facets, rosettes, fine vertical flutes and rows of tiny dots. Coloured glass appears in cobalt blue, emerald green, amber and ruby red, with gilded rims and enamelled floral motifs of tulips, carnations and cypress trees in gold and white. Tumblers are heavy with a round base, and carafes have domed lids and tall necks. Photos show tea trays with sugar cubes on a patterned cloth, brass samovars, and carpet in the background. Mood is hospitable, bright, tidy, domestic and sparkling, with glassy reflections and warm light.
