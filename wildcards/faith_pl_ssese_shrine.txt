the shrine of Mukasa on Bubembe, a large round house of reeds and thatch in a grove of old trees, a great cone-shaped building whose thick roof of layered dry grass sweeps almost down to the ground, the low wall beneath it made of neat rows of upright pale reeds bound with dark fiber bands, a wide low doorway at the front, and a swept yard of bare earth under enormous fig and mahogany trees
a shrine house with spears, drums and paddles leaning by its door, a round thatched house with a reed wall, and beside its dark entrance a row of offerings left by lake travelers, long iron-bladed spears with wooden shafts, tall cone-shaped drums with cowhide skins laced on with strips of hide, and several carved wooden canoe paddles with leaf-shaped blades propped upright against the reeds
bark cloth, brown cloth beaten from fig tree bark, hung in the doorway of the shrine, a wide soft sheet of rust-brown and terracotta-colored cloth with a slightly felted, uneven surface and visible seams where strips were joined, hanging as a curtain across the low entrance of the round reed house and stirring a little in the breeze from the lake
