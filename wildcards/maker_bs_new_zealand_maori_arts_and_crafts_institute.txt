In the style of New Zealand Maori Arts and Crafts Institute, ornately carved wooden meeting house posts, panels and canoe prows covered in dense curved relief. Figures have large heads with wide eyes inlaid with paua shell, protruding tongues, three-fingered hands and bodies decorated with spirals, interlocked koru curves and notched ridges. Wood is dark, often totara, polished brown, with red ochre pigment and details of white and black. Lattice panels and woven reed work, such as tukutuku panels in diagonal patterns, fill the spaces between the posts. The mood is ancestral, powerful, sculptural and rhythmic, with continuous flowing curves.
