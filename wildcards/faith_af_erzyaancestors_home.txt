in the log house on the fortieth day after a death, the dead man's new clothes lie in the red corner, the holy corner across from the stove, a white linen shirt, a woven belt and a pair of shoes plaited from strips of lime-tree bark, a thin wax candle burning above them
the memorial table is set with round loaves, pancakes, a bowl of thick oat pudding and a jug of beer, and the oldest woman of the house, in her seventies, calls each ancestor to the table by name, the door standing open for them
an Erzya woman in her fifties sits weeping on the bench, in a white linen tunic embroidered in red and black at the shoulders and hem, the pulai, a heavy hip ornament of beads, coins and black wool tassels, hanging at the back of her waist
