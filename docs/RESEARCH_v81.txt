RESEARCH NOTES v81 (2026-11-04)
Four research passes, each with web searches and Wikipedia and other page fetches: real-world robots (about 83 entries), fictional robots from film, TV, games and literature (about 86 entries), handheld and wearable devices (about 115 entries) and computers and peripherals (about 111 entries). Each file ends with a PATTERNS section (recurring parts: heads, eyes, torsos, limbs, locomotion, finishes, screens, bezels, keys, ports, wear) and a SOURCES list of the URLs actually fetched.
How it was used: the PATTERNS lists and the entry descriptions set the part lists for the 25 robot types and 42 gadget types. Names of real or fictional robots and devices were kept out of all prompts; they appear only in the maker_ reference files.
Rules applied: every part fully described by its look; any text on a screen, label or marking written in quotation marks, one line each; invented unit names only; colour words removed from body, head and limb descriptions so the colour scheme sentence is the only colour source.
Weak spots: Wikipedia pages rarely state colours or bezel details, so many colours come from general knowledge (tagged [mem] in the notes); about half the later gadget entries and some fiction entries are thin; in the maker_ files some fields read awkwardly because they are built straight from the notes; robot and gadget parts are picked independently, so a few combinations are odd (a tiny device placed in an oversized setting).
