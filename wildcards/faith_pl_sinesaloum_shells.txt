Fadiouth, an island of white seashells joined to the mainland by a long wooden bridge, its streets paved with shells, a low crowded island village built on centuries of heaped clam and cockle shells, narrow lanes of crushed white shell running between pastel plastered houses with shell fragments set in their walls, and a slender plank footbridge on hundreds of wooden pilings crossing the shallow green lagoon to the shore
a cemetery of crosses and Muslim graves on a hill of white shells, a small rounded island mound made entirely of bleached shells, crowded with white-painted Christian crosses and with low Muslim graves marked by plain headstones and iron plaques, great gray baobab trees rising among them and the shallow lagoon and mangroves visible on every side
granaries on stilts standing in the water off the shell island, a cluster of small thatched storage huts with walls of woven reeds and pointed straw roofs, each perched on tall thin wooden poles above the shallow green lagoon, their reflections trembling beneath them, with mangroves behind and a narrow dugout canoe tied to one of the poles
