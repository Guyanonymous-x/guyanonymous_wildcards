in Ethiopia, Ethiopian Orthodox faithful in their twenties to eighties, women and men alike wrapped in the netela, a thin white cotton shawl with a narrow colored border woven along its edge, sit on low stools in the compound of a rock-cut church at Lalibela, sharing injera, a wide soft gray flatbread full of tiny holes, spread with red lentil stew and yellow split peas, in the hot gold light of mid-afternoon
in Egypt, Coptic men and women in their twenties to seventies, the women in long dark dresses with lace scarves over their hair and the men in plain shirts and trousers, a small blue cross tattooed on the inside of each right wrist, sit at long tables in the hall beneath a Coptic church in Cairo, in the white light of fluorescent tubes and the bright noon sun at the high windows
in Armenia, Armenian Apostolic faithful in their thirties to eighties in dark wool coats and knitted scarves stand at long tables in the courtyard of a church of pinkish volcanic stone with a cone-shaped dome, sharing lavash, a thin soft flatbread folded in sheets, with fresh herbs and bean soup, in the cold clear light of a March afternoon
in Kerala in south India, Malankara Orthodox Christians in their twenties to seventies, the women in white saris with gold borders and the men in the mundu, a white cloth wrapped at the waist and falling to the ankles, with white shirts, sit on mats on the floor of a church hall eating rice and vegetable curry from banana leaves, in the warm green light of a monsoon afternoon through tall open windows
in the Tur Abdin hills of southeast Turkey, Syriac Orthodox villagers in their forties to eighties, the women in dark headscarves and long skirts and the men in jackets and flat caps, break the fast at dusk at tables in the stone courtyard of the Mor Gabriel monastery, its yellow limestone walls glowing in the last orange light of sunset
