and the eldest man, in his eighties, lifts both arms toward the white peaks with his palms open, the other elders raising their staffs and fly whisks after him
and an elder in his sixties pours honey beer and milk from a gourd onto the roots of the fig tree as an offering, while the others bow their heads toward the mountain
and two elders in their sixties kill a fat ram of a single color for the sacrifice, holding it on its side on the grass and smothering it by clamping its mouth and nostrils shut with their hands, the old Kikuyu way that keeps the blood inside the body, before it is skinned and its meat roasted on a fire beneath the tree
and an elder in his seventies dips a bundle of green leafy twigs into a gourd of water mixed with the half-digested grass from the sacrificed ram's stomach and sprinkles it over the bowed heads of the people as a blessing
