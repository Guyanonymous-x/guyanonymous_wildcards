the English butler's pantry as the type, with the press bed beside the plate safe as a near-universal arrangement in houses of any size between 1750 and 1914
the Scottish version, identical
the Irish version, identical
the French office du maître d'hôtel, which separates the plate from the sleeping arrangement and keeps the silver in a strong room
the American butler's pantry, which from about 1880 becomes a serving pantry between kitchen and dining room with a dumb waiter, two sinks and a great deal of built-in cupboard, loses the plate safe and the bed entirely, and is the direct ancestor of the modern fitted kitchen
the Russian and German equivalents, which keep the strong room; and the hotel and club pantry, which standardizes the whole arrangement and is where most of the fittings were bought from
