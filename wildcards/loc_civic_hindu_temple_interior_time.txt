Six in the morning, the building unlocked, the sanctum curtain drawn back, the image woken, bathed in water, milk, honey and sandalwood with the used liquid running out through the channel, dried, dressed in the day's silks, garlanded with fresh flowers and offered the first lamps
Half past seven, the first service of six, with between four and forty people
Mid-morning, a steady few, mostly individuals walking the circumambulation and bringing flowers from the counter
Midday, an offering of food
The early afternoon, the curtain drawn and the rest period, when the building is open, the hall is lit and the sanctum is closed, which surprises every first-time visitor
Four, the curtain back
The evening service at about seven, the best attended of the weekdays, with lamps, bell, conch, drum and cymbals and the recitation on the loudspeakers
Half past eight, the last lamps, the image undressed of its heavy ornament, offered rest, and the curtain closed
A festival, when the building holds ten times its weekday number, the silks and the silver come out of the safe, the kitchen runs all day and the parking lot is the limiting factor
A Sunday, the busiest ordinary day in Britain because of the working week, which is a local adaptation rather than a traditional pattern and is worth noting
