above the earth rise the twelve heavens as curving bands of cloud and light, one above another, and in the highest, Te Toi-o-nga-rangi, the topmost band of pure white light, some Maori teachers set Io, the supreme one, shown as a blaze of light, its entry guarded by his messengers, shining figures of men and women in their thirties to fifties
Ranginui, the Sky Father, spreads across the top of the scene as a huge dark blue figure of a man in his sixties, his body covered with stars and his tears falling as rain onto the Earth Mother far below
Tane, god of the forest, a man in his thirties with a tattooed face, stands on his head with his feet pushing Ranginui up into the sky, holding the sky and the earth apart so that light could enter the world
