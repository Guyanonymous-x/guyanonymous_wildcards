beyond the hall the Duat stretches away in dark bands, the sun god Ra with the head of a ram standing in his boat under a canopy, towed through the night by gods in their forties hauling ropes over their shoulders
along the lower edge of the scene the great serpent Apep, the enemy of the sun, coils in long loops of green and yellow, pinned to the ground and cut through by knives held by the gods standing over it
in the lowest band of the Duat the enemies of Ra kneel in rows with their arms bound behind their backs, and the headless bodies of the condemned lie in a pit of red flames, their severed heads stacked beside it, all drawn plainly in red and black
past the judgment the Field of Reeds opens out, a green land cut by blue canals where the justified dead, a man and a woman in their forties in white linen, plow behind a pair of dappled cattle and cut tall golden wheat
the gates of the Duat stand one behind another, each a tall doorway guarded by a gatekeeper god with a knife in each hand and the head of a snake, a lion or a crocodile, a rearing cobra breathing fire on the lintel
