round-bellied clay pots of clear river water standing in a row on the altar, their dark surfaces dusted pale, with dashes and dots of white chalk marked all around their rims and shoulders
white chalk, called orhue, the pure white river chalk of the Edo, in rough lumps and molded cones heaped on a shallow white enamel plate, fine white powder dusted over the plate and the cloth around it
cowrie shells, small glossy white sea shells with a toothed slit down one side, scattered loosely over the white altar cloth among a few coins and strung in short strands hung over the necks of the pots
