the farmstead of 1740 as a barn, a byre and a yard with no plan to speak of
the model farm of 1790-1840, rebuilt as a formal quadrangle with a horse engine, a granary and a published plan, which is the type described here and is a genuine agricultural and architectural movement
the farm of 1870 at its most productive, with a portable steam engine hired for threshing, eleven dairy cows, forty bullocks and the railway taking the produce
the farm of 1918 at full production again
the farm of 1950 with a tractor in the cart shed, the horse stable empty and the byre condemned by the milk regulations, which is what actually ended most of these buildings
the farm of 1980 with the ranges too small for modern machinery and a steel portal-framed shed in the field beyond, which is the universal pattern
the farm today as barn conversions, which is overwhelmingly the commonest fate and has preserved more of this architecture than anything else
the farm still farmed, with the quadrangle used for storage
the farm mothballed
