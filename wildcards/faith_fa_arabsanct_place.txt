the place being the Kaaba at Mecca about the year 600, a low roofless box of rough dark stones laid dry on the floor of a bare valley between rocky hills, its walls a little higher than a tall man and hung with striped cloths from Yemen, a black stone set in its eastern corner, the image of the god Hubal inside, a man's figure of red carnelian with a right hand of gold, small idols and upright stones of many tribes standing in a wide ring on the open ground around it, and pilgrims of the outside tribes, men in their twenties to forties, walking around it wholly nude, each with his penis and scrotum visible, while the townsmen walk clothed
the place being the sanctuary of the goddess al-Lat at Ta'if, a green highland valley of vineyards, orchards and walled gardens, where she lives in a square block of white rock as high as a man's chest, hung with necklaces and bright cloths, under a small flat-roofed house of stone kept by the tribe of the town
the place being the sanctuary of the goddess al-Uzza in the valley of Nakhla east of Mecca, three old acacia trees with spreading thorny crowns standing together on dry gravel, hung with strips of cloth and weapons, a small stone house beside them, and before it a pit in the ground stained dark where the blood of the offerings runs
the place being the sanctuary of the goddess Manat at Qudayd on the Red Sea shore, a rough black rock taller than a man standing alone on pale sand between the sea and a line of dark hills, with men of the oasis tribes, in their twenties to fifties, seated before it having their heads shaved bare at the end of their pilgrimage
the place being the sanctuary of Dhu al-Khalasa at Tabala on the road to Yemen, a block of white quartz as high as a man's waist with the shape of a crown cut into its top, set on a platform of stones inside a low square wall on a hillside of thorn scrub
the place being the High Place above Petra, the rock city of the Nabataean Arabs, a mountaintop of rose-red sandstone cut flat by hand, with a shallow sunken court, a raised altar block reached by steps, a round basin with a drain cut in the rock to carry off the blood, and nearby the black squared block of the god Dushara standing on a base covered in beaten gold
the place being the Awwam temple of the moon god Almaqah at Marib in the land of Saba in the south, a great oval wall of fitted limestone blocks on the edge of the desert, entered through a hall fronted by a row of eight tall square pillars of single stones, their faces cut with rows of sharp angular marks, with bronze figures of worshippers on stone bases along the walls
