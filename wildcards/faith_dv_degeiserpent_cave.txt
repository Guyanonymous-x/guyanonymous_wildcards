on a ledge beside his head perches Turukawa, his bird, a dove with soft gray-brown feathers and a pale breast, who wakes the god each morning, and before him his son, a man in his thirties in a bark cloth wrap
around the cave mouth hang vines and tree ferns, and before him kneels a couple in their forties with deep brown skin and black hair, the man in a bark cloth wrap and the woman in a fringed skirt, holding clay pots tight to their chests
inside the cave the walls are dark and damp, a stream running from it, and on the slope below a small village of tall thatched houses with steep peaked roofs stands among coconut palms
