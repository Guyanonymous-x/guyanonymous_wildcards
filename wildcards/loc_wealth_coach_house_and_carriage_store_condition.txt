the coach house of 1780 with a traveling coach, a post chaise and a chariot, and a coachman whose status in the household was that of an upper servant
the same building in 1860 with five vehicles and a specialized one for every purpose, traveling, town, station, shooting, which is the arrangement here and represents the type at its fullest
the building in 1905 with the first motor car in the end bay, a gas store outside, an inspection pit dug in the floor and the dead-level drainless floor now a liability, which is the change that destroys the type
the building in 1925 as a garage with three cars and one dog cart kept for sentiment, which is an extremely common and very specific condition
the building in 1950 with the carriages broken up or sold and the bays holding a tractor and a trailer
the building today as garaging, holiday lets, a tea room or a vintage-vehicle store, and in a number of estates as a carriage museum displaying the very vehicles that were sold off and bought back
the building mothballed, vehicles sheeted and chocked, doors barred
the building open to the weather, where the carriages go within fifteen years because they are varnished wood and leather, and the brick floor and the door openings survive indefinitely, and a coach house with its roof off is the fastest total loss of value in this entire document
