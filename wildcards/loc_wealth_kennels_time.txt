Half past five, the huntsman or the kennel man in, the lodges opened to the court, thirty-four hounds out, the benches stripped and fresh straw down
Six, the court hosed, which happens twice a day without exception
Seven, the boiling house lit if there is flesh to do, and a boiling takes four hours and is done two or three times a week
Eight, the pack into the grass yard and looked over individually, which is the day's real skill, thirty-four animals checked for feet, coats, condition and lameness by one man who knows every one of them by name and by voice
Mid-morning, the feed, which is once a day for a working pack and is the loudest four minutes on the estate
Midday, the lodges swept out beneath the benches
The afternoon, exercise on the road, which is the pack out at walk with two men for two hours
Four, the flesh house, the meal bin, the medicine cupboard
Six, the hounds in, the lodges shut, the court hosed again
Nine, one lantern and thirty-four hounds settling
A hunting morning in November, the yard at four, the horn, and the whole establishment away by six
