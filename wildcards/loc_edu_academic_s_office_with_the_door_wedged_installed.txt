a tutorial room and a place of pastoral contact, and which is held by one person for perhaps twenty years, which makes it the most personalized room in this entire family and the only one where the occupant's own history is the dominant content; a door that can be wedged, which is the room's working signal and in many departments has entirely replaced a formal appointment system, so the wedge is a piece of social infrastructure; a desk set at right angles to the window rather than facing the door, so the occupant turns to a visitor rather than confronting them across a desk, which is the same arrangement as the head teacher's office arrived at for the same reason; two visitor chairs, because a tutorial is one or two students; shelving on every wall that is not a window or a door, which is the room's defining physical fact and is the reason these rooms are never large enough; a window that opens
often a sash in an older building; a vision panel in the door in newer buildings and none in older ones, which is a live safeguarding question in universities and is usually resolved by the wedge rather than by glazing
