Himba women in their twenties to forties, their skin and hair coated in otjize, a deep red paste of red ochre and butterfat, are bare above the waist, wearing short pleated goatskin skirts, heavy white shell necklaces and thick metal coils at the ankles, their hair in long thick red ochre-covered plaits
a married woman in her thirties wears the erembe, a soft leather headpiece shaped in three points on top of her head, red ochre glowing on her skin and her bare breasts, a heavy iron-bead necklace at her throat, kneeling at the grave
a headman in his fifties with dark skin wears a cloth wrap tied over his head and a pleated skirt of cloth at the waist, a long carved staff in his hand, and he stands at the holy fire speaking to the ancestors
Himba men in their twenties to forties with dark skin, wearing short skirts of cloth and leather and strings of beads at the neck, drive a herd of humped cattle with long curved horns past the grave
