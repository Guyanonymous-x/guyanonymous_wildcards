the lodge from the plaza, a broad earth dome about as wide as twelve tall men lying head to foot, the largest building in the town, twice the width of the household lodges beside it, its front flattened into a straight wall of upright timbers, a covered entrance passage of logs and earth jutting out, and green willow boughs stacked over the door
the roof of the lodge, grass sprouting from the packed earth, townspeople in their twenties to sixties in painted buffalo robes seated and standing all over the dome to watch the plaza, and tall poles beside the smoke hole hung with strips of blue and red cloth and a white buffalo robe given to the spirits
the entrance passage with its hide door flap pulled aside, four buffalo-hide sacks of water sewn in the shape of great turtles, the sacred drums of the rite, carried out by men in their sixties, and a heap of green willow twigs and sage laid at the threshold
the opening of the lodge on the first day, a lone man in his fifties walking in from the prairie, his body painted white with clay, a robe of four white wolf skins on his shoulders and a headdress of two raven skins, carrying a long pipe, as Lone Man come to open the rite
