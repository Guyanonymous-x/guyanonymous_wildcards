and as the red sun sets, at the high priest's signal, the men cut each lamb's throat with a single stroke of the knife, and the blood pours from the neck onto the stones and runs in a dark red stream toward a gutter in the floor, the sky behind them orange and pink
and in the last light of dusk the men dip a finger in the lambs' blood and press a small red dot onto each other's foreheads, laughing and embracing, the dead sheep lying in rows on the stones behind them
and in the dusk under floodlights the men pour boiling water from large pots over the dead sheep and pluck the wet wool away by hand, then hang each pale carcass on a hook, slit open the belly and lift out the guts into metal basins
and after dark the men push each whole salted carcass onto a very long wooden pole and lower the poles upright into the deep fire pits, then cover the openings with metal grates, wet cloths and mud, the orange glow of the fire lighting their faces
and at midnight the Samaritan men and women in their twenties to eighties eat the roasted meat in haste with flat unleavened bread and bitter herbs, standing, belts tied at the waist and walking sticks in their hands, lit by lamps and the glow of the pits
