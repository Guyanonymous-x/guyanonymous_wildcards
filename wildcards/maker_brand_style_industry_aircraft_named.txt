In the style of Boeing, clean, confident, bright aluminium-and-white American jet-age engineering. Long smooth tubular volumes with gently rounded ends and a slightly raised swell, swept-back lines and large cylindrical forms in white and silver. White upper surfaces over polished bare metal, one narrow deep blue stripe running the full length above a tidy row of small round-cornered openings, and one tall element carrying a single large bold colour mark. Precise seams, crisp edges and white hardware. Optimistic, orderly, dependable and modern.
In the style of Airbus, smooth, calm, computer-drawn European modernism. A perfectly round tubular body ending in a blunt, softly rounded end, with dark glazing wrapped in a sleek black swoosh. Slim swept lines with upturned tips that curve skyward, large round volumes and one tall element in bold colour. Clean white finish with a restrained palette of blue and white, a neat line of small rounded openings and flush, seamless surfaces. Uncluttered, cool, efficient and quietly high-tech, with a gentle sense of digital precision.
In the style of de Havilland, light, nimble, gracefully proportioned British forms of the 1930s to 1950s. Slim bodies with smooth wooden curves, elliptical outlines and softly curved coverings that hug what is inside. Taut fabric surfaces in silver, with bright trainer yellow for contrast, and aluminium panels left bare and polished. Glazed bubble forms, fish-like pod shapes and simple round roundel marks in red, white and blue. Streamlined, clean and elegant, craftsman-built, with a hopeful pre-war and post-war amateur charm.
In the style of Supermarine, sleek, purposeful British aerodynamic purity of the 1930s and 1940s. Graceful elliptical curves, long slim volumes, tapering ends and a narrow stance, all in polished metal skin with flush, tidy rivets. Colour is either dark green and earth brown shading to pale sky blue, or glossy blue and silver lacquer. Bright round markings in red, white and blue give crisp contrast. Fine, thin lines and smooth rounded transparent forms. Fast-looking, elegant, lean and precise, pared down to speed alone.
In the style of Cessna, friendly, simple, practical design from the 1960s and 1970s. Boxy but approachable white forms with softened corners, generous wraparound windows and plain, honest shapes that are easy to read. Painted sweeping side stripes in red, blue, gold or green add a note of optimism, with small neat numerals as the only lettering. Sturdy, unfussy, utility-first and casual, with a weekend-adventure cheerfulness and clean enamel finish.
In the style of Concorde, needle-sharp, supersonic, futuristic elegance of the 1970s. A tall, slender white form narrowing to a long pointed tip, with tiny oval windows set in a slim line. A pure, smooth, sweeping triangular outline tapers back into a narrow end. White paint is relieved only by one thin blue line. Knife-slim, bird-like, pared-back and aerodynamic, with a refined, rarefied air of speed and luxury.
In the style of Zeppelin, stately, monumental, silver-giant 1920s-30s elegance. An enormous cigar-shaped form stretched taut over a light frame, covered in silvery metallic cloth, with a small streamlined volume beneath and neat rows of windows. Cruciform tail shapes and bold black angular lettering along the length, plus a red accent at the rear. Smooth, gleaming, serene and huge, with a majestic, floating, slow-moving presence and a dignified period grandeur.
