at the second burial, years after the death, Wayuu women in their thirties to sixties in long loose dresses of bright printed cotton lift the dead man's bones from the opened grave in the kin cemetery
a woman in her fifties cleans each bone of the dead with her hands, laying them in order on a white cloth, the other women around her, their faces painted dark brown
the cleaned bones are laid in a clay urn and set in a new tomb of plastered stone in the cemetery of the clan, the kin in their twenties to seventies standing around it
the cleaned bones are put into a small cloth bag of bright woven cotton, and the women of the dead man's mother's clan, in their thirties to sixties, carry it to the shared cemetery of their clan and lay it in the tomb with the bones of their other dead
at the first wake the dead woman in her seventies lies in a hammock under a shelter of wood and cactus stems, the mourners in their twenties to seventies sitting around her, cattle killed for the feast
