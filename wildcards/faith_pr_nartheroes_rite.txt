at the end of the room a storyteller in his sixties with a gray mustache plays a shichepshin, a long-necked two-stringed fiddle of carved wood held upright on his knee, and recites the deeds of the Narts, his mouth open and his eyes half closed
in the open space by the table Murat, a man in his twenties in a black cherkeska, dances the fast Circassian dance on the tips of his toes, arms flung out, before Zalina, a woman in her twenties in a long white dress, who glides with small steps
outside under an old sacred oak a ram is laid on its side and killed with a knife cut at the throat, the blood running onto the grass, as an elder in his seventies prays with both hands raised, palms up
the elders in their sixties to eighties hold out their open palms and bow their heads as the thamade says the prayer to Thashkhue, the high god, before the first toast
