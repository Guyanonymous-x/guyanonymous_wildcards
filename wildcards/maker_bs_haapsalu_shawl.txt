In the style of the Haapsalu lace shawl from Estonia. A rectangular or triangular scarf is knitted in extremely fine, cobweb-thin lamb wool or wool-silk yarn, so light that it can be drawn through a ring. The shawl is made of a central field, a border and a separate edge sewn on. Patterns are airy open-work lace motifs, nupp bobbles, diamond lozenges, leaf shapes, fir cones, lily-of-the-valley bells, snowflakes, ripples, and a scalloped, saw-toothed edge with a dense row of tiny points. Colour is traditionally natural white or ivory, sometimes pale grey, soft peach or faded rose. The texture is fuzzy, misty and ethereal, with a delicate halo of loose fibres, and hung against a dark background it resembles frost on a window or cobwebs in morning dew. The look is delicate, geometric and romantic, handmade with mathematical patience.
