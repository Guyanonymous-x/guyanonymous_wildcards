In the style of Dona Rosa black pottery from San Bartolo Coyotepec in Oaxaca, hand-built barro negro vessels with a deep, mirror-like metallic black sheen. Clay is shaped by hand on a turning plate with no wheel, so forms are softly rounded and slightly asymmetrical, such as tall jars, round-bellied pots, vases with narrow necks, lidded urns, candle holders, whistles and the famous monkey-shaped mezcal flask. The surface is rubbed with a smooth quartz stone before firing in a smoky pit kiln, giving a silky gunmetal-to-jet shine with soft grey matte patches where the burnish is lighter. Decoration is restrained, with applied clay flowers, rosettes, twisted ropes and small handles, delicate pierced or cut-out lacework openwork on lamps, and fine incised lines. Unpainted pieces stay monochrome black or silver-grey, while some are coloured in flat bright paints. The look is earthy, sculptural and quietly elegant, an ancient shape language with a polished modern gleam, photographed against plain pale backgrounds.
