and the mystai wade knee-deep into the shallow clear water of the Ilissos river over pale stones while a priestess in her fifties pours river water from a bowl over each bowed head, plane trees with broad leaves leaning over the banks, in cool pale light of an early spring morning
and at a low stone altar on the riverbank a priest in his forties holds a pink piglet by its hind legs over the stone and cuts its throat with a short iron knife, the blood running down the side of the altar, mystai in their twenties to fifties watching with lit torches, in gray light just before dawn
and the procession winds along the river path toward the small marble temple of four columns at Agrai, the mystai in their twenties to sixties holding their lit torches high, the flames bright against the trees, in deep blue light just after sunset
and inside the small marble temple the mystai in their twenties to fifties stand in rows before painted marble statues of Demeter and Persephone holding sheaves of wheat, spring flowers heaped at their feet, in warm flickering torchlight at night
and on the grassy bank among red and purple wild anemones in bloom, sponsors in their forties tie white wool ribbons around the right wrists of kneeling mystai in their twenties, in bright clear light of a spring midday
and on the riverbank Nikias, a mystes in his twenties, sits on a low stool spread with a white ram's fleece, his head covered by a thin dark veil, while Philonis, a priestess in her forties, holds a flat woven winnowing fan above his head and Lykomedes, a priest in his fifties, lowers a burning torch close beside him, in cool pale light of a late winter morning
