a dwelling whose fabric is made by its occupants from their own animals, which is true of nothing else in this family, long narrow woven panels of black goat hair sewn edge to edge and stretched over several poles with guy lines to pegs or stones
a roof that is deliberately loose-woven so it breathes in heat and swells tight in rain
walls of the same material that are raised
lowered or removed entirely according to wind
sun and season so the dwelling's plan changes through the day
a dividing curtain separating the guest and family halves which is the most important internal feature and is observed strictly
a hearth in the men's side for coffee and another for cooking
a floor of rugs over sand
bedding that is also seating
no fixed furniture at all
a structure that is struck
loaded and re-pitched as a matter of routine, and an orientation chosen for wind and shade
