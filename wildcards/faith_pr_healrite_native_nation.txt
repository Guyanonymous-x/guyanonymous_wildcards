among the Lakota of the northern plains, inside a sweat lodge, a low dome of bent willow saplings covered with heavy tarps and blankets, a healer in his sixties with long gray braids, in shorts, pours water from a dipper onto glowing hot stones in a pit, and the steam fills the lodge
among the Diné, the Navajo people of the desert Southwest, inside an eight-sided hogan of logs and earth, a medicine man in his seventies in a velvet shirt and a red headband has the sick person sit on a sandpainting, a large circle of figures and rainbow bands made of colored sand on the floor
among the Haudenosaunee, the Iroquois peoples of New York and Ontario, in a longhouse, a long low wooden hall with benches, the Medicine Society healers in their fifties to seventies in ribbon shirts wave turtle-shell rattles over the sick person
among the Ojibwe of the Great Lakes woods, in a lodge of birchbark, a healer in her sixties with gray braids, in a long ribbon skirt, burns sweetgrass and fans its smoke over the sick person with an eagle-wing fan
