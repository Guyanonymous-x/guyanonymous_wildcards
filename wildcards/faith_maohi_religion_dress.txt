the maro ura, the sacred girdle of red feathers of the high chiefs of Tahiti in the Society Islands of French Polynesia, a very long belt of tapa bark cloth covered with hundreds of small red and yellow parrot feathers stitched on in rows, so long it winds around the waist several times with its end hanging down, wound around the waist of a chief in his thirties to fifties, his body bare above a tapa wrap
feather girdles of red and yellow worn over a white tapa loincloth with a tall headdress of feathers and a cape of feathers, the chief's legs and buttocks tattooed in black
a long sash of red feathers wrapped around the waist and hanging down, worn by a high chief at a marae, an open stone court, with a tapa cloak over his shoulders
the red feathered belts of the high chiefs, the color of the gods, over wraps of pale bark cloth, with gorgets, crescent breastplates of wicker covered in feathers and shark teeth
