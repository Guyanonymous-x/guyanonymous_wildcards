The overall look is gentle, light, sophisticated, calm and domestic, with the quiet modernism of Scandinavian dining, in a restrained palette of white, blue, soft green and brown.
In the style of Gustavsberg, Swedish mid-century studio ceramics. Pieces are hand-thrown or moulded vases, bowls, dishes, ashtrays, tiles and stoneware figurines in soft, curved and slightly lopsided organic shapes, such as pebble forms, leaf dishes, squashed spheres and wide-hipped jugs. Glazes are matte and richly mottled, in sage green, rust, mustard, deep turquoise, charcoal grey and cream, often with sgraffito, incised stripes and dots, circles, linear figures, animal motifs and silhouetted leaves. A signature type is a dark green-brown stoneware inlaid with silver, formed in rounded jars with a delicate silver leaf pattern. Tableware is plain white with coloured berries and flowers. The mood is warm, humane, playful and Scandinavian modern, with intimate handmade surfaces. Surfaces show glaze pooling at the edges, visible finger marks, soft shoulders on every vase and a calm, slightly earthy palette that suits teak shelves and linen curtains.
In the style of Vista Alegre, Portuguese porcelain. The body is thin, bright white hard-paste porcelain with a smooth shiny glaze. Tableware has decorations drawn from aristocratic traditions, with gold rims, fine landscape vignettes in sepia or blue, delicate floral sprays, garlands of roses, medallions, heraldic crests, Greek-key borders and raised relief, while service pieces include soup tureens, teapots, platters and cake stands with scrolling handles, high-footed shapes and finials in the form of flowers. Modern collections turn to bold graphic prints, hand-painted strokes in cobalt, black or coral, and sculptural art editions. The traditional palette is white, gold, navy, rose pink and sage green. The look is elegant, formal, light and slightly romantic. Backstamps and tiny gilt crowns appear on the bases, and sets are laid out in generous, symmetrical table settings with white linen, silver cutlery and crystal glass.
In the style of the Caldas da Rainha ceramic factory in Portugal, naturalistic majolica. Plates, bowls, tureens, jugs and platters are moulded in the form of oversized cabbage leaves, with veined, ruffled edges and glossy, vivid green glaze, with a pale ribbed back. Other pieces are modelled as realistic lobsters, crabs, snakes, frogs, lizards, fish, snails, beetles, flies and sunflowers, crawling across plates, ewers and jugs in lifelike detail. Glazes are glossy, juicy and saturated, with leaf green, mustard yellow, turquoise, coral, tomato red, aubergine purple and cream. The colours are exaggerated, the textures are lumpy and tactile, and the humour is cheeky. The look is whimsical, sculptural, rustic and slightly surreal, designed for a very Portuguese table. Colours glisten as though freshly wet, and pieces cluster on rustic tables among real vegetables, shellfish and wicker, with a humorous, almost trompe loeil effect.
In the style of Lladro, Spanish porcelain figurines. The figures are tall, graceful and elongated, representing young women with parasols, shepherd girls, dancers, ballerinas, children with geese, nuns, clowns, horses and swans, with slim bodies, long limbs, small heads and flowing, swirling robes. Faces are serene, with softly closed or gentle eyes, a pale rosy complexion and tiny delicate hands. The porcelain is mostly matte, chalky and soft, in pastel colours such as pale blue, grey-green, lilac, rose, cream, white and muted gold, with occasional glossy accents and fine hand-applied porcelain flowers. Forms are fluid and somewhat stretched, with the folds of clothes made into rippling, wavy ribbons. A glazed gres line has deeper earthy tones. The look is sentimental, romantic, delicate and fragile. The bases are small and rounded, with tiny flowers and ribbons, the poses are quiet and tender, and displays use soft spotlighting in glass cabinets with mirrored backs.
