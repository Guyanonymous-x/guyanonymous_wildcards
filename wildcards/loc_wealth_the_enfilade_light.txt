The one lighting effect in the house that is architectural rather than decorative, the axis runs close to the window wall, so each room's windows throw a block of light across it and each cross wall throws a band of shadow, giving a measured alternation of bright and dark bays down sixty meters that changes with the hour and is strongest at nine in the morning or five in the afternoon when the sun is along the front rather than across it
The far end is always the brightest point because it terminates on glass, so the eye is pulled down the line
At night the alternation reverses, with each room's candles making a pool and the thresholds dark, and a pair of candle stands at every threshold exists precisely to fill those gaps
In the modern house with two rooms unlit the run reads as a tunnel with a lit mouth
