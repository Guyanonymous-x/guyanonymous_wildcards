before dawn, the fire keeper kindles the new fire by friction, spinning a wooden stick against a dry board until smoke and a glowing coal appear, in the pale gray light before sunrise
at night the dancers circle the fire counterclockwise in the stomp dance, a long spiraling line, the leader at the front calling out and the men answering, the women stepping behind, all lit orange by the leaping flames against the black sky
at midday under a hot bright sun, ears of new green corn in their husks and a bowl of new corn kernels are laid in the fire as the first offering, and the smoke rises white
in the gold light of late afternoon, the people line up to drink a dark cleansing medicine of boiled roots and herbs from a big iron kettle, and some vomit at the edge of the ground to cleanse their bodies
