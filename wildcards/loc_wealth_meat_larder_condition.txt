the larder of 1800 with a whole carcass hanging, a salting trough in constant use and a household that butchered its own
the same room in 1880 with meat delivered three times a week from a town butcher, the salting trough dry and the brine tub used only at pig-killing in November
the room in 1910 with an ice chest in the corner and ice delivered weekly, which is the first real change in five hundred years
the room in 1935 with a gas or electric refrigerator standing on the slate shelf and the gauzed window boarded over, which is the moment the type dies and is identifiable to within two years in most houses
the room as a flower room, which keeps the slate and the gully and is the commonest surviving use
the room as a plant room for a modern boiler, with the slate shelves cut away for pipes
the room mothballed, limewashed again, swept, the hooks left
the room open to the weather, where it is one of the most durable spaces in the building, slate, brick, iron and lime survive, and the room's only perishable element was always its contents
