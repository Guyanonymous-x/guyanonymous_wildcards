the square empty at dawn, pigeons on the paving stones, the vast cobbled oval bare from colonnade to colonnade, a few gray birds pecking between the stones, lines of wooden barriers stacked to one side and the basilica front pale pink in the first light
the Swiss Guards in blue, red and yellow striped uniforms with helmets and halberds standing at a gate, two adult men in puffed doublets and knee breeches of broad vertical stripes with white collars and white gloves, wearing polished steel helmets with red feather plumes, each holding upright a long pole with an ax blade and spike at its tip
crowds of pilgrims filling the square, tens of thousands of adults packed shoulder to shoulder between the colonnades, some waving small national flags and holding up phones and rosaries, groups in matching colored caps and scarves, rows of gray plastic chairs near the basilica steps
the pope in white waving from the balcony, an adult man in a white robe with a short white shoulder cape, a white skullcap and a gold cross on a chain, standing small at the stone balustrade above the red drape with one arm raised and the palm open toward the crowd
