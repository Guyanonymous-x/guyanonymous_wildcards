the dress of the Apsaalooke, the Crow people of Montana, at Crow Fair, the great August gathering of tipis by the Little Bighorn River, adult women in their twenties to seventies in long dresses of deep red, pink or blue wool cloth or of cream hide covered with rows of real or carved elk teeth, wide beaded belts and beaded moccasins
beaded regalia in the Crow style, bold blocks and triangles of pale blue, pink, yellow and dark blue seed beads on white hide, worn as vests, armbands, leggings and moccasins by adult men with eagle-feather bustles at the back and roaches, stiff upright crests of dyed porcupine hair, on the head
Crow riders at the parade in their thirties to sixties, the men in beaded buckskin shirts and leggings and feather bonnets, the women in elk-tooth dresses with beaded collars, their horses hung with beaded bands across the chest
Nora, a woman in a long dress of trade wool with a hide belt of brass studs and a beaded bag, a blanket folded over one arm, beside Leo, a man in a ribbon shirt and a beaded vest of geometric Crow designs, both in beaded moccasins
