Nanih Waiya, the mother mound, stands in the woods of Mississippi as a long flat-topped earth mound covered in grass, the old forest of oak and pine around it
the Choctaw homeland spreads below, rolling hills of pine and oak, a brown river winding between them and cornfields along its banks
a Choctaw village stands among the trees, houses of logs and bark with round roofs, a stickball field cleared beside it and the sun shining on the cornfields
