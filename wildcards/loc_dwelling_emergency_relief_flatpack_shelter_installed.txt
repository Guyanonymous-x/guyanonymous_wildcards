a shelter designed against a specification written by humanitarian agencies and refined through two decades of field evaluation, and the most thoroughly tested small building in the world, a kit that ships flat at a volume and weight chosen so that a given aircraft or container delivers the maximum number of households
panels and frame members numbered and keyed so that assembly requires no measuring
no cutting and no power
an instruction sheet that is entirely pictorial
a build time of four to eight hours by a family with minimal assistance
a design life stated as three years that is routinely exceeded five-fold
a lockable door and shuttered windows because dignity
privacy and security were identified as the priorities that distinguish a shelter from a tent
ventilation detailed for hot climates
insulation that is minimal and is the design's known weakness in cold ones
a floor that is often not supplied so the family lays its own
anchoring for wind
panels that can be dismantled and rebuilt elsewhere
materials chosen for fire performance and for recyclability
a unit cost that is debated in every evaluation, and a deployment context in which the shelter is one of thousands laid out on a grid by a site planner
