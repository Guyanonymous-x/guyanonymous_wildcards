Inshushinak, lord of Susa, a god in his forties with a long square beard and a horned crown, sits on a throne made of a great coiled serpent, a staff in his hand, judging the dead
before the throne of Inshushinak the dead, men and women in their thirties to eighties in long robes, wait their turn in a long line in the dim halls of the underworld
behind Inshushinak rises the stepped temple tower of Chogha Zanbil, its tiers of baked brick glowing dark red against the shadows of the underworld
