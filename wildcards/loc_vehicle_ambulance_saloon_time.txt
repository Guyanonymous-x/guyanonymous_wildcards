The handover at the start of a shift, with the compartment checked against a printed list, seals examined, the drugs safe opened and signed, oxygen contents noted and the floor washed
The first call, when the compartment goes from spotless to fully disordered in eleven minutes
The clean-down after, which takes almost as long as the job and is done in a hospital bay with the doors open and the lights on, and which is the compartment's most frequent single activity
The waiting, parked on standby, with the crew in the cab and the saloon dark and empty, which is where most of the hours actually go
The night job, with the saloon on its blue running light, the patient looking at the ceiling and the crew's faces lit from below by the monitor
The queue outside an emergency department, where the vehicle becomes a stationary treatment room for two or three hours and the compartment's limitations, no lavatory, one seat, no privacy, nowhere to put anything down, are felt most acutely, which is a genuinely modern condition of this space; and the deep clean at the end of a shift, with the trolley out, the lockers emptied and the floor hosed
