Eight, a housemaid in with a box of cloths and a bundle of sticks, grates cleaned and laid, carpet swept with damp tea leaves
Ten, the room empty and the muslins drawn
After luncheon, the room in use for four hours and the only occupied room in the house
Four, tea brought in on a tray and the fire made up
After the women withdraw at half past ten, the room at its most used, coffee, the piano, the work baskets out
Midnight, the fire guarded, the lamps out, a candle taken upstairs
A wet Tuesday in February, which is the room's truest condition and is six people in a space built for thirty
