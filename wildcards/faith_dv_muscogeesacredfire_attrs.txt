four open brush arbors of poles roofed with green willow and cane stand on the four sides of the square ground, men in their thirties to seventies seated on benches under them
Muscogee women in their twenties to sixties, shell shakers, dance in a line around the fire in long ribbon dresses, rattles of dry turtle shells tied on their lower legs
Seminole men and women in their thirties to sixties in bright patchwork clothing of red, yellow, blue and black bands stand in a ring around the fire
a Muscogee mekko, a town chief in his sixties, in a ribbon shirt and a turban of cloth with a white feather, feeds green corn ears into the fire
