the still room of 1700 doing what its name says, distilling cordials, waters and medicines, with a small alembic on the charcoal stove, which is the original function and the reason for the name
the room of 1800, now a preserving, baking, tea and coffee room with the distilling gone
the room of 1880 at its fullest, with a still-room maid, a second maid under her, and a daily output of cakes, breads, preserves and all the hot drinks for the principal floor
the room of 1910 with the charcoal stove bricked up and a gas ring on the marble, which is the end of the type
the room of 1930 absorbed into the kitchen as a scullery or a store
the room as a tea-room kitchen in a house open to the public
the room in a divided house as a bathroom, with the marble slab left in as a shelf
the room mothballed, 140 jars boxed and the dresser empty
the room open to the weather, where the jars are looted first and the marble slab last
