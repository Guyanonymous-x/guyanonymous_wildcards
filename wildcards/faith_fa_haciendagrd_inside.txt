the prayer hall, a wide room spread wall to wall with white sheets, and at the front under a fringed canopy of blue and gold cloth a raised platform piled with cushions where the holy book rests wrapped in embroidered silk, an attendant in his fifties in white seated behind it waving a long whisk of white hair above it
the east wall of the hall, covered from end to end by a mural painted in 1975, robed and turbaned figures in glowing blues, golds and reds, with the canopy of the holy book in front of it and vases of fresh flowers along the edge of the platform
the hall seen from the back, the golden underside of the dome overhead, a center aisle of blue carpet leading to the holy book, men seated cross-legged on the left and women on the right, a wooden money box before the platform and a tray of sweet warm dough being handed out by a server
to the right of the holy book a low stage for the players, Simran Kaur, a woman in her forties in white, seated at a small hand-pumped keyboard organ, Gurdev Singh, a man in his thirties beside her with a pair of small hand drums, a microphone on a short stand between them
