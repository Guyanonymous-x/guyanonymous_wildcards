Tuya, the Big-Headed Dancer, a man in his thirties whose huge headdress is a great round ball of long thin willow rods, each tipped with a feather or a flower, standing out around his head
Kuksu dancers, men in their twenties to forties with bodies painted in black and white stripes, wearing feather skirts and headbands of red woodpecker scalps, dancing around the center pole
the inside of the round dance house, an earth-covered lodge with a great center post and a smoke hole above, dancers circling a fire on the dirt floor
