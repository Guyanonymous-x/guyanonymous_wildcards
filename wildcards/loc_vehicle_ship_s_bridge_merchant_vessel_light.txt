The most rigorously specified lighting in any vehicle here, and the only one where getting it wrong is both an offense and a hazard, by day, nine to fifteen raked windows giving a hard flat light off the sea with every display at full brightness and the room's own surfaces specified so they do not reflect into the glass
By night, every fitting switched to a red circuit at the lowest level that still allows a chart, a record book and a 900-point panel to be read, so that the watchkeeper's dark adaptation is preserved and a person standing at the center window sees the horizon, other ships' navigation lights and the loom of a coast clearly while seeing almost nothing inside the room
Displays dimmed to a night setting and still the brightest things present, which is the reason bridge-display dimming is itself a regulated function
The chart table under a shielded red lamp or a hood
The wheelhouse door fitted with a light trap or a notice, because one white light costs the watch twenty minutes of night vision and this is enforced more strictly than any other rule aboard; and the room's defining condition, a radar, and twelve minutes until the watch alarm needs resetting
