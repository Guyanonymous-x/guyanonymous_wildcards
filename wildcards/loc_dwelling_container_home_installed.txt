a conversion that is far more difficult and far less cheap than its reputation suggests, a shipping container is a structure engineered to be strong at its corner castings and nowhere else, so every opening cut into it requires framing and welding and every cut reduces its strength
the internal width is fixed at about two point four meters which dictates every plan
the steel skin is a perfect thermal bridge and a condensation risk so insulation must be continuous and is almost always spray foam applied inside at a cost in headroom
the floor is treated plywood that should be replaced
a single container gives about thirty square meters at twelve meters long and the long thin plan is the type's defining characteristic
multiple containers can be stacked and joined but joining requires cutting and structural steel which erases most of the cost saving
foundations are simple because the loads go to four corners
the finished building is heavy and needs a crane, and the main genuine advantages are speed
transportability and the structural robustness of the corner-cast frame
