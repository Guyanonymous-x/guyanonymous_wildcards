the house's refrigerator before refrigeration, and a room whose every feature is a temperature or an air-flow decision, placed on a north or north-east external wall
at the cool end of the service range
with no wall facing the sun at any hour and in many houses with its own small projecting block so that three of its four walls are external; a window with no glass at all
only a wire gauze or a louvre, so the room is at outside temperature minus two or three degrees and in continuous ventilation, which is the whole technique and is why a larder works and a cupboard does not; slate shelves rather than timber or stone, because slate is dense
cold to the touch
non-absorbent and can be scrubbed, and because its thermal mass holds the night's cold into the afternoon; a ceiling of iron rails and hooks, so that meat hangs in air on all sides rather than lying on a surface; a floor of blue brick or stone laid to a fall with a channel and a gully, because the room is washed down and because meat drips; walls limewashed to full height and renewed annually
limewash being mildly antiseptic, which was known empirically long before it was understood; a meat safe of perforated zinc for the cuts in use; a brine tub and a salting trough
since salting and brining were how a side of beef or a pig was kept for months; no heating of any kind and no flue
ever; a door of two leaves in some examples
the inner one gauzed, making a fly lock; and no bell
no pull and nothing that rings
