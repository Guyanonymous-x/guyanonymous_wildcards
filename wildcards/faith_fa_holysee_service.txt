the prayer service, hundreds of worshippers in plain white tunics and trousers kneeling in straight rows on the tiled steps, men on the right and women on the left, hands clasped at the forehead, adults from their twenties to their eighties
the dignitaries at the front in bright silk robes, some in yellow for Buddhism, some in sky blue for Taoism, some in red for Confucianism, with tall squared or rounded headdresses bearing the Divine Eye, men in their fifties to seventies kneeling on the upper levels
the loft above the door, a small orchestra of men in their forties and fifties in black turbans and white tunics playing two-string fiddles, a zither and a drum, and a choir of women in their twenties and thirties in white áo dài, the long split Vietnamese tunic worn over trousers
visitors along the upper side galleries, travelers in their twenties to sixties from Vietnam, Europe, Korea and Australia looking down on the rows of white, their shoes left at the door, a white-clad usher in his sixties pointing the way
