a Nyau dancer stands as a spirit of the dead, a man in his thirties in a carved wooden face mask painted red with white teeth bared, heavy black brows and a fringe of real hair and feathers, his body hidden in a suit of torn colored rags hanging in layers, a stick in each hand, his feet in a cloud of dust near the bottom of the frame
Kasiya maliro, the great antelope spirit, a huge woven frame of dry grass and corn husks built in the shape of an antelope with long sweeping horns and a high back, standing taller than the men around it, carried by dancers hidden inside, their bare feet under its grass fringe on the earth near the bottom of the frame
Chadzunda, the old chief spirit, a dancer in his forties in a black-painted wooden mask of an old man with deep furrows across the brow, ears set high on the temples, a bulbous nose, wide-open eyes and mouth showing white paper eyeballs and teeth, a cap of furry animal skin over the top and back of the head, his body in a costume of dry banana leaves and fiber to his ankles, leaning on a long stick, his bare feet on the earth near the bottom of the frame
Maria, the woman spirit, danced by a man in his thirties in a pale pink-painted wooden mask of a woman with long straight black hair, red-painted lips and earrings, in a long cotton dress and headscarf, taking small careful steps, his bare feet on the earth near the bottom of the frame
Njovu, the elephant spirit, a great frame of woven grass and cloth painted gray in the shape of an elephant with long tusks of carved white wood and wide ears, carried by dancers hidden inside, their bare feet on the earth near the bottom of the frame
