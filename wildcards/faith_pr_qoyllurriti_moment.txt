and at night the ukukus climb in a long single line up the white face of the glacier, each holding a lit candle, a large wooden cross planted in the ice ahead of them, in deep blue starlight and the small yellow points of the candle flames
and, as in the older form of the rite, ukukus in their twenties to forties come down off the glacier at dawn with large blocks of clear blue-white ice tied on their backs with ropes, the ice glinting in the first pink light of the sun
and at sunrise on the last morning thousands of pilgrims in their twenties to seventies kneel on the slope of the valley facing east as the sun clears the ridge, their hands raised, the snow peaks above lit gold
and in the valley a group of dancers in their twenties to fifties wheels in a ring before the white church, a band of men in their forties with drums and flutes beside them, in hard bright sunlight of a cold clear afternoon
and two ukukus in their twenties lash at each other's legs with braided leather whips in a ritual duel on the snow, the blows landing across the bare skin of their calves and leaving red welts, in flat gray light of early morning
