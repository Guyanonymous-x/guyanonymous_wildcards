the game larder of 1780 as a modest louvred shed serving a household that shot walked-up birds over dogs in small numbers
the same building rebuilt about 1860 as a substantial octagon with a rotating ring, because the arrival of the driven shoot, the breech-loader and the railway increased a day's bag from twenty birds to four or five hundred and the old larder could not hold them, which is the single change that produced this building type in the form described here
the larder of 1900 at its fullest, with 140 birds hanging and a game dealer's cart calling twice a week to take most of them to London by rail, which is the fact that most surprises people, the majority of a great estate's game was sold, not eaten
the larder of 1917 with the keepers at the front and the ledger's figures a tenth of normal
the larder of 1950 with the shooting let and the building used twice a season
the larder today still in use on a shooting estate, which many are, or converted to a garden store, a summerhouse or a folly, which is easy because it is already a pretty octagon
the larder mothballed, hooks greased, louvres repaired
the larder open to the weather, where it survives remarkably well because it was never weathertight to begin with, the louvres rot on the south-west face first, the double roof's inner boarding fails, and the slate floor, the central post and the iron ring are frequently found complete in a wood with nothing else of the estate left standing
