Eight in the morning, the lamps collected from every principal room by a footman on a tray, which is a circuit of the whole house and takes half an hour
Nine to one, the room's work, each lamp emptied of its dregs, the wick trimmed level with the crescent scissors, the burner brushed, the font filled through a gauze funnel, the chimney washed, dried and polished with whiting and chamois, the shade washed, and the whole thing entered in the book if the wick was renewed, at a rate of about five minutes a lamp and so four hours for forty-eight
One, dinner in the hall
The afternoon, the chimneys and shades, the stable lanterns, the candles counted and boxed
Half past five in winter, the lighting round, forty-eight lamps lit and checked here, then carried out two at a time on a tray to the rooms in a fixed order, which takes forty minutes and is why there is a period every winter evening when the house is half lit
Eleven at night, the lamps extinguished room by room as the family goes up, and left where they stand until eight
The morning, the same circuit again
A summer week in June, eleven lamps in use and the room nearly idle
