In the style of Cadbury, deep royal purple wrappers in a glossy foil and paper finish, with a flowing gold script wordmark in thick looping lettering, often placed in a swooping white or gold ribbon swirl. Chocolate bars are moulded into rectangular blocks with the same cursive mark embossed on each square. A glass-and-a-half motif of milk pouring into a bar appears in cream and white. Gold and purple foil, cream accents, rounded friendly curves. Gift boxes use purple with a gold border, ribbon bows and chocolate swirl graphics. Warm, traditional, comforting British confectionery look with a rich purple dominating the shelf.
In the style of Toblerone, a long triangular prism carton in gold and cream-yellow, resembling a row of mountain peaks, with sloping saw-tooth chocolate triangles inside. Packaging has a deep blue lettering band and a mountain silhouette in the sky with a bear hidden inside the peak in negative space. Yellow-gold field, brown and dark blue serif and rounded sans lettering, white snow-capped peaks. Chocolate triangles in a repeating pyramid shape, foil wrapped in silver, lightly embossed. The mood is Alpine, Swiss, clean, with a mountain-and-bear emblem and warm honey-gold tones.
In the style of Kinder, white and red packaging with a smiling cartoon child face in a red halo, bright red lettering in a rounded sans-serif, and a milk-and-chocolate theme in cream and white. Egg-shaped hollow chocolate with a plastic yellow capsule inside, wrapped in shiny red and white foil with an image of a toy. Bars are slim rectangles in white with red stripes and a blue-white milk splash, cheerful, safe and playful. Palette of white, bright red, blue accents and warm yellow. The look is child-friendly, clean and bold, with rounded fonts and a mascot face on every pack.
In the style of Hershey, a deep chocolate-brown wrapper with silver lettering in bold sans-serif capitals, laid horizontally across the pack, and a silver foil inner wrap. Milk chocolate bars are flat segmented rectangles with raised square grids. Tear-drop foil-wrapped kisses with a small white paper flag plume and a brown-and-silver label, shaped like a conical swirl with a flat base. Brown, silver and white palette, with an occasional red or gold accent for seasonal packs. The look is classic American drugstore candy, plain, sturdy and warm, with condensed capital lettering and a monochrome brown field.
In the style of Haribo, transparent clear cellophane bags with a bright yellow-gold header, a red outlined bold sans lettering with rounded corners, a pair of cartoon children holding hands in a circle, and a window showing glossy translucent gummy bears in red, green, yellow, orange and white. Small gummy animals, rings, cola bottles and fried-egg shapes in jewel colours with a sugar-dusted or polished glaze. Gold bear mascots on a yellow gold field. Cheerful, retro German confectionery with a primary-colour palette, rounded type and a window pack to display the candy inside.
In the style of Pocky, slim rectangular cardboard sleeve boxes in saturated red for chocolate, pink for strawberry and other flavour colours, with a photographic close-up of thin biscuit sticks half dipped in glaze. Bold rounded sans-serif katakana and Latin lettering, glossy lamination, a bright white wave or splash of glaze. Inner foil sachets containing thin straight sticks with one end bare as a handle. Clean Japanese convenience-store packaging with a cheerful pastel-and-primary palette, cute food photography, flavour icons and seasonal limited-edition boxes.
In the style of Meiji, plain milk chocolate bars wrapped in a bright red paper sleeve with a white script wordmark, a silver foil inner, and a cream-white panel with a small chocolate illustration. Mushroom and hill-shaped cookie snacks (a biscuit stem with a chocolate cap) in a pale-yellow-and-brown cartoon box, with illustrated forests and mushrooms. Flat illustration, rounded fonts, cheerful and nostalgic, in red, brown, cream and white. Panel dividers, small bilingual text, and a simple clean grid. The look is tidy, friendly Japanese convenience confectionery.
In the style of Laduree, pastel macarons in pistachio green, rose pink, lilac, vanilla yellow and coffee brown, stacked in neat pyramids. Boxes in pale mint green or pink with gold embossed script, gilt edges and a satin ribbon, wrapped in tissue. Shops with powder-blue and pistachio paintwork, gilded mouldings, painted ceilings of cherubs, marble tables and glass cabinets. Cursive gold lettering, a delicate oval crest, and floral flourishes. Palette of pistachio, rose, cream and gold. The look is Second Empire Parisian tearoom, dainty, sugary, ornate and gilded.
In the style of Sprungli, small round pastel truffles and rectangular pralines nestled in pleated paper cups inside slim boxes in turquoise-blue, silver and cream. Tall elegant tea-room interiors with pale marble tables, mirrored walls, and polished wooden bars, set on a Swiss lakeside square. Wordmark in a clean, thin, spaced serif in silver or white on pale aqua blue, with fine linear borders. Palette of pale aqua, silver, cream and chocolate brown. The look is genteel, Swiss, understated, delicate confectionery craft, with soft pastel boxes and refined restraint.
In the style of Parle-G, a flat rectangular wax-paper packet in bright yellow with a red banner, a cartoon image of a smiling toddler girl with dark hair and a round face in the centre of an oval frame, framed in red and white. Lettering in bold red and white sans-serif with an Indian and Latin script combination. Plain, glossy, high-contrast, with a plate of biscuits shown as a photograph. Palette of sunshine yellow, red, white and black. The look is bright, nostalgic, wholesome, mass-market Indian with a toddler mascot and bold yellow field.
In the style of Havanna, an Argentine alfajor and coffee house brand. The alfajor is a round sandwich of two soft pale cookies with a thick dark layer of dulce de leche in the middle, glazed fully in glossy dark chocolate or white meringue-like icing, sold individually wrapped in a shiny foil. The wrapper and tin boxes are a deep turquoise-blue or royal blue and white scheme with a warm cream, a large rounded white script logo in the form of a flowing signature over a navy background and slim gold or silver details, and the circle of a small orange rising-sun mark. The packaging of boxes has soft rounded rectangular shapes, with maritime references such as a seaside town and small gulls. The cafes are warm wood-and-brass spaces with dark wood counters, tall windows, beige marble tables and lamps with amber light. The mood is friendly, sweet and nostalgic seaside Argentina with a trustworthy clean cream and blue palette.
In the style of Arnotts, Australian biscuit packaging. The brand mark is a multi-coloured parrot perched on a T-shaped stand eating a biscuit, drawn in a late nineteenth-century engraving style with a red, yellow, green and blue plumage, inside a red oval or circle. Boxes and wrappers are bright and cheerful, a red base for chocolate biscuits, yellow for sweet ones and blue or green for crackers, with a bold white block rounded sans-serif title, large appetising photos of the biscuits and a swirl or ribbon behind. Biscuits themselves are distinctive shapes, a rectangular two-layer chocolate-coated finger with a pattern of fine diagonal ridges, round crackers with small holes in a neat grid, square plain crackers, teddy-bear shapes, round cream sandwiches and a large round orange-sectioned disc with sugar frosting. Tins are tall rectangular metallic boxes with printed scenes. The mood is homely, nostalgic and cheerful, a family lunchbox feel.
In the style of Chupa Chups, Spanish lollipops. The sweet is a round ball candy on a white paper stick, wrapped in thin shiny plastic twisted at the base, with a label in the centre on top. The logo is a bright flower, a daisy-like shape with a yellow circle in the middle and red petals, with the name in friendly rounded white and yellow letters across it, on a bright red, yellow or orange background. Flavours are marked by colours, such as pink strawberry, orange, green apple, cola brown and lemon yellow, and some sweets are swirled with two colours. Display stands are hot-pink or red and shaped like a round tree, with dozens of lollipops pointing outwards in a sunburst. Posters show cartoon children licking lollipops. The mood is playful, sugary, cheeky and very pop.
