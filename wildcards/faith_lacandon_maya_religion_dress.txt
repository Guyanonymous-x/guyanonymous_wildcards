the dress of the Lacandon Maya of the rainforest of Chiapas in southern Mexico, adult men and women in their twenties to seventies in long loose white cotton tunics to the knee or the shin, plain and short-sleeved, the black hair hanging long and loose to the shoulders with bangs cut straight across the brow
simple white smocks of hand-spun cotton and bare feet, long straight black hair with straight bangs at the forehead, worn by men and women alike
the women in floral or embroidered tunics over bright print skirts, with ribbons and bird feathers tied in their long hair and bead necklaces
older men in long white tunics stained with red dye from the offerings, or for a rite in the older ceremonial tunic of pale beaten bark cloth painted with large red circles each with a dot at the center, their hair loose to the shoulders
