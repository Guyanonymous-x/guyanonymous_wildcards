In the style of Leica, German rangefinder cameras. Compact rectangular bodies with a flat top plate, black enamel paint or polished silver chrome, wrapped in finely pebbled black vulcanite leatherette. A small viewfinder window and coupled range window sit at the top front, with a knurled shutter-speed dial, a thin film-advance lever and a round rewind knob on top. Lenses are slim black or chrome barrels with fine knurled focusing rings, engraved white and red distance and aperture numerals, and a small red dot on the front. Minimal, no clutter, brass showing through worn paint on edges. Lettering is a small script logo in white. Mood is understated, crafted and timeless.
In the style of Hasselblad, Swedish medium-format cameras. A cubic body with a square profile, finished in satin black paint with bright chrome trim or all-black crinkle texture, topped with a folding waist-level viewfinder hood or a pentaprism. A large detachable square film magazine fits the back and a big round lens with chrome or black barrel, coloured aperture and shutter rings, and engraved white numerals mounts at the front. Winding crank on the side, a single large shutter button, and a bold white capital wordmark. Special editions use bright colours or exposed carbon. Mood is serious, rugged, professional and sculptural.
In the style of Polaroid, instant film cameras of the 1970s and 1980s. Bulky, sleek folding or boxy bodies in beige, white or black plastic with a dark leatherette grip panel, a large single lens set above a bright rectangular flash bar and a big red rectangular shutter button. A chocolate-brown and tan palette is typical, with a rainbow stripe in red, orange, yellow, green and blue across the front. Film prints are square with a thick white border and a wide chin at the bottom, glossy surface, soft warm washed-out colours and slightly faded saturation. Lettering is bold rounded sans-serif in white. Mood is nostalgic, friendly and snapshot-casual.
In the style of Nikon, Japanese single-lens reflex cameras of the 1960s to 1980s. Rugged, boxy bodies with a pentaprism hump, black enamel or silver chrome finish, and a leatherette covering with fine pebble grain. Heavy metal knurled dials on top for shutter speed and film rewind, a chunky film advance lever, and a bold yellow and black stripe on the lens mount. Lenses are black barrels with deep rubber-ribbed focus rings and engraved white, orange and green numerals. Wordmark is bold white capital letters on the prism front. Mood is rugged, professional and mechanical.
In the style of Canon, 1970s and 1980s Japanese film cameras. Compact boxy SLR bodies with a black or silver top plate, wood-grain or fine black leatherette covering, rows of tiny metal dials and small silver buttons, and a red-lettered diagonal bar logo. Lens barrels are black with subtle knurling and a red ring accent around the front of professional lenses. Bodies have a clean rectangular layout with a large angular pentaprism, a round film-speed dial and a thick advance lever. Lettering is a stylised red script mark. Mood is dependable, practical and quietly handsome.
In the style of Fujifilm, retro-styled digital cameras. Compact rangefinder-style bodies with a flat top plate in silver and black, finished in matte black paint over metal with a fine textured grip. Many machined aluminium dials sit on the top plate, each with engraved numerals for shutter speed, exposure compensation and film simulation, and a ring of chunky aperture rings around the lens barrel. A small offset viewfinder in the corner, red ruler-like labels, and a neat pebbled leatherette in black. Lettering is crisp white capitals. Mood is tactile, classic, clean and understated.
In the style of Kodak, American popular cameras and film packaging. Early cameras are leather-covered boxes with a round lens and a simple viewfinder. Mid-century models have art deco ribs and chrome stripes, then moulded plastic in the 1970s and 1980s. Packaging uses a bold yellow, red and black colour scheme with big sans-serif lettering and a red stylised K mark. Bright yellow film boxes with red panels. Mood is cheerful, familiar and snapshot-friendly.
In the style of GoPro, tough action cameras. Small matte black or dark grey rubberised cubes with a large round glass lens on the front, a tiny front screen, and a few chunky buttons with white icons on top. Mounts are bright black plastic frames with chunky tabs, three-prong folding connectors, orange-yellow or blue rubber straps and thumbscrews. Cases are transparent waterproof boxes. Typography is bold white sans-serif in a rounded style. Mood is rugged, active and outdoorsy.
