Everyday tableware is thick, durable and rounded, including heavy white cups, bowls, plates, jugs and teapots in a glossy glaze, along with the famous swan vases with an elegant curved neck and a hollow back, produced in white, matte pastel, pink, turquoise and black. Glazes include speckled turquoise, mustard yellow, tangerine, sage green, a dripping trickle glaze where one colour runs over another, and dark manganese brown with scratched-line sgraffito patterns. Decals show modern abstract florals, bamboo, diamond shapes, starburst, ferns and Maori-inspired koru spirals in mid-century colours. A classic cafe set has a deep brown-and-turquoise cup with a chunky handle and shallow saucer. Shapes are plump, rounded and friendly with simple lines. The mood is retro, warm, optimistic and distinctly 1950s and 1960s New Zealand.
In the style of Fisher and Paykel, New Zealand domestic appliances. Washing machines and fridges are clean, boxy, rounded-corner white enamel cabinets, from the 1950s through the 1970s with a thin chrome strip, a simple round dial and a stylised metal badge. Later designs are bright white or stainless steel with a slim control panel, a smooth top, large soft-touch rounded dials, a gentle blue or green backlit display and neat vertical or horizontal handles of brushed steel. The signature is the double-drawer dishwasher, two slim stainless steel drawers stacked in a cabinet, with a recessed handle and a narrow screen. Ranges are plain stainless steel with big oven doors in black glass, chunky round knobs in silver and a plain, modern seamless profile. The mood is practical, quiet, friendly and design-led, in white, silver and grey with minimal ornament.
In the style of the Soviet Ukrainian ZAZ Zaporozhets microcar. The car is tiny, boxy and rounded, a two-door rear-engined saloon about three metres long with a short stubby bonnet, a steep sloping or humped rear deck with large cooling air intakes on the rear flanks, small round headlights set in simple chrome-ringed bezels, a plain narrow grille or a handful of vertical slats, thin chrome bumpers and a tiny roofline. Rear-hinged doors, slim pillars and a big curved windscreen give it an airy look. Paint is flat cheap-looking gloss in pea green, cream, sky blue, light yellow, orange-red, brick red and grey, often with a thin chrome side strip. Wheels are small, with plain hubcaps and narrow tyres. The interior is spartan with a single round speedometer and thin metal dash. The mood is humble, endearing, utilitarian and drably cheerful.
In the style of Antonov, Ukrainian aircraft design. Aircraft are rugged, utilitarian and enormous, with a high wing set on a deep boxy fuselage, a glazed navigator nose with a downward-looking bulge, an upswept rear cargo ramp, and multiple small wheel bogies in a long row under bulged fairings on the belly. The large transports have double or twin tail fins, four wide engines under the wings, and a giant slab body painted in simple colours, matte military grey or white with a blue and yellow cheatline, a thin red or blue stripe and plain block lettering in Cyrillic and Latin script. A small classic biplane has fabric-covered wings with struts and wires, and a big radial engine in a rounded nose cowl, painted silver, green or orange. The look is heavy, honest, workmanlike and spectacular in scale.
In the style of the Polish School of Posters, post-war hand-painted film and theatre posters. The image is an expressive, surreal and witty painted or collaged illustration, typically a single bold metaphor instead of a literal scene, such as a giant eye, a flower with a thorn, a distorted face, a bird with a human body, a single broken object, or a childlike doodle on an empty space. Colours are saturated and unusual, such as acid yellow, blood red, deep blue, hot pink, mustard, turquoise and black, with ragged brushwork, gouache texture, torn paper edges and uneven print texture. Lettering is hand-drawn, quirky, irregular and integrated into the composition, in large loose letters in a contrasting colour. The format is a tall portrait sheet with roughly textured paper. The mood is poetic, dark, humorous, expressive and arty.
In the style of the Polish FSO Syrena. This is a small, upright, rounded two-door saloon from the 1950s and 1960s with a bulbous bonnet, a smooth two-box body, a notched or fastback tail and separate bulging front wings. The first version has a high slim roof, small round headlamps flush with the body, a short plain chrome grille with a few horizontal bars, thin chrome bumpers, small door handles and rear-hinged suicide doors. Later versions have a restyled front with a squarer grille, bigger lights, a lowered waistline and a larger rear window. Paint is plain glossy pastel, such as cream, sky blue, pistachio green, pale grey, light red and mustard, with chrome trim along the flanks and a small crest on the nose. The interior has a bench seat, a simple metal dash, a big thin steering wheel and a small speedometer. The mood is modest, cute and period-correct socialist-era motoring.
In the style of Jawa, Czechoslovak motorcycles. The bike has a compact two-stroke engine with finned cylinder and a rounded engine casing in silver, a teardrop fuel tank with a chrome knee pad side panel and a bold enamel badge, a flat bench seat, a swept-back handlebar, a round chrome headlamp, and thin tubular steel frame. Paint is two-tone glossy enamel, such as maroon and cream, black and red, deep blue and white, or olive and cream with fine hand-painted gold pinstripes on the tank. Twin chrome exhaust pipes flare slightly at the end like tapered trumpets. Mudguards are deep and curved, wheels are wire-spoked with chrome rims and a small chromed hub brake. Electrics are in a round chrome-ringed headlight with a bulb and a small instrument pod. The look is solid, simple, mid-century and slightly sporty, with plenty of chrome and shine.
In the style of Olympic Airways, the Greek airline of the 1960s and 1970s. The aircraft has a gleaming white upper fuselage and polished silver belly divided by a navy blue cheatline with a thin sky blue stripe running along the windows, large dark blue capital letters in a smart modern sans-serif and a Greek script version beside them. The tail is plain white or deep blue with five interlocking rings in a ring emblem in crisp contrasting colours, sometimes stacked as a vertical sign. The timetables and posters show sun-washed Aegean scenes, white temples, blue domes, fishing boats, columns, sparkling sea and bright blue sky. The mood is glamorous, sunny and jet-set.
In the style of Ski-Doo snowmobiles of the 1960s and 1970s. In the style of Rorstrand, Swedish mid-century porcelain. The best-known pattern is a white porcelain tableware with softly shaded cobalt-blue flowers painted on the rim and inside, with loose organic leaf outlines and a soft airbrush-like gradient, accompanied by a plain curving silhouette. Cups have a slender round handle, saucers are shallow, plates have a thin slightly rounded rim, and tureens have domed lids with simple knob handles. Other patterns offer a rustic mottled blue-and-white, green-and-brown speckled stoneware, and a trailing line of blue stripes. Earlier designs show Purple Lace transfer prints, a chinoiserie willow scene and a spray of green leaves. The overall look is gentle, light, sophisticated, calm and domestic, with the quiet modernism of Scandinavian dining, in a restrained palette of white, blue, soft green and brown.
In the style of Gustavsberg, Swedish mid-century studio ceramics. Pieces are hand-thrown or moulded vases, bowls, dishes, ashtrays, tiles and stoneware figurines in soft, curved and slightly lopsided organic shapes, such as pebble forms, leaf dishes, squashed spheres and wide-hipped jugs. Glazes are matte and richly mottled, in sage green, rust, mustard, deep turquoise, charcoal grey and cream, often with sgraffito, incised stripes and dots, circles, linear figures, animal motifs and silhouetted leaves. A signature type is a dark green-brown stoneware inlaid with silver, formed in rounded jars with a delicate silver leaf pattern. Tableware is plain white with coloured berries and flowers. The mood is warm, humane, playful and Scandinavian modern, with intimate handmade surfaces. Surfaces show glaze pooling at the edges, visible finger marks, soft shoulders on every vase and a calm, slightly earthy palette that suits teak shelves and linen curtains.
In the style of Dale of Norway, Scandinavian wool sweaters. The garment is a thick, close-knit wool pullover or cardigan with a high collar or round neck, and a patterned yoke pattern worked in stranded colourwork, a circular band of stars, snowflakes, diamonds, reindeer, zigzags and stepped borders around the chest and shoulders. Colours are strong, with navy, red, white and grey as the classic combination, plus charcoal, forest green and cream. The garment has a snug, athletic fit and shows crisp zigzag edging, tiny star motifs in white and a thick shawl or turtle collar, as seen on alpine ski slopes in the 1960s and 1980s.
In the style of Hadeland Glassverk, Norwegian hand-blown glass. The glassware is clear, thick and slightly bubbled, mouth-blown into rounded organic shapes such as heavy tumblers, wide bowls, tall stemmed vases, candle lights and pitchers, with lightly wobbly lines. Colours are cool and soft, such as pale smoky grey, aqua green, ice-blue, amber, deep cobalt and deep purple, with swirled layers, trapped air bubbles and uneven rims. Some pieces are cut with simple vertical grooves or concentric rings, others engraved with fir branches or snowflakes. Mid-century pieces have fluid free-form silhouettes, such as leaf-shaped bowls and asymmetrical vases. The mood is clean, cold, bright and Nordic, evoking frozen lakes and fjords. Lighting through the glass creates soft pools of colour on tabletops, and the overall impression is hushed, handmade, a little irregular and unmistakably Scandinavian.
In the style of Fiskars, Finnish scissors and garden tools. In the style of Lladro, Spanish porcelain figurines.
