in the log house the table is laid with round loaves, pancakes, boiled meat, eggs and dark beer in a wooden ladle carved in the shape of a duck, and the head of the house, a man in his sixties in a white linen shirt embroidered in red at the collar and hem, calls the dead to come and eat
before kalam the bathhouse is heated for the dead, steam drifting from its small door, the household leaving a wooden bucket of hot water, a birch bath whisk and a clean linen towel inside for the ancestors
married women in their thirties to sixties in the khushpu, a close cap with a long tail down the back covered in rows of small silver coins and red beads, set out food in bowls for the dead on a bench by the door
