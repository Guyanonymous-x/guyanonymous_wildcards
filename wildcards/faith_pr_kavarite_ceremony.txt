in Fiji, at a sevusevu, the welcome rite, men in their thirties to seventies in wrap skirts of patterned cloth and printed shirts sit cross-legged facing the chief, and each claps once with cupped hands before draining the cup in one go, and behind the bowl the mixer, a man in his twenties with a bare chest and a garland of leaves, kneads the wet kava in a cloth bag and squeezes it out with both hands
in Samoa, at an ava ceremony, chiefs in their forties to seventies in wrap skirts of plain cloth, bare-chested with tattoos from the waist to the knees, sit along the posts of the house, and the talking chief Leota, in his sixties, calls out each name, and behind the bowl the taupou Sina, a woman of high rank in her twenties in a skirt of bark cloth and a necklace of red seeds, strains the kava with a bundle of pale hibiscus fiber
in Tonga, at a royal kava ceremony, men in their thirties to seventies in black wrap skirts and wide mats of finely woven pandanus tied around the waist sit in a great oval, the noble of highest rank at its head, and behind the bowl a man in his thirties stirs and wrings the ground root in a cloth with strong slow twists
in Fiji, chiefs in their fifties to seventies in skirts of brown and white bark cloth and garlands of green leaves sit at the head of the ring, and Seru, a herald in his forties, carries the full cup to them on bent knees, and Jone, a man in his forties, pours fresh water into the bowl from a long bamboo tube
