at the center Anubis, a man in his thirties with the head of a black jackal, kneels beside the tall balance and steadies its plumb line, the heart drawn as a small red pot in one pan and the white curling ostrich feather in the other, the beam perfectly level
ibis-headed Thoth, a slim man in his forties with the long curved bill of a white ibis, stands beside the balance to record the verdict, a reed pen in one hand and in the other a long narrow wooden board with two round ink wells at one end, black and red, and a slot holding thin reed pens, a small seated baboon perched on top of the balance post
Ammit the Devourer crouches beside the balance, ready to eat the heart if it proves heavy with wrongdoing, a beast with the head of a crocodile, the front body of a lion and the hindquarters of a hippopotamus, her long jaws open toward the pan
above the scales a row of forty-two judge gods sits across the top of the hall, each a small seated figure in a white kilt with a tall white feather on the head, some with human faces and some with the heads of animals
