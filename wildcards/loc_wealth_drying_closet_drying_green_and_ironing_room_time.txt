Ten on a Monday, the decision, the laundry maid looks at the sky and either the green or the closet gets the first load, and the week's plan follows from that
Eleven, if it is fine, which takes two people an hour and looks, from the park, like a flag day
On a wet day the drying closet is lit and the clothes horses rolled in
Two in the afternoon, the first horse rolled out and the linen felt rather than looked at
Four, the green cleared before the dew, which is a hard deadline
Tuesday and Wednesday, the ironing, two people, eight irons in rotation on a stove, three days of it for a weekly wash, with each iron used for ninety seconds and returned to the cooktop, so the work has a constant rhythm of walking four paces to the stove and back
Wednesday afternoon, the screw press on the sheets and the table linen
Thursday, folding
Friday, the stove cold, the table swept, the irons oiled against rust
A wet week in November, when nothing dries, the closet runs for four days and the coal bill shows it
