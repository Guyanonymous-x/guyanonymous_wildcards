In the style of Acoma Pueblo potters of New Mexico, extremely thin-walled hand-coiled ceramics with fine-line painting. Vessels are rounded jars with high shoulders and small mouths, bowls, canteens, seed pots and low wide shapes, with a very fine white slip surface that is smooth, matte and chalk-like. The walls are paper thin, and a finished piece rings clearly when tapped. Decoration is painted with a yucca-leaf brush in black and rusty orange or red-brown on the creamy white ground, in dense precise geometric bands. Motifs are fine-line lattices and hatching, interlocking triangles, parrots, thunderbirds, rainbows, stepped clouds, deer with a heart line and scrolls, with pattern bands wrapping horizontally around the shoulder and neck and leaving tidy empty zones at the base. Unglazed, low-fired and a little powdery, with slightly brown tonal variations from the fire. The look is crisp, disciplined, optical and graceful, like delicate op-art on earthenware.
