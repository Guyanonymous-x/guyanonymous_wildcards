in Birak, the hot dry early summer, camped by the shore of a coastal lake fringed with paperbark trees, the men naked but for a belt of spun possum-fur string around the waist, each with his penis and scrotum visible, small and relaxed, the women in short skin aprons, under hard white sun
in Makuru, the cold wet winter, moving inland through tall forests of jarrah and marri trees with dark rough bark, all of them wrapped in bookas, cloaks of kangaroo skin sewn together and worn fur side in, under low gray clouds and drifting rain
in Djeran, the cooler fall, camped on the bank of the Swan river where they fish with stone traps, red-flowering gum trees along the bank, in soft golden late afternoon light
in Djilba, the late winter, walking across open country spread with yellow and white wildflowers on a cultural walk with a guide, all of them in present-day jeans, boots and warm jackets, under bright cold sun between showers
in Kambarang, the warm spring, gathered for a smoking ceremony on country in a clearing of tall grass trees with dark trunks and green spiky crowns, all of them in T-shirts and jeans or long cotton skirts, in clear warm morning light
