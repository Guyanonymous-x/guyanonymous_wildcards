at the senkotsu, the bone washing, women in their forties to seventies in white kneel by the opened coffin years after the death and wash the bones of the dead woman with water and awamori liquor
at Shiimii in spring the kin in their twenties to eighties sit on mats in the tomb's courtyard, eating from lacquer boxes of food set out for the ancestors and burning paper money in a stone burner
a noro, a woman priest in her sixties in a white robe and a white headcloth, stands at the shore with a branch of green leaves raised toward the sea where Nirai Kanai lies
a yuta, a seer in her fifties in a white blouse, kneels at the tomb door and calls the ancestors, a bowl of rice, incense sticks and a cup of tea set before her
