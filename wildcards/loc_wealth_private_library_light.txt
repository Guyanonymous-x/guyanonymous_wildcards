The most carefully managed light in the house and the only room where light is treated as a hazard, two or three windows on one side, with the bookcases returning across the window wall to the reveals so that no shelf faces a window directly, which is a damage decision as much as a planning one
A strong directional daylight that lands on the library table and nowhere else, the table being positioned for it
The shelves themselves in a soft even shade that falls off toward the floor, so the bottom two shelves are read by candle or by crouching
Curtains and shutters closed whenever the room is empty in summer, which means the room is dark for most of the hours it is lit outside
Firelight on the leather chairs and on the gold tooling of the nearest shelves, which is the effect everyone remembers; and after dark a pair of candle branches at the piers and a reading lamp on the table
