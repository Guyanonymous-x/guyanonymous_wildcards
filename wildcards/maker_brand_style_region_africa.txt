In the style of Bonwire kente weaving houses of Ghana, hand-loomed narrow strips of silk and cotton, about four inches wide, woven on a horizontal strip loom and sewn edge to edge into large cloths worn draped over the shoulder. Brilliant geometric patterns of interlocking bands, squares, zigzags, stepped diamonds and blocks in gold, kente yellow, deep red, emerald green, royal blue, black and white. Weft-faced areas alternate with warp-striped plain sections, and supplementary weft motifs create tiny symbols such as ladders and checks. Surfaces are rich, thick, slightly ridged and rhythmic, each strip a different arrangement. Regal, ceremonial, celebratory mood, sunlit courtyard setting with looms and coloured threads.
In the style of Studio 189, Accra-made ethical fashion centred on hand-dyed indigo and hand-batiked cloth, adire-style resist patterns, hand-woven fabrics and block-printed African wax prints. Clothing is relaxed and modern, with wide-leg trousers, boxy tops, flowing wrap dresses, jumpsuits, kimono-style jackets and long skirts in cotton and linen. Palette includes deep indigo blue, white, earthy ochre, rust, black and bright accent colours such as orange and green, in tie-dye swirls, hand-drawn stripes, dots, geometric grids and flowing batik lines. Visible hand-dyed unevenness and crisp artisanal texture. Breezy, warm, socially conscious, sunlit West African mood.
In the style of Maxhosa Africa, South African knitwear built on bold Xhosa beadwork patterns turned into jacquard knit, with sharp geometric bands of triangles, diamonds, chevrons, zigzags, stepped blocks and horizontal stripes. Colours are vibrant and contrasting, such as red, orange, mustard, cobalt blue, emerald, turquoise, pink and black against cream or deep neutral grounds, in rich saturated blocks. Items include chunky sweaters, cardigans, blankets, scarves, beanies, long coats, dresses and trousers in mohair, merino wool and soft cotton. Patterns repeat symmetrically across the chest and sleeves like beaded collars. Warm, proud, celebratory, heritage-meets-contemporary mood.
In the style of Lisa Folawiyo, Lagos womenswear mixing African wax print cotton with heavy hand-embellishment, using beads, sequins, crystals, pearls, metallic thread and cowrie-like details sewn in dense clusters across bodices, collars, sleeves and hems. Silhouettes are modern and sculptural, with cropped jackets, peplum waists, structured shoulders, ruffled trumpet skirts, fitted dresses, wide trousers and playful cut-outs. Prints are bold and graphic in electric blue, tangerine, fuchsia, yellow, emerald and black and white, with embroidery that highlights the pattern outlines. Glamorous, festive, Afro-modern mood, glossy with sparkle and contrast textures.
In the style of Vlisco, wax-print cotton cloth sold across West and Central Africa, with enormous vividly coloured designs printed identically on both sides with resin, leaving fine crackle veins where dye seeped through the wax. Motifs are oversized flowers, fruit, birds, feathers, fish, keys, eyes, circles, hands, clocks, fans, scrolling vines and bold geometric patterns, outlined and shaded like block-print drawings. Palette is saturated and clashing such as cobalt and orange, scarlet and green, yellow and violet, turquoise and pink, black and white, with the crackle texture. The cloth is cut into wrapped skirts, head ties, tailored dresses and matching sets. Exuberant, expressive, festive market-stall mood.
In the style of Thebe Magugu, South African contemporary womenswear that combines sharp tailoring with storytelling prints, with structured blazers, pleated skirts, shirt dresses, cropped jackets, suits and wrapped pieces. Fabrics include wool, silk, crisp cotton and leather, with printed illustrations of portraits, plants, historical imagery and map-like graphics. Palette moves between deep forest green, burgundy, cobalt blue, mustard, cream, black and pastel pink, with precise seams, contrasting piping, strong shoulders and nipped waists. Playful and intellectual, polished, artful and modern Johannesburg mood, shown in clean gallery light.
In the style of Fes Zellij Workshops, mosaic panels of hand-cut glazed clay tiles inlaid into an interlocking geometric puzzle. Stars with eight, twelve or sixteen points radiate from the centre, with kite, hexagon and chevron pieces in vibrant glossy colours, namely cobalt blue, emerald green, saffron yellow, white, black, deep red-brown and turquoise. Fountains, walls and floors carry the repeating tessellations, and edges are crisp with thin white grout lines. Borders carry calligraphic bands or carved plaster, and surfaces sit below carved cedar and stucco. The result is a precise, endlessly repeating, jewel-like pattern.
In the style of Ntonso Adinkra Cloth Makers, handwoven cotton cloth stamped with symbols using carved calabash stamps and a dark, glossy tree-bark dye. Rows of small, bold black motifs, such as concentric circles, spirals, crosses, ladders, hearts and stylised plants, repeat inside a grid divided by hand-drawn comb lines. Backgrounds are dyed deep red, brown, indigo, black or left natural cream, and mourning cloths are very dark. Symbols are slightly irregular, with inky edges, giving a handmade rhythm and graphic quality. The cloth is wrapped around the body like a toga and has stitched strips joined together.

In the style of Kenya Airways, an African airline identity in red, white and black. Aircraft have a white fuselage with the airline name in black italic capitals along the forward body, and a bright red tail fin carrying a stylised K inside a circle, in white and black. Older aircraft had four coloured stripes of red, green, black and white running the length of the fuselage in the colours of the national flag. Engines are red or white. A small flag appears near the cockpit, along with the slogan about pride of Africa. Cabins use red and charcoal seats with white head covers, grey carpets, and patterned fabrics with woven motifs. Crew wear red uniforms with black and white scarves and neat caps. Mood is warm, hospitable, proud, bright and savannah-like, with red against clear blue sky.
In the style of Castle Lager, a South African lager with a heraldic castle. In the style of Da Gama Textiles, South African shweshwe printed cotton. The fabric is a stiff starched cotton with a papery finish, ninety centimetres wide, printed with all-over repeating patterns that look like an intricate engraving. The classic colour is indigo blue with white discharged motifs, alongside chocolate brown, deep red, plum, teal and mustard. Motifs are small geometric diamonds, squares, circles, stripes, flowers, paisleys and tiny dotted trellis designs, finely detailed from the pinned engraving technique and very regular. The reverse is a flat solid colour and bears small printed brand marks of three cats. Garments include full A-line skirts, wrap dresses, aprons, headwraps and wedding outfits with panels sewn side by side, edged in braid and ribbon. Mood is traditional, cheerful, communal, crisp and deeply colourful. The cloth holds sharp creases when ironed, shows a faint white chalky bloom along folds, and is often seen in village yards, hanging on lines in bright sun.
In the style of Basotho Blankets, thick woolen blankets worn as wraps in Lesotho. Colours are strong and earthy, with ochre, maroon, navy blue, forest green, black and cream. The blanket is pinned at the shoulder with a large safety pin and draped over riders on horseback wearing rubber boots and a conical straw hat. Mood is proud, blanket-wrapped, mountain-chilled and heritage-rich, set against rocky highlands in cool light.
In the style of Ardmore Ceramic Art, flamboyant hand sculpted South African ceramics. In the style of Kazuri, Kenyan hand made ceramic beads and pottery. Beads are rolled and shaped by hand from local clay, then glazed in glossy colours and fired, so each one is slightly unique and irregular. Shapes are round balls, discs, short tubes, cones, ovals, chunky cylinders and tiny seeds, with smooth glass-like surfaces and a visible hole. Colours include cobalt blue, cherry red, mustard yellow, forest green, turquoise, terracotta orange, black, white and speckled earthy brown. Some are hand-painted with swirls, dots, stripes and zigzags in contrasting glaze. They are strung into chunky necklaces, multi-strand bracelets, long beaded ropes and earrings on cotton thread or leather cord, often mixing many colours. Pottery includes mugs and bowls with matching speckled glaze. Mood is joyful, earthy, handmade, colourful and sunny.
