Half past six, the butler up, the bed folded against the safe, the lamps collected from the principal rooms for trimming
Eight, the plate counted out for breakfast and counted back
The morning, lamps, knives, glass, the wine book, the cellar
Midday
Four, decanting for dinner, which is an hour of careful work by a window
Seven, the pantry at its busiest, three men, forty pieces of plate, nine trays, the bells going
Half past ten, the women gone up, the men still at the dining table, the pantry waiting
One in the morning, the plate washed, dried on chamois, counted a third time, locked in, the safe keys on a cord round a neck, the bed folded down, and a man sleeping against a door with sixty thousand pounds behind it
