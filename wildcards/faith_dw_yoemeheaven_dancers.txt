the maaso, the deer dancer, a man in his twenties, bare-chested, with a deer head tied on a white cloth on top of his head, red ribbons on the antlers, a shawl wrapped at his hips and strings of dry moth cocoons wound around his legs, dances with a gourd rattle in each hand
pascola dancers, men in their thirties to their fifties, bare-chested, with small black wooden masks pushed to the side of their heads, rows of moth-cocoon rattles around their ankles and a belt of brass bells at the waist, dance before the ramada
during the Lenten Waehma, chapayekas, masked men in their twenties to their forties in helmet masks of painted hide with long ears and noses, blankets over their shoulders and deer-hoof rattles at the belt, walk through the town
matachines, men in their twenties to their sixties in tall crowns of paper flowers and ribbons and bright scarves, each carrying a gourd rattle and a wand of feathers, dance in two rows before the church in honor of the Virgin
