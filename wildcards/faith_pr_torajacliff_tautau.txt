the tau-tau, lifelike wooden figures of adult men and women carved from pale jackfruit wood, stand shoulder to shoulder in a balcony cut into the rock, each with white-painted eyes and black hair, dressed in real faded shirts, jackets, sarongs and headcloths, their right hands held out with open palms
older tau-tau, worn gray with age, their faces simple and round with large staring eyes of shell, stand in a long row behind a wooden rail on a ledge, wrapped in faded blankets and woven cloths
new tau-tau with finely carved faces, each a portrait of the dead person as an older adult with wrinkles and gray-painted hair, stand behind a carved railing in clean shirts and batik headcloths, glasses on the face of one of the men
