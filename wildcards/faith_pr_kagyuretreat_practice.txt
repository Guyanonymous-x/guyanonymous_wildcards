in the shrine room the retreatants do prostrations, each on a long polished wooden board, sliding flat along it on cloth pads under the hands and rising again, the boards worn smooth and pale by tens of thousands of bows
at dawn in the snow-covered courtyard, retreatants in their thirties to fifties sit cross-legged and bare-chested in thin white cotton cloths, practicing tummo, the yoga of inner heat, and wet cotton sheets laid over their shoulders steam and dry on their bodies
the retreatants practice trulkhor, the yoga of breath and movement, bare-chested in short cotton shorts, leaping up from cross-legged sitting and dropping back down onto thick cushions, their long hair flying
a retreatant in his forties practices chod, the rite of cutting through the self, shaking a two-sided hand drum of wood and painted skin in the right hand and lifting to his lips a trumpet made from a real human thighbone in the left
a retreatant in her fifties sits in a meditation box, a square wooden seat with low walls on three sides, in which she also sleeps sitting upright through the night, her eyes half closed and her hands folded in her lap
