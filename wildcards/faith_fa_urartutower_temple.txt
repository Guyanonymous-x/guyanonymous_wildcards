the temple at Ayanis, the fortress of King Rusa on a headland above the blue water of Lake Van in eastern Turkey, the tower standing at the back of a paved court ringed by square pillars, its lower walls of smooth dark basalt blocks cut on both sides of the doorway with long rows of small wedge-shaped strokes, and inside its single room a low platform of pale alabaster carved with fine outlines of lions, bulls and winged creatures
the temple at Altıntepe, a fortress on a steep cone-shaped hill above a green river plain near Erzincan in eastern Turkey, the square tower standing in the middle of a courtyard with a covered walk on all four sides, the roof of the walk carried on wooden columns set on round stone drums, the walls behind painted with bands of rosettes, winged figures and kneeling bulls in blue, red and white
the temple at Çavuştepe, the fortress of King Sarduri strung along a narrow knife-backed ridge above a wide farmed valley southeast of Lake Van, the tower rising at the upper end of the ridge, its base of black basalt blocks polished until they shine as dark glass, fitted with hairline joints and cut with rows of small crisp wedge-shaped strokes
the temple at Toprakkale, the fortress of King Rusa on a spur of bare rock above the plain of Van, the tower of pale and dark stone laid in alternating courses standing at the cliff edge, a broad stair cut in the rock climbing to its court, and the roofs of the storehouses and the walls of the fortress stepping down the slope below it
