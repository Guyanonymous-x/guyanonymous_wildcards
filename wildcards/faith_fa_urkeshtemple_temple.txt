Urkesh seen from the plaza about 2250 BC, the gray stone wall of the terrace curving away to both sides taller than two men, the monumental stair rising through it, some twenty-five broad steps of rough-cut stone slabs with a wider fan of low steps flaring out on one side for people to sit, and high above against the sky the plastered mud brick temple with one door
the top of the Urkesh terrace, the temple of the god Kumarbi built by King Tish-atal, one long room of thick mud brick walls plastered pale brown on a footing of stones, a ramp of packed earth leading to its door, a stone statue of a crouching lion as long as a man's arm beside the entrance, and the whole plain of wheat fields and far blue hills spread below
