before dawn a priest kindles the new fire by friction, spinning a straight wooden drill between the palms against a dry hearth board until a glowing coal drops into a nest of shredded bark, in the gray light before sunrise
at midday the first green ears of corn in their husks are laid on the new fire as the first offering, and white smoke rises past the thatched temple on the mound top, under a hot bright summer sun
at night a long line of dancers circles the new fire counterclockwise, men in front and women behind, stamping in step, their bodies lit orange by the leaping flames against the black sky
in the late afternoon the people drink a dark cleansing tea of boiled holly leaves, the black drink, from cups cut from great whelk shells, and some vomit at the edge of the plaza to cleanse the body, in warm gold light
