In the style of Din Tai Fung, a Taiwanese dumpling restaurant with an exacting, clean presentation. Dumplings are small steamed buns with paper-thin translucent skins, each gathered by hand into a tidy spiral of eighteen fine pleats with a little knot at the top, sitting in a round bamboo steamer lined with a leaf or paper, with glints of broth showing. Open glass-walled kitchens show white-uniformed cooks in caps, masks and gloves rolling dough on stainless steel tables under bright white light, weighing each piece on small scales. Dining rooms are modern, with pale oak tables, blond wood screens, red brushed lettering signs, dark slate floors and slatted lanterns, and tea served in plain white cups. Condiments come in tiny dishes of black vinegar with sliced ginger. Menus are glossy cards with photographs. Mood is meticulous, hygienic, bustling and warm, with a limited palette of cream, bamboo tan, steam white and a single red accent.
