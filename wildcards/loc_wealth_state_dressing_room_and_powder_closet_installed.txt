in practice the most used room in the state apartment and frequently the best-decorated room in the whole house, which is the fact most likely to surprise, because the dressing room is where a person actually sat for two hours a day
it is where the best pictures were hung, and a great many of the finest picture collections in these houses were formed in and for a dressing room rather than a gallery; placed between the state bedroom at record 10 and the next room in the sequence
with doors to both, so that it also functions as a passage; a powder closet opening off a corner
with a stool
a hook
a shelf and a pierced tin or louvred ventilator to a flue or an outside wall, whose entire purpose is to contain the cloud of starch powder so it does not get into the bedroom silk; a chimneypiece with a small grate, because this room is heated every single day and the state bedroom is not; a clothes press built in or free-standing with sliding trays rather than hanging space
since clothes of this period are folded; a washstand position near the service door; no plumbing whatever, so every drop of water in the room arrived in a jug and left in a pail; shutters, and curtains kept thin because this is a room you need to see in; a concealed service door; and one bell pull, which in this room is used twenty times a day
