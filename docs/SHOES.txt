SHOE WILDCARDS  (files shoe_*, built by _tools/shoe_build.py after the merge)
===============================================================================
Source: your 1000_shoe_prompts_with_prices.txt.  Every sentence of it was split into slots (tier word, type, designer, material, colour, construction, toe, detail,
technique, sole, closure, practical/whimsical extras, finish, lining, price).  All harvested values are kept in _tools/data/shoe_vocab.json and every one of them is
still used; the lists were then expanded (see counts below).  Nothing in the shoe files contains curly braces or pipes.

WHAT THE SAMPLE TAUGHT US
  * Each prompt is one paragraph: "A pair of <tier word> <type> designed by <designer>, made of <material> in <colour>, <construction>. They have <toe> and feature <detail>, <technique>.
    They rest on <sole>. <closure>. <practical/whimsical extras>. [They are finished with <jewel/finish>.] <lining>."   (the sample's price overlay sentence is NO LONGER in any prompt)
  * QUALITY drives everything: six tiers, and the tier decides the tier word, materials, construction method (cement-bonded ... hand-sewn at forty stitches per inch ... a guild of artisans),
    hardware (plastic buckle ... white-gold buckle set with sapphires), lining and finish, and the PRICE BAND.

NOTE: construction lines contain no colour or thread-colour words (colours come only from shoe_colour*); stitching is mentioned only generically (contrast stitching, gold-wire welt, silk thread).

THE SIX TIERS                                   price band (EUR)       share in shoe_prompt_master
  t1 budget            inexpensive, no-frills ...      12 - 57                22 %
  t2 value             sturdy, practical ...           40 - 155               24 %
  t3 mid-range         well-crafted, quality ...       135 - 595              22 %
  t4 premium           polished, refined ...           510 - 1,950            16 %
  t5 luxury couture    artisanal, exquisite ...        4,000 - 20,000         11 %
  t6 museum-grade      one-of-a-kind, opulent ...      110,000 - 311,000       5 %

SEVEN CLASSES (about 280 types):  boot, heel, flat, sneaker, sandal, slipper, clog.  Each type is tagged with how it closes (lace, hook, zip, button, buckle, strap, hookloop, elastic, pull, slip,
ribbon, thong, toggle) and what sole/heel it has (high, mid, stack, cuban, wedge, platform, flat, sport, lug, soft, wood ...), so a loafer slips on and a stiletto gets a heel, not a lug sole.
Open shoes (sandals, flip-flops) get a footbed sentence instead of a lining.

FINISHED PROMPTS
  shoe_prompt_master                 any tier (cheap more often), any class
  shoe_prompt_t1 .. shoe_prompt_t6   one tier          shoe_prompt_boot|heel|flat|sneaker|sandal|slipper|clog   one class
  shoe_full_<class>_t<n>             the 42 files of 250 finished prompts each (coherent type/sole/closure/lining); shoe_full_t<n>, shoe_full_<class>, shoe_full pick among them
  shoe_prompt_mix_t<n>, shoe_prompt_mix   "free mix": every slot picked independently from the component files (closest to the sample's freedom; also shows how to build your own templates)
  shoe_prompt_feet                   close-up of someone's feet in shoes (uses shoe_worn)
For character prompts:  shoe_worn (everyday shoes, mostly t1-t3), shoe_worn_<class>, shoe_worn_senior (comfort shoes), shoe_floor.  Example:  "A full-length portrait of __f_prompt_master_by_age__, __shoe_worn__"
(the existing outfit files were NOT edited: put __shoe_worn__ where you want shoes mentioned).
