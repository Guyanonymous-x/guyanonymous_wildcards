the great temple of Amun at Jebel Barkal by Napata, built against the foot of a flat-topped mountain of red-brown sandstone that rises sheer from the desert beside the Nile, a tall needle of rock standing free at the mountain's corner in the shape of a rearing cobra, the temple's pylons, courts and halls running out from the cliff toward the river, and six gray granite rams lying on pedestals along the road to its door
the temple of Amun at Kawa, built by King Taharqa about 680 BC among date palms on the east bank of the Nile, a pylon of pale yellow sandstone carved with the king as a sphinx trampling foes, a court of palm-shaped columns, and before the doorway granite rams lying with a small standing figure of the king sheltered under each chin
the temple of Amun at Naqa in the dry grassland east of the Nile, built by King Natakamani and Queen Amanitore about AD 50, reached by a ramp and an avenue of twelve sandstone rams with tightly curled fleece lying six on each side, a small kiosk of columns in the middle of the avenue, and the doorways of the temple standing one behind another in a straight line up the slope
the Lion Temple at Naqa, a single-roomed temple of red-brown sandstone behind a pylon carved on one tower with King Natakamani raising an axe and on the other with the broad, heavy Queen Amanitore raising a sword, each grasping a bunch of enemies by the hair, a lion mauling fallen prisoners at their feet, the lion god Apedemak carved on the back wall with three lion heads and four arms, and on the pylon's edge as a snake with a lion's head and human arms rising from a leafy acanthus plant, its broad, deeply cut leaves curling outward
the Lion Temple at Musawwarat es-Sufra, a small temple of yellow sandstone in a wide valley ringed by low hills, built about 230 BC, its outer walls carved with a row of gods and the king in procession, lions and elephants leading bound captives, six columns inside carved with animals, and before it a walled court with stone lions seated on either side of the door
the temple of Amun at Meroe, the royal city on the Nile, a long temple of red brick faced with sandstone, its pylon plastered white and painted, a paved avenue lined with stone rams and small shrines leading up to it from the east, and beyond the city wall the low black hills crowned with rows of small steep-sided pyramids
