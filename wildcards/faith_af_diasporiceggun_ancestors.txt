the Egungun of the island of Itaparica, tall masked forms covered from head to foot in layered panels of bright cloth sewn with mirrors, sequins and beads, sweep through the yard, men in their twenties to fifties in white holding long rods beside them
the ancestors stand behind the living as pale figures of men and women in their fifties to nineties, some in the clothes of the plantation days and some in white, their hands on the shoulders of their kin
Baron Samedi, the lord of the dead in Haitian Vodou, a tall man in his forties in a black tailcoat and top hat, his face painted as a white skull, stands at a cemetery cross among the graves
