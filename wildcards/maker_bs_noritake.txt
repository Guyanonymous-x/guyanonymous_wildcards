In the style of Noritake, hard white porcelain tableware of the early twentieth century export period, glossy bone-white glaze with a faint bluish translucency, formal dinner sets with rounded lidded tureens, scalloped plates and footed cups with closed loop handles and small thumb curls. Decoration is hand painted and gilded, with wide borders of heavy gold, cobalt blue and deep rose red, central panels of lake scenes with cottages and trees in sunset orange and teal, and moriage work where slip trailed dots and beads of enamel stand proud of the surface like raised pearls. Later Art Deco sets turn angular, with stepped triangles, black and orange bands, stylised fans, sunbursts and parrots in pearly lustre glazes with a rainbow sheen. Lids carry bud or fruit finials. The underside shows a small green or magenta backstamp of a laurel ring around a letter M. Mood is refined, ornate, glittering and slightly nostalgic, like a treasured cabinet set from a grandmother house.
