the miring, the feeding of the gods, a row of plates on a mat holding puffed rice, rice cakes, boiled eggs, betel nut and tobacco, an elder in his seventies waving a live rooster by its legs over the plates and the seated guests
a pig lying bound on the gallery floor, a man in his forties cutting its throat with a long knife, and its dark liver then laid on a white plate and studied closely by three elders in their sixties
the bards walking the length of the gallery in single file, four men in their fifties to seventies in woven jackets and feathered caps, each striking the floor with a tall wooden staff as he recites
the festival tree at the middle of the gallery, a tall pole hung with packets of food, bottles, cigarettes and cloth, men in their twenties and thirties dancing around it with wooden shields and long knives
women in their forties pouring tuak, cloudy white rice wine, from kettles into small glasses for a long line of guests seated cross-legged on the mats, the house chief at the head of the line
