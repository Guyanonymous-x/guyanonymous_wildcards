the salute, a line of ounsi in their twenties to sixties in white dresses and white head wraps, barefoot, turning and dipping at the knee before the center pole while a manbo in her fifties in a white lace dress shakes the beaded rattle and pours a thin stream of water from a jug at the pole's foot
the dance circling the pole counterclockwise, women and men in white in their twenties to sixties moving with shoulders rolling and skirts swinging, three drummers in their thirties, bare-armed and sweating, striking the drums with hands and sticks, onlookers pressed around the edge
a possession, Rosemène, a woman in her forties ridden by Ogou the warrior, eyes wide and jaw set, a red scarf tied around her arm and another around her head, swinging a machete flat-bladed overhead while two ounsi in their thirties steady her and Wilfrid, a man in his fifties, offers her rum and a cigar
a sacrifice, an oungan in his sixties in a blue shirt and straw hat holding a speckled rooster by the wings, passing it over the heads of kneeling initiates in their twenties and thirties, then twisting its neck and letting the blood drip onto the cornmeal drawing and the base of the pole
