the lower branches hung with strips of cloth in white, red, blue and yellow, some new and bright, others faded to gray and torn into threads by the weather, the knotted strips crowded so thickly along some boughs that the bark is hidden
white towels and long white cloths tied around the trunks and fluttering from the branches, some with bands of red embroidery at their ends, knotted at chest height in thick layers one over another, the newest bright and clean and the oldest gray-green with moss
ribbons, small brass bells, bundles of herbs and strings of beads tied among the twigs, thin ribbons of many colors knotted in bunches, the little bells dull gold and green with age, the dried herb bundles bound with thread, and loops of glass beads catching the light
the horns of goats and rams fixed to the trunks and piled in the forks of branches, curved and spiraled horns bleached gray and yellow-white by the weather, some joined in pairs on a piece of skull, older ones cracked and green with moss, stacked in heaps where the big limbs divide
the bark bare and untouched, with only a few coins pressed into the cracks of one great trunk, the edges of small copper and silver coins showing as dull glints in the deep furrows, some so old that the bark has begun to grow around them
