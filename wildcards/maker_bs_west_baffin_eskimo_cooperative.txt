In the style of the Cape Dorset Inuit printmaking and carving cooperative in Arctic Canada, bold stonecut prints and soapstone sculpture. Prints show simple powerful animals such as owls, geese, walruses, bears, seals, caribou, ravens and spirit figures with big expressive eyes and strong feathers drawn as repeating curved shapes, printed with flat ink in a limited palette on thin pale cream or white handmade paper. The ink is deep black, with blue-grey, ochre, red-orange, green and teal appearing as flat blocks, with a soft grain from the stone and a subtle ridge around each shape, often with generous empty paper space around them. Stone carvings are smooth, rounded, polished dark green-grey or black soapstone and serpentine in heavy, compact forms of bears, birds, hunters, mother and child, dancing figures and hybrid creatures. The mood is quiet, northern, graphic and dignified, with a simple, bold and direct line.
