above the hall the dead man in his seventies, in a white kimono with a small bag of six coins at his neck, wades the Sanzu river of the dead, its three crossings of shallows, rapids and deep water stretching across the scene
on the far bank of the river the old hag Datsueba, a gaunt woman in her eighties with sagging bare breasts and wild white hair, strips the clothes from the dead and hangs them on a tree whose branches bend to weigh their sins
in the hall of the Ten Kings the judge Enma, a heavy man in his fifties in a red Chinese robe and a square black crown, sits behind a desk with a scroll as demons drag the dead before a round mirror that shows their deeds
in the hells of the scroll red and blue demons in tiger-skin loincloths boil naked sinners in their thirties to sixties in an iron cauldron and drive others up a hill of upright blades, their bodies cut and bleeding
Amida Buddha in gold robes descends on a cloud with his bodhisattvas, Kannon in her forties holding out a gold lotus seat for the dead woman Haruko, in her eighties, to rise to the Pure Land of the west
