the monumental stairway of Urkesh, broad steps of rough gray stone climbing the side of the mound to the temple terrace, wider at the bottom and narrowing toward the top, some two dozen steps of big unshaped blocks fitted dry and worn smooth along their front edges, a wide fan-shaped apron of smaller stones flanking them on one side in rough low tiers, pale lichen and dry grass in the joints, and the brown earth slope of the mound rising above the last step to the open sky
the stairway seen from below, its stone steps rising steeply, walls of mud brick at its sides, each gray step a row of heavy boulders as high as a knee, stacked one behind another until they fill the view, the flanking walls of flat sun-dried bricks the color of dry earth crumbling at their tops, and at the head of the flight a thin edge of the temple terrace against a pale blue sky
a plaza at the foot of the stairway, paved with stone, where the people of the city gathered, a broad open floor of trodden earth and flat gray paving stones spreading out below the first step, bounded on one side by the tall curving stone wall of the terrace, with the excavated sides of the dig rising around it in clean-cut layers of brown and ash-gray soil
