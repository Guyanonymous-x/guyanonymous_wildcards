the shrine of Sharfadin at the north foot of Mount Sinjar in northern Iraq, a low square hall of pale yellow limestone blocks with a single tall ribbed cone of the same stone rising from its roof, a gilded ball and crescent on the cone's point, an arcaded courtyard of pointed arches before its door, and the bare tawny ridge of the mountain behind
the shrines of Bashiqa and Bahzani, twin towns at the foot of a bare hill on the plain of Nineveh in northern Iraq, a dozen white ribbed cones of different heights rising above the flat roofs and the gray-green olive groves, the nearest a whitewashed cube with a fluted cone three times its own height, a low arched door and a walled yard of swept earth
a village shrine in the Shekhan hills of northern Iraq, a single whitewashed room hardly larger than a garden shed with a stubby ribbed cone on its roof, standing on a knoll among the upright headstones of the village graveyard, a mulberry tree beside it hung with knotted strips of colored cloth
the shrine of Chel Mera, the Forty Men, on the highest crest of Mount Sinjar in northern Iraq, a small hall of rough gray-brown stone with a white ribbed cone, alone on a windy ridge of bare rock and thin grass, a stony track climbing to it and the brown plain spread far below on both sides
the Yazidi holy ground at the village of Aknalich on the Ararat plain of Armenia, the small Ziarat shrine of pale cut stone with one ribbed cone, and beside it the far larger temple opened in 2019, of white marble with seven ribbed cones around a tall central one, each tipped with a golden sun, and the snow cone of Mount Ararat on the horizon
a shrine above a village in the foothills of Mount Sinjar, a cube of mud-plastered stone washed pale ocher with two ribbed cones side by side, one tall and one short, a strip of red cloth tied to the iron crescent on each, fig and pomegranate trees in its walled yard and a spring running into a stone trough below
