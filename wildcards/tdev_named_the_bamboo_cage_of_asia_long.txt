the bamboo cage of Asia, a small cage of lashed bamboo, a small squat cage of thick bamboo poles lashed together with brown rope, just big enough for a crouching adult, a hinged bamboo door tied shut, empty, its four corner posts thicker than the rest and sunk into the ground, the bars spaced a hand apart so the inside shows clearly, the roof flat and woven tight, the door a small square frame tied on with loops of rope, the whole cage reaching only to an adult's waist, the bamboo poles split and greyed with age, the rope lashings frayed into tufts at every crossing, a rough wooden bowl and a coil of spare rope on the ground beside it, standing on packed earth among ferns in a museum diorama, lit by warm spotlights. Close up, the bamboo shows split nodes, a pale grey weathered surface and rope fibres sticking out at every knot. Around it the room has paper screen walls and a dark wooden floor, a low table with an ink stone and brush to one side, a hanging scroll on the wall. Through an open screen a small raked gravel garden and a stone lantern can be seen. Soft daylight through the paper screens lights it evenly
