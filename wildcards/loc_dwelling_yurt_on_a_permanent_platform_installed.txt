a Western adaptation of a Central Asian structure into a permanent low-cost dwelling, and a quite different object from its ancestor, a lattice wall and roof poles under canvas rather than felt
erected once on a raised insulated timber deck and not moved again
a crown ring glazed with an acrylic dome so it can be weathertight in rain
insulation quilted into the wall and roof build-up
a liner for appearance and condensation control
a flue through the canvas on a properly flashed and fire-stopped collar
no mains water or drainage in most cases so water is carried or piped from a standpipe and a composting toilet stands outside
electricity from a solar array and battery or a buried cable
a canvas cover with a life of around ten to fifteen years that is a recurring capital cost nobody expects
a continual battle with condensation
rodents under the deck and mould at the skirt, and a planning status that is the real governing fact of the record because in the UK and much of Europe a yurt occupied as a dwelling requires permission and very often does not have it
