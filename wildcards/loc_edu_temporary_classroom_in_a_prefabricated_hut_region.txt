English and Welsh schools, where these units are called demountables or simply the huts and where a large share of the stock has been in place for decades, the pattern reflecting capital funding that arrives for new places and not for replacing what is already there
Scottish schools, the same arrangement under the same pressures
Irish provision, where prefabricated accommodation became a sustained national issue and a large replacement program followed
American portable classrooms, extremely close in construction, siting and reputation, often in long rows behind the main building and often with the same absence of a washroom
Australian demountable classrooms, a very large and professionally managed fleet that is moved between schools as rolls change, which is the most systematic version of the idea and the one that works best
Japanese and Korean schools, where temporary classroom buildings are far less common
emergency and post-disaster school units, which are the same construction to a different brief
