the room of 1790 with a housekeeper running a staff of twenty women and issuing stores daily
the same room in 1880 at the height of the arrangement, with a printed stores book and a bell board repeated on the wall
the room in 1925 with the staff at nine, the stores bought weekly from a town grocer rather than issued, and the great cupboard half empty, which is the change that ends the post
the room in 1955 with no housekeeper, used as a sitting room by a cook-general or let as a flat
the room as a volunteers' room, an office or a shop in a house open to the public, which is very common and which usually keeps the cupboard and the fender
the room in a divided house as a bedsit, with the linen press as a wardrobe
the room mothballed, the cupboard emptied and left open, the carpet rolled
the room open to the weather, where the carpet and the horsehair go within two winters and the cupboard's four locks are found still locked on empty shelves
