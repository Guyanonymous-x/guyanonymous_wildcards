shown at Cerveteri in about 550 BC, in the Banditaccia, the great city of the dead, a wide round mound of earth grown with grass rising from a carved drum of gray-brown tufa ringed with moldings, a dark doorway at its foot, a funeral procession going down the stepped passage, men in their twenties to fifties carrying Avle, a dead man in his fifties, on a wooden litter, women in their thirties to sixties with their hair loose and their cheeks scratched raw, men in long cloaks of red and white wool, Vel, a flute player in his thirties, at their head, in the low gold light of a summer evening
shown inside the Tomb of the Reliefs at Cerveteri, a square chamber carved from the rock as the main hall of a house, two thick square pillars holding a ceiling cut with a ridge beam, walls and pillars covered in painted stucco reliefs of the household's things, swords, helmets, round shields, knives, pots, a goose, a dog and a coil of rope, in red, white and yellow, deep niches along the walls holding stone beds with carved pillows, the dead laid on them in their best clothes, a mourning woman in her forties setting a bronze bowl beside a bed, lit by the small yellow flames of oil lamps on the floor
