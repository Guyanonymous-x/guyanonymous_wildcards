In the style of Lomonosov Porcelain, thin white porcelain used as a canvas for striking graphic energy. Geometric abstract shapes in flat red, black and white, bold angular lettering, stars, factories and stylised figures in the manner of revolutionary agitprop. Also a classic cobalt net pattern with gilded cell edges and small white relief bumps. Flat bold colour, dynamic diagonal compositions, a stern idealistic mood and a historical museum feel.
In the style of Gzhel, folk decoration painted entirely in cobalt blue on bright white glaze. Brushstrokes shade fluidly from deep ink to pale sky blue, forming flowing flowers, leaves, scrolls, dots and drops that wrap around the form. Glossy smooth surfaces, a clean blue and white contrast, rounded plump forms and a joyful, fresh, handmade charm. Decoration is hand-brushed with confident single strokes, leaving plenty of bright white ground around each motif.
In the style of Khokhloma, glowing gold-like swirling patterns painted on black or deep red lacquer. Curling vines, rowan berries, strawberries, leaves and flowers in red, black, gold and green flow in rhythmic curves across glossy varnished wood. A shiny golden-yellow undertone shows through the hardened varnish. Rich, warm, curvilinear, festive and fairy-tale peasant treasure. Ornament is hand-brushed in confident repeating curls, evenly spread over the whole surface with little empty ground.
In the style of Kutahya and Iznik ceramics, intense colour painted under crystal-clear glaze, with cobalt blue, turquoise, sage green, tomato red and white. Symmetrical, flowing patterns of tulips, carnations, hyacinths, rosebuds, long saw-edged leaves and curling arabesques repeat across surfaces, with radiating floral rosettes and tile-like repetition. Glossy, bright, crisp and elegant, evoking mosque walls and bazaars. Colour areas are crisp and flat, with fine dark outlines and bright white ground showing between motifs.
In the style of Talavera de Puebla, thick creamy white glossy tin glaze painted by hand in cobalt blue, yellow, green, orange and black. Flowers, birds, vines, geometric borders and Moorish-inspired arabesques are laid down in bold brushwork, with slightly uneven edges, drips and pooling glaze. Rustic, festive, joyful, like a sunlit courtyard and hacienda kitchen. Colours stay bright and flat, edged with a thin dark outline, with white ground left between motifs.
In the style of Royal Selangor, polished silvery grey pewter with a soft lustrous gleam and either smooth or hammered finish. Heavy-bottomed, rounded forms with simple curves, sometimes mixed with dark wood or leather accents. Engraved or embossed decoration of hibiscus flowers, bamboo, batik-like flowers and rope borders. Cool, bright, refined, with a tropical colonial elegance. Surfaces are clean and tactile, with ornament kept shallow and restrained against large plain areas of polished metal.
