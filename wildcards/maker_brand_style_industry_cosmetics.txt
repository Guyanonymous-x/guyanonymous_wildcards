In the style of Aesop, amber brown glass or matte brown plastic bottles and tubes with plain cream-white paper labels, set in neat black type in a thin, clean serif such as a classical roman with a long detailed ingredients list below. Pump dispensers in black or brass, squat apothecary jars, and aluminium tubes in muted tones. Stores have raw timber shelves, concrete floors, brass sinks, stacked brown bottles and dim warm lighting. Palette of amber brown, cream, black and grey-green. The look is literate, apothecary-inspired, restrained, tactile, minimal and quietly luxurious.
In the style of Lush, handmade soaps cut in large hunks, bath bombs and shampoo bars displayed naked on wooden shelves and wicker baskets, coloured in vivid pinks, greens, purples and blues with swirling marbled patterns. Black plastic pots with bold, handwritten black-marker labels on kraft-brown or white stickers, and small pots stamped with marker-pen doodles. Stores are open-fronted with green and black decor, chalkboards and exposed wood. The scent drifts outside. Look is hand-made, playful, messy, with hand-lettered brush type and kraft wrapping, bright and intense.
In the style of The Body Shop, forest-green painted shop fronts and shelves, with a glossy dark green and white label, plain plastic bottles with a simple matt coloured cap and thin white typography. Clear plastic bottles of lotions in rows, with a stack of small refill pots, jute and kraft paper bags, wicker baskets, and hand-written ingredient stickers. A forest-green painted panel with white lettering runs across the signboard. Natural ingredients such as shea, cocoa and tea tree are represented by small simple illustrations. The look is earnest, earthy, ethical, with a green and white palette and plain utilitarian containers.
In the style of Yardley, English lavender soap and talc tins with a pale lilac and white palette, decorated with a woodcut-style vignette of lavender flower sellers in a flowing, curved design. Cream-white oval soap cakes in thick paper wrappers printed in violet and purple, with a copperplate script wordmark and ornate border lines. Round talc tins with a metal lid and a sifter top, in pastel purple, white and gold. A hint of 1930s to 1960s drugstore elegance, soft and delicate, with floral motifs and fine line illustrations.
In the style of Shiseido, Taisho and early Showa period cosmetics packaging and print advertising, where Art Nouveau curves meet Art Deco geometry. Face powder tins and glass perfume bottles have faceted shoulders, domed stoppers and slim silhouettes, with gilt metal caps and lacquer red or ivory boxes tied with ribbon. Graphics use a stylised camellia flower mark in white or gold on red, thin elegant hand drawn lettering in an Art Nouveau block script, sinuous floral borders, and flat colour illustrations of slender women with swept up hair and long necks in kimono or Western dress, painted in muted rose, jade green, indigo and warm cream. A rainbow of seven powder shades lines up in matching round boxes. Surfaces are glossy, with gold foil edging and delicate line work, and photography is soft and monochrome with theatrical lighting. Mood is luxurious, modern for its time, feminine and carefully composed, with generous empty margins around small precise decorative motifs.
In the style of Mysore Sandal Soap, a South Asian toilet soap packaged like a treasured gift. The bar is a rectangular brownish-gold cake with softly rounded edges and a pressed emblem. Its carton is a vivid rectangular box in warm red, gold and cream, with an ornate frame and a central mythical lion with an elephant head, rendered in heraldic style, with big curling mane, raised paws and a crown. A name in elegant serif capitals and a little line drawing of sandalwood leaves and flowers decorates the front, with fine print on the sides. Wrappers use a metallic gold paper with red printing. Gift boxes come in sets of three bars bound with a thin ribbon. Advertising shows sandalwood logs, a brass lota, jasmine flowers and a woman in a silk sari. Mood is traditional, perfumed, royal, heritage and calm, with sandy yellows, vermilion, and glossy gold detail.
In the style of Tiger Balm, a medicated ointment sold in small round jars. The jar is a tiny glass or plastic pot in warm hexagonal or octagonal faceted shape, with a screw lid in bright red or white. The label is a tight wrap in red or white with a bold side view tiger striding with a curved tail drawn in an orange and black woodcut manner, and the name set in Latin and Chinese characters. Tins are small round steel containers painted with a red and gold tiger, shown beside tidy lines of tiny print. Packaging boxes are rectangular cartons in red, yellow and green, with vertical layout, an arc of text and the tiger leaping in the middle. The balm itself is a waxy pale or reddish paste. Vintage posters show cartoon tigers, hand lettering and a rich orange, yellow and black palette, with a distinctive old Singapore and Hong Kong pharmacy feel. Mood is vivid, nostalgic, medicinal, bold and pungent.
In the style of Granado, a Brazilian apothecary and perfumery with a nineteenth-century pharmacy look. Soaps, talcs, colognes and creams come in simple old-fashioned packaging, glass bottles with cork or screw stoppers, frosted apothecary jars, tin boxes and paper-wrapped bars. Labels are cream, white, pale pink or soft green paper with a thin double-line border, centred oval cartouches, fine ornamental scrollwork and curling flourishes, with text in elegant serif and decorative Victorian display lettering in deep burgundy, bottle green, navy or gold. Soap wrappers are printed with tiny repeating floral and botanical prints and sometimes a small crest with a crown. Products are displayed in shop interiors of dark polished wood cabinets, glass-fronted shelves, marble counters and tiled floors in black and white or terracotta. The mood is nostalgic, refined and slightly aristocratic, a clean tropical pharmacy from another century with muted pastel and old-gold accents and restrained, tidy typography.
In the style of Claus Porto, Portuguese soap and perfumery packaging. Each bar of soap is wrapped in thick paper with a lavishly patterned lithographic print in the Art Deco and Belle Epoque style, such as peacock feathers, ferns, roses, swallows, geometric fans, arcs and rays. Colours are rich and layered, with teal and gold, burgundy and cream, coral and navy, emerald and gold, with metallic foil stamping, a fine coloured border and a central oval or square label with ornate lettering in elegant serif and decorative capitals, and a tiny crest or ribbon. The soaps are heavy, smooth, pale creamy rectangles, embossed with a crest. Bottles are rounded glass in a vintage pharmacy style with a stopper. The shop interior has a high ceiling, wooden shelves, and colourful rows of soap boxes. The mood is nostalgic, glamorous, and carefully crafted.
