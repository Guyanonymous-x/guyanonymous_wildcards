the Coricancha at Cusco in Peru, the first temple of the Inca empire, long halls of dark gray stone blocks fitted with hairline joints around a courtyard, a great curved wall at one end, a band of gold plate as wide as two hands running along the top of the walls under the thatch, and before the doorway of the sun's own hall three priests in their forties to sixties with copper-brown skin in long red and white tunics, gold discs in their stretched earlobes, raising gold cups of corn beer toward the sky
the Torreón of Machu Picchu in Peru, a small Inca tower of pale granite with one perfectly curved wall wrapped around a natural rock outcrop, a window in the curve placed so that the first sun of the June solstice falls across the carved rock inside, a cave shrine with stepped stonework beneath it, steep green peaks all around, and a priest in his fifties in a white tunic and a red headband laying coca leaves on the rock
the Inca Temple of the Sun at Ingapirca in the highlands of Ecuador, a long oval platform of green-gray stone blocks with rounded ends, twice the height of a man, a small two-roomed building with a thatched roof on top reached by a stair, open grassy hills around it, and on the platform four chosen women in their thirties to fifties in long dark dresses pinned at the shoulders with silver pins, pouring corn beer from tall jars
the Temple of the Sun at Sogamoso in Colombia, the holiest house of the Muisca, a vast round hall ringed by upright tree trunks each thicker than a man, its wall of split cane and pale clay, its cone roof of thatch as tall as a great tree, one low door facing east, a fence of tall posts around its yard, and at the door four priests in their forties to seventies with long black hair in white cotton cloaks painted with red patterns, crescents of gold hanging below their noses, holding small clay bowls of smoking resin
the Sacred Rock on the Island of the Sun in Lake Titicaca in Bolivia, where the Inca held that the sun first rose, a long crag of brown sandstone as high as a house, one face covered with sheets of gold and the other hung with fine bright cloth, a round stone basin for corn beer before it, terraced slopes and deep blue water beyond, and pilgrims in their twenties to sixties in wool tunics and mantles walking barefoot toward it
