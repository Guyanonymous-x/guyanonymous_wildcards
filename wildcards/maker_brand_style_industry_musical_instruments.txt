In the style of Zildjian Cymbal Foundry, hand-hammered bronze cymbals with a bright warm gold sheen, circular and dished with a raised central bell. The surface shows concentric lathing grooves and dense dimpled hammer marks, with darker, burnished tones in the recesses and polished highlights on the ridges. Traditional versions look dark, brown and dull gold while newer versions are brilliantly polished and mirror bright. A small hole in the bell takes the strap, edges are thin and slightly wavy, and a printed stamp with an ornate logo, in black or gold ink, sits near the bell. Variations include flat rides, crash cymbals and hi-hats.
In the style of Steinway and Sons, concert grand pianos in a glossy black polished lacquer, with a mirror-like curved rim, sweeping bentside and a large raised lid. The legs are sturdy, round and tapered, ending in brass casters, with a lyre-shaped pedal assembly in gold-toned metal beneath. A gilt cast-iron plate shows within, the name is in gold lettering on the fallboard and keys are white with ebony sharps. Decoration is elegant and restrained. Alternate finishes include dark mahogany or burl walnut. The mood is grand, opulent, formal and concert-hall dignified.
In the style of Bosendorfer, Viennese concert grand pianos with a distinctive case in glossy black or rich warm wood, such as walnut, cherry or maple, with a longer, extended bass end of extra black keys. The rim is slender and curved, with a refined and quiet profile. Legs are carved and tapering, and the music desk is lace-like with a shallow ornate shape. Edging is thin, and trim in gold or brass is tasteful. Some models have elaborate inlay, gilt carving and painted panels in the Baroque and Secession manner. The mood is aristocratic, warm, ornate and old-world.
In the style of Selmer Paris, saxophones and clarinets with an elegant curved brass body finished in lacquer, with a warm honey-gold hue, or silver plating. Keys are neatly arranged, pearly rounded buttons with fine engraving on the bell showing swirling floral scrollwork in the Art Deco manner. The neck is long and gently bent, and bells are wide and flared. Tone-hole rims are finely drawn and key guards are tidy. Cases are black and plush-lined. The mood is jazz-age, Parisian, tidy and rich in warm gold.
In the style of Taylor Guitars, acoustic guitars with a modern, clean, sleek look. Bodies are slim with a cutaway, bright natural-coloured spruce tops, and sides and back in sapele, rosewood or koa with fine grain. Finishes are thin, glossy and flawless, and the edges have rounded bevels, especially at the lower arm. Inlays are minimal, such as slim abalone or ebony markers in an angular, elegant shape, with thin bindings and a rosette ring of dark inlay. Headstocks have a tapered, smooth curve with an engraved logo. The mood is precise, bright, contemporary, and carefully finished.
In the style of Martin Guitars, classic dreadnought acoustic guitars with a large, rounded rectangular body, a warm golden spruce top that ages to amber, and rosewood or mahogany sides and back. Bindings are cream or ivoroid and dark herringbone purfling rings the top. The soundhole is circled with fine rosette rings, and the pickguard is tortoiseshell. Fretboards are dark ebony with simple pearl dots or snowflake inlays on premium models. The headstock is squared with an inlaid pearl logo. The finish is satin or gloss. The mood is traditional, folksy and rootsy.
In the style of Gretsch Guitars, large hollow-body electric guitars with a flamboyant orange or deep red-brown lacquer finish, a gold-plated pickguard and bold decoration. Details include twin cutaways, gold sparkle or white binding, a G-shaped brand cutout on the body, a large V-shaped tailpiece in gold, and a tall headstock with a wide shape. Hardware is flashy, with chrome, gold, thumbscrew plates and tall knobs. Pickups sit in gold-plated surrounds. Alternate finishes include white falcon pearl, two-tone green and sparkle. The mood is rock and roll, showy, rockabilly and retro.
In the style of Rickenbacker, electric guitars and basses with a distinctive thin semi-acoustic body, with a glossy red, fireglo sunburst, jetglo black, mapleglo natural or white finish and white plastic binding. Features include a slotted headstock, a sharply pointed cutaway horn on one side, a stylish chrome bridge cover, triangular fretboard inlays, a chrome toaster-shaped pickup and a white pickguard with a tapered contour. Some models show a rounded cutaway with a curvy body edge and a rear control panel with tiny knobs. The mood is jangly, mod, 1960s British Invasion and psychedelic.
In the style of Bechstein, concert and salon grand pianos in glossy ebony black, with a classic Germanic design. Curved cases have a sharply defined rim, bold Jugendstil carved legs or plain tapering legs, and a gold-leaf painted decoration on historic art cases showing Neo-classical or Art Nouveau scenes in muted colours. The lid is large and the music desk is carved. Gold-toned lettering marks the fallboard. Alternative woods include rosewood, walnut and burr maple with warm mellow tones. The mood is cultured, stately, ornate, and drawing-room elegant.
In the style of Yamaha Pianos, grand and upright pianos in a flawless gloss black polyester finish, with smooth, crisp straight lines and uniform surfaces. Grand pianos have a simple curved rim, a clean music desk, square or rounded legs and a gold or silver fallboard emblem with three crossed tuning forks. Uprights are boxy, with neat panels, a fold-down lid and plain brass pedals. Alternative finishes include satin ebony, polished mahogany, walnut and white. The mood is modern, tidy, dependable, schoolroom and conservatoire, with perfect reflections.
In the style of Petrof, Czech pianos. Grand and upright pianos have deep, glossy black polished cases, with smooth curved sides, a plain tall lid, rounded cheeks and thick tapered legs on brass castors. The lid is opened on a stick to show an arrangement of cast-iron plate in gold bronze, strings running at an angle and felt hammers. The name is shown in gold letters in the fallboard or on a small brass plate on the front. Alternative finishes include warm walnut, mahogany or white lacquer, with a matte or satin sheen. Upright pianos have a plain front with a music desk and simple candle brackets in older models. The keys are ivory-white with ebony black sharps, and the pedals are brass. The look is solid, dignified, classical and carefully made.
