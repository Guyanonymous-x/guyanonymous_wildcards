the mausoleum of 1760-1800 newly built as part of a park improvement, with the first interment and the first tablet, which is the moment the building's whole purpose begins
the building in 1860 with eleven tablets, nineteen loculi filled and a gardener paid a shilling a week to sweep it and keep the grove, which is the arrangement here and is the type at its most cared-for
the building in 1918, with one plaque cut by a village mason for a man buried in France and therefore not in the loculus the plaque names, which is a situation repeated in hundreds of these buildings and is the saddest single fact available about them
the building in 1935 with the family still interring but the grove unswept
the building in 1950 after the house is sold, when the mausoleum is almost always retained by deed with a right of access across somebody else's land, or is included in the sale by accident, or is left in a legal limbo belonging to nobody, and this is the real subject of the record's later history, because a mausoleum cannot be sold, cannot be used, cannot be demolished without a faculty and an exhumation, and costs money to keep, so it is the one building on the estate that is pure liability with no conceivable return
the building in 1975 with the doors forced, the plaques broken, four loculi opened and the lead stolen off the coffins, which happened to a great many and is the specific form their vandalism takes, because the lead is worth money
the building in 1990 consolidated by a mausolea trust, a county council or a family association, with the doors welded, the oculus boarded and a notice on it, which is the commonest surviving condition
the building restored and open by appointment, which a number are
the building open to the weather, where the dome holds for a century and a half after the glazing goes, and the four blank panels stay blank and clean under the dome long after the roof of the house it belongs to has come in, which is the condition this record exists to describe
