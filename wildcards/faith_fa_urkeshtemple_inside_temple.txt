the temple of Nuzi, a Hurrian town east of the Tigris River in northern Iraq, about 1400 BC, two shrines side by side in one walled court, one for the storm god Teshub and one for the goddess Shaushka, the goddess's room hung with strings of blue and white glass beads along its walls and guarded by lions of baked clay as large as dogs, glazed green and yellow, crouching on either side of the altar
the temple of the Mitanni kings at Nagar, now Tell Brak in northeastern Syria, about 1400 BC, beside the palace on the top of a great city mound, seen through its wide doorway between walls of red mud brick, a single room behind with a brick altar against the back wall
the inside of the temple of the god Kumarbi on the Urkesh terrace about 2250 BC, one long room of thick mud brick walls plastered pale brown, light coming only from the open door, a brick altar against the far wall and a low hearth of baked clay in the floor
