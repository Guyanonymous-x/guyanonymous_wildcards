In the style of the late 1990s Nokia mobile phones, compact candy bar handsets with a rounded plastic shell, a small monochrome greenish LCD screen with chunky pixel digits, a slightly raised grid of rubber keys with curved rubbery number pad, and a curved arch navigation key. Interchangeable snap-on faceplates in bold pop art colours such as lime green, cyan blue, hot pink, orange, translucent clear, camouflage and glossy metallic, with printed pattern covers, a stubby retractable antenna or none, and a soft rounded base. Clean sans serif numerals in white or blue, cheerful pop art personalisation, lightweight and tough plastic, very early mobile phone craze look.
In the style of Braun, calm functional German industrial design of the 1950s to 1970s. Plain rectangular boxes with softly radiused corners, matte satin-finish sheet metal or textured plastic in off-white, pale grey, black and graphite. Controls are small round dials, short slide switches and flush push buttons, grouped in neat grids and set in a ruled layout. Tiny accents of orange, yellow or green mark the key control. Lettering is lowercase or clean grotesque sans-serif, thin and evenly spaced, printed in black or white on the face. Speaker grilles are fine perforated or slotted aluminium. Shavers, clocks, radios, record players and kitchen blenders share this quiet, honest, ornament-free look with generous empty space and precise tolerances.
In the style of Apple, minimalist consumer electronics with seamless slab forms. Thin rectangles with uniformly rounded corners and continuous tight radii, milled aluminium bodies in silver, space grey, gold and soft pastel anodised colours, edge-to-edge glass fronts with thin black bezels. Glossy white polycarbonate in the early 2000s, then brushed metal unibody construction. No visible screws, almost no buttons, only a few precisely cut slim keys, circular camera lenses grouped on a glass back, and a small polished logo silhouette of a bitten fruit centred on the rear. Interfaces use clean sans-serif type, generous white space, soft translucent blur layers and glossy rounded icons. Packaging is white and sparse. Mood is clean, premium and calm.
In the style of Sony, Japanese consumer electronics of the 1980s and 1990s. Compact black or silver boxes with beveled edges, brushed aluminium faceplates and dense rows of small rectangular rubber or plastic buttons. Portable cassette players in metallic blue and silver with orange accents, Trinitron televisions with deep black cabinets and flat-sided tubes, mini hi-fi stacks with tiny green and amber LED level meters and blue-green vacuum fluorescent displays. Fine stencil-like capital sans-serif labels, thin embossed model numbers, small rounded corners and engineered, high-tech miniaturised proportions. Later years bring minimalist matte black slabs with glowing coloured lighting strips. Mood is precise, technical and aspirational.
In the style of Technics, 1970s and 1980s Japanese hi-fi separates. Wide flat black or silver components with thick brushed aluminium front panels, heavy rotary knobs with knurled edges, rows of slim piano-style push keys, and rectangular illuminated meters with needle gauges or segmented green and amber LEDs. Direct-drive turntables with a flat rectangular plinth in black or silver, a strobe-dotted platter ring, a slim tonearm and a large pitch slider with finger-sized fader. Neat fine white sans-serif lettering, small engraved scale lines, rack-mount proportions and glossy dark smoked acrylic lids. Mood is serious, studio-grade and quietly luxurious.
In the style of Sega, 1990s Japanese game consoles and arcade hardware. Glossy black plastic shells with sharp angular wedge shapes and circular recessed cartridge slots, moulded ridges and vents, curved controller grips with three or six oversized round buttons and a round directional disc. Cobalt blue lettering and swirl logos, white and orange accents, loud saturated cabinet artwork with bold outlines and hard-edged speed lines, and glowing red or green power LEDs. Dreamcast-era units are pale off-white and square with a soft spiral on the lid. Typography is slanted bold italic capitals with a futuristic, fast feel. Mood is edgy, energetic and arcade-bright.
In the style of Atari, late 1970s and early 1980s American home game consoles and computers. Heavy wedge-shaped boxes clad in simulated dark woodgrain vinyl on the front with black ribbed plastic tops, chunky silver toggle switches in a row along the top edge, and thick black joysticks with a single round red button on a square base. Warm brown, black and silver palette. Cartridges are black with ridged grips and brightly illustrated paper labels showing painted spacescapes in orange, purple and teal. Logo is a stylised three-bar fuji-like trident shape in white or red with rounded geometric capitals. Computers are beige-grey with chunky keys. Mood is warm, retro-futuristic, 1970s den.
In the style of Commodore, 1980s home computers. Wide beige or brown-beige plastic keyboard-case computers with a sloped wedge profile, thick rows of chunky rectangular keys with grey and light-brown caps carrying coloured graphic symbols on their fronts, a rainbow-coloured stripe or slim dark band across the case, tiny red power LEDs and a ribbed vent grille. Matching cream-coloured floppy disk drives and cassette decks, joysticks with black bases and red fire buttons, and boxy CRT monitors with rounded dark bezels. Interfaces show a blue screen with a light-blue border and blocky pixel text. Logo is a bold angular chevron mark in stylised capitals. Mood is nerdy, hobbyist and cosy.
In the style of Olivetti, Italian office machines of the 1950s to 1970s treated as sculpture. Typewriters in bright signal red, lemon yellow, teal and orange glossy moulded plastic or pressed steel with soft flowing organic curves, rounded keyboards with white round-topped keys, and compact carry-case shells in matching colours. Calculators and computer cabinets have smooth enamelled panels, recessed rows of tiny coloured keys and small round dials. Lettering is lower-case or rounded sans-serif in white or black, often a small stripe of colour on the face. Mood is playful, elegant, optimistic and artisanal, with simple shapes polished to a gleaming industrial finish.
In the style of Bang & Olufsen, Danish luxury audio and television design. Slender, tall, minimalist forms in brushed and polished aluminium, black glass and pale oiled wood, with thin plates floating in space, tapered pyramid or cone speakers, and fabric-covered grilles in charcoal or warm grey. Controls are almost invisible, with hairline touch strips, a single large circular dial, a slim slide-down panel hiding hidden buttons, and subtle white or amber displays. Edges are razor-sharp with tiny perfectly even gaps. Palette is silver, black, natural oak, dark walnut and occasional soft earthy tones. Lettering is tiny, thin and lowercase. Mood is refined, sculptural and serene.
In the style of Nothing, transparent tech gadgets. Clear see-through plastic and glass casings that reveal internal components, circuit boards, screws and coils. Palette is white, black and transparent with a signature bright red dot accent. Phone backs have glowing white LED light strips arranged in segments around the camera. Typography is dot-matrix lettering with round dots forming monochrome numerals, and icons are pixelated. Edges are flat and squared with soft corners. Mood is stripped-back, nerdy, futuristic and quirky.
In the style of Samsung, Korean consumer electronics. Thin glossy glass slabs with curved edges, metallic frames in black, silver, gold and blue, vivid AMOLED screens with saturated colours and nearly invisible bezels. Camera modules stand out in separate circular lenses on a vertical or horizontal strip. Televisions and appliances use thin brushed metal and glossy black or white panels with minimal touch controls. Interface graphics use rounded square icons, smooth sans-serif type and soft pastel colour gradients. Mood is polished, bright, flat-glass and modern.
In the style of Xiaomi, Chinese consumer electronics. Simple matte white plastic or glass shapes with softly rounded corners, orange squared-rounded logo block, and small flat circular lens bumps. Clean minimalist products such as air purifiers, scooters and rice cookers in white and light grey with a single glowing coloured LED ring. Interface uses flat colourful icons with soft rounded corners and a friendly sans-serif font. Packaging is plain brown cardboard with a single orange square mark. Mood is clean, affordable-minimal and friendly.
In the style of Bose, American audio products. Compact, small rounded boxes and cubes in matte black or white with fabric-covered speaker grilles, curved or tapering enclosures, and small clean lettering. Headphones have plush rounded ear cups in soft black or silver with a thin padded headband and subtle buttons. Wave radios have a smooth rounded body with a brushed silver face and tiny buttons. Surfaces are soft matte and neutral with few visible fixings. Mood is understated, comfortable and home-friendly.
In the style of Sonos, wireless speakers. Smooth matte cuboid or cylinder shapes in black or white with a perforated metal mesh grille wrapped around the whole body, and a flat top with a touch-sensitive strip of tiny glowing icons. Soft rounded corners, no visible screws, a small status LED, and a tiny mark. Soundbars are long thin horizontal slabs. Mood is quiet, domestic, matte and minimal.
In the style of Zenith Radio, American radios and televisions of the 1930s to 1950s. Wood cabinets with streamlined curved corners and a large round dial with an illuminated glass face in cream with black numerals. Bakelite knobs in black or ivory, tall slim fabric grilles with gold lines, chrome trim bars and rounded speaker panels. Early televisions have wood cabinets with a small curved glass screen and rounded edges. A lightning bolt Z logo appears at the front. Mood is polished, elegant and mid-century domestic.
In the style of Philips, Dutch radios and appliances of the 1930s to 1960s. Wooden or bakelite cabinets with rounded edges, big round dials in glass with illuminated station names, and pale ivory or chocolate-brown plastics. Controls are big ribbed knobs and piano-style keys. Fabric grilles with vertical gold slats, and a small star and wave logo. Later products use bright plastic in cream, mustard and pastel with gently curved forms. Mood is homely, rounded and optimistic.

In the style of Nokia, Finnish mobile phones of the 1990s and 2000s.
