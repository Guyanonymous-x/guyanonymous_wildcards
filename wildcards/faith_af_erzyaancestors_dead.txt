beneath the earth the village of the dead lies among the birch roots, log houses as in the village above, where the ancestors, men and women in their fifties to nineties in old-style white linen, sit at their own tables waiting for the meal sent down
the soul of the newly dead man, a pale figure in his sixties in his white burial shirt, walks back along the village street on his last visit before the fortieth day, a candle in the window guiding him home
the ancestors come up out of the cemetery in a long line, grandparents and great-grandparents in their sixties to nineties, the men in white shirts belted over trousers and bark shoes, the women in embroidered tunics and tall headdresses, walking toward the open door
high in the night sky over the village stand the many log houses of Nishkepaz, the sky god of the Erzya, where the souls of good people live, men and women in their fifties to nineties in white linen at the windows, and around the houses the souls of the happy dead shine as small bright stars
