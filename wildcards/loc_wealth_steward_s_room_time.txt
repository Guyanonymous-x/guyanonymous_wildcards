Eight, the upper servants' breakfast, which is taken here and not in the hall and is cooked separately
Nine, the room empty and used as an office by whoever needs it
One o'clock, the upper servants at the head of the table, rising to withdraw for the pudding and the cheese
Two, port or beer in here with the newspaper
The afternoon, the room used for the housekeeper's and the butler's accounts
Five, the upper servants' tea
Eight, their supper, after the family's dinner has gone up
Ten, two armchairs by the fire, a lamp, a newspaper and two or three people who are not permitted to be seen upstairs and are not willing to sit downstairs
Eleven, the fire guarded and the lamp out; and a Sunday afternoon, when this is the most comfortable occupied room in the building
