the dancers, Zapotec women of the Isthmus in their twenties to sixties, called Tehuanas, in short velvet blouses and long skirts thickly embroidered with big red and pink roses, a stiff headdress of white lace framing each face, gold coin necklaces on their chests
the dancers, Zapotec men of the Danza de la Pluma in their twenties to forties, each wearing a huge round headdress of dyed feathers in rings of red, yellow and green as wide as their outstretched arms, mirrors set in it, and white trousers with fringed short capes
the dancers, Afro-Mexican men of the Costa Chica in their twenties to fifties with dark brown skin, in tattered trousers and shirts of black cloth, wearing carved wooden devil masks with horse-hair beards and tall horns
the dancers, Mixtec women in their thirties to sixties in dark wool wrap skirts tied with red sashes and white embroidered blouses, baskets of flowers on their heads, with Mixe men in their forties in white cotton and palm hats
