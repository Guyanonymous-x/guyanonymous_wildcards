the nave of 1600 after the Reformation, whitewashed over its paintings, the screen's loft cut away
the nave of 1750 full of box pews with doors, the rents recorded in a book, a three-decker pulpit halfway down and a west gallery for the singers and the band
the nave of 1870 after a restoration, the box pews and the gallery swept away, uniform benches, encaustic tiles, an organ in a chamber, the walls scraped back to bare rubble
the nave of 1950 with a congregation of eighty
the nave of 2020 with a congregation of eleven, the pews removed at the west end for chairs and a coffee urn, a lavatory in the tower, a servery
the nave shared between a congregation and a community use, a post office counter, a food bank, a concert
the nave declared closed for worship and vested in a conservation trust, maintained, unheated and opened by a keyholder
the nave converted to a dwelling, a climbing wall, a gallery or a flats scheme
