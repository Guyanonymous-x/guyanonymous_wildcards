on a stony hillside stands the grave of a headman, a long low cairn of gray and red stones, the curved horns of many oxen killed at his funeral set upright on wooden poles around it and laid on the stones, some long and lyre-shaped, some short and thick
the grave of a dead man lies under a mopane tree, a heap of stones with a row of cattle skulls with their horns set along its top, sun-bleached white, a few dry branches fenced around it
at the funeral men in their thirties to fifties kill the dead man's favorite oxen in the kraal, each with one stab to the neck, and lay the horns on the new grave as the oxen's lives go with him to the ancestors
