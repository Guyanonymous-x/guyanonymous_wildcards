In the style of Zildjian Cymbal Foundry, hand-hammered bronze with a bright warm gold sheen, circular and gently dished with a raised central swell. The surface shows concentric lathe grooves and dense dimpled hammer marks, with darker burnished tones in the recesses and polished highlights on the ridges. Older looks are dark, brown and dull gold, newer looks are brilliantly polished and mirror bright. Edges are thin and slightly wavy. Any marking is small, ornate and printed in black or gold ink. Warm, metallic, tactile and handmade.
In the style of Steinway and Sons, glossy black polished lacquer with a mirror-like curved edge and a sweeping asymmetric curved side. Supports are sturdy, round and tapered, ending in brass, with a lyre-shaped gold-toned metal assembly. A gilt metal frame shows within, lettering is in gold, and contrast comes from white and ebony. Decoration is elegant and restrained. Alternate finishes are dark mahogany or burl walnut. The mood is grand, opulent, formal and concert-hall dignified.
In the style of Bosendorfer, Viennese grandeur in glossy black or rich warm wood such as walnut, cherry or maple. Proportions are extended and generous, with a slender curved edge and a refined, quiet profile. Supports are carved and tapering, and openwork details are lace-like with shallow ornate shapes. Edging is thin, and trim in gold or brass is tasteful. Some versions have elaborate inlay, gilt carving and painted panels in the Baroque and Secession manner. The mood is aristocratic, warm, ornate and old-world.
In the style of Selmer Paris, elegant curved brass forms finished in warm honey-gold lacquer or silver plating. Details are neatly arranged, with pearly rounded buttons, and flared curves carry fine engraving of swirling floral scrollwork in the Art Deco manner. Lines are long and gently bent, with wide flared ends. Rims are finely drawn and guards are tidy. Black plush linings contrast with the metal. The mood is jazz-age, Parisian, tidy and rich in warm gold.
In the style of Taylor Guitars, a modern, clean, sleek look with slim bodies and a cutaway, bright natural-coloured spruce tones, and fine-grained sapele, rosewood or koa. Finishes are thin, glossy and flawless, and edges have rounded bevels. Inlays are minimal, slim abalone or ebony markers in an angular, elegant shape, with thin bindings and a dark inlaid ring. Terminations have a tapered, smooth curve with an engraved mark. The mood is precise, bright, contemporary and carefully finished.
In the style of Martin Guitars, classic large, rounded rectangular bodies, a warm golden spruce tone that ages to amber, and rosewood or mahogany sides. Bindings are cream or ivoroid, and dark herringbone purfling rings the top. Openings are circled with fine concentric rings, and protective plates are tortoiseshell. Dark ebony surfaces carry simple pearl dots, or snowflake inlays on premium versions. Ends are squared with an inlaid pearl logo. The finish is satin or gloss. The mood is traditional, folksy and rootsy.
In the style of Gretsch Guitars, large hollow bodies with a flamboyant orange or deep red-brown lacquer finish, gold-plated accents and bold decoration. Details include twin cutaways, gold sparkle or white binding, a G-shaped cutout and tall, wide proportions. Hardware is flashy, with chrome, gold and tall knobs. Gold-plated surrounds frame the details. Alternate finishes include pearl white, two-tone green and sparkle. The mood is rock and roll, showy, rockabilly and retro.
In the style of Rickenbacker, a distinctive thin body with glossy red, fireglo sunburst, jetglo black, mapleglo natural or white finish and white plastic binding. Features include slotted details, a sharply pointed horn on one side, stylish chrome covers, triangular inlays and a chrome toaster-shaped element with a white tapered contour. Some versions show a rounded cutaway with a curvy edge and tiny knobs. The mood is jangly, mod, 1960s British Invasion and psychedelic.
In the style of Bechstein, glossy ebony black with a classic Germanic design. Curved forms have a sharply defined edge, bold Jugendstil carved supports or plain tapering ones, and gold-leaf painted decoration on historic art pieces showing Neo-classical or Art Nouveau scenes in muted colours. Proportions are large and details are carved. Gold-toned lettering marks the front. Alternative woods include rosewood, walnut and burr maple with warm mellow tones. The mood is cultured, stately, ornate and drawing-room elegant.
In the style of Yamaha Pianos, a flawless gloss black polyester finish with smooth, crisp straight lines and uniform surfaces. Shapes have a simple curved edge, clean planes and square or rounded supports, with a small gold or silver emblem of three crossed tuning forks. Boxy shapes have neat panels and plain brass accents. Alternative finishes include satin ebony, polished mahogany, walnut and white. The mood is modern, tidy, dependable, schoolroom and conservatoire, with perfect reflections.
In the style of Petrof, solid, dignified, classical and carefully made. Deep, glossy black polished forms with smooth curved sides, plain tall planes, rounded cheeks and thick tapered legs finished in brass. Warm walnut, mahogany or white lacquer with matte or satin sheen are alternatives. Interior metalwork is gold bronze with angled strings and felt. The name appears in gold letters or on a small brass plate. Older models have simple candle brackets. Ivory-white with ebony black contrast, and brass accents.
