at Taputapuatea on the island of Raiatea in French Polynesia, the greatest marae of the Pacific, voyaging canoes, double-hulled canoes with tall triangular sails, rest in the lagoon, and navigators in their thirties to sixties in pareu, wrap cloths of red and white, and capes of brown bark cloth walk up onto the long platform of black volcanic slabs, in the gold light of dawn over the water
at Marae Arahurahu in a green valley of Tahiti, below steep cliffs of fern, priests in their forties to sixties in long white bark-cloth robes and tall headdresses of feathers and woven palm stand on the stepped stone platform, and dancers in their twenties in skirts of shredded green ti leaves kneel before them, in the warm light of afternoon
on Rarotonga in the Cook Islands, at a marae of upright basalt stones under huge old banyan trees, a high chief in his sixties, wrapped in bark cloth and crowned with a headdress of red feathers, is seated on a stone backrest as his new title is given, warriors in their twenties in leaf skirts holding carved spears around him, in green shade on a bright morning
on Nuku Hiva in the Marquesas Islands, at a meae, a terraced sacred ground of black stone among tall banyan trees with squat stone tiki, ancestor figures with huge round eyes, dancers in their twenties to forties tattooed from head to foot, in skirts of shredded bark, spread their arms in the bird dance, in the humid green light of afternoon
in New Zealand, before a carved red meeting house on a marae of cut grass, Maori women in their forties to seventies in black dresses with wreaths of green leaves on their heads lead a party of guests onto the lawn, and men in their twenties to sixties in skirts of black-and-gold flax strands stand waiting, in the gray light of a cool morning
at a marae of the war god Oro on Tahiti in the 1770s, priests in their forties to sixties in white bark cloth stand at the stone platform beside the body of a man in his thirties, killed beforehand by a club blow to the head as an offering, his body bound in a long wrapping of plaited coconut fronds and laid on the stones, in the flat white light of an overcast day
