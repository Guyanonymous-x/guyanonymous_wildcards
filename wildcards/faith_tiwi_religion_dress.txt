the ceremonial paint of the Tiwi, the Aboriginal people of Bathurst and Melville Islands off Darwin in northern Australia, adult dancers in their twenties to sixties painted in bold patterns of white, yellow, red and black ochre, dots, stripes, crosshatching and circles over the face and body, feathered armbands tied above the elbows
faces painted as masks with lines of dots across the brow and cheeks, chests painted in bars of yellow and red, armbands of woven pandanus with white cockatoo feathers
mourners at a pukumani ceremony, the funeral rite, in cloths at the hips with ochre body paint and feather armbands, the men bare above the waist, the women in skirts and tops
the older form, men and women painted all over, the men in a short cloth at the hips, armbands and headbands of feathers
