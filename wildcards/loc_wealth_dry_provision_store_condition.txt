the store of 1780, issuing against a book to a staff of thirty with everything bought in bulk twice a year at a fair or from a factor
the same room in 1880 at its fullest, with a printed stores book, standardized canisters bought as a set and a weekly grocer's order
the room in 1920 with the bulk buying gone, the hogsheads empty and a van delivering twice a week, which is the change that makes the room unnecessary
the room in 1950 as a store for garden chairs
the room as a shop stockroom in a house open to the public, which is a fairly exact continuation of function
the room in a divided house as a larder cupboard or a meter cupboard
the room mothballed, shelves swept, canisters stacked, cat flap screwed over
the room open to the weather, where the deal shelving is the first thing to go because it is the only timber in the cool range, and the stone floor and the limewash survive
