the tomb called the Treasury at Petra, a front as tall as a twelve-story building cut into a rose-red cliff at the end of a narrow canyon, six columns with leafy capitals below a gable, and above it a small round columned pavilion crowned by a stone urn between two half gables, worn figures of riders with horses, women with axes, winged women and eagles carved in its panels
the Urn Tomb at Petra, a tall plain front of four huge engaged columns under a gable crowned by an urn, cut high in a pink and cream cliff, a deep open court before it lined with a row of rock-cut columns and raised on two stories of arched stone vaults
the Palace Tomb at Petra, the widest front of all, cut into a cliff of banded red, pink and yellow rock, four gabled and arched doorways below and three stories of short close-set half columns stacked above them, the top story built up in masonry
the Obelisk Tomb at Petra, four tall tapering obelisks of yellow sandstone standing in a row above a plain doorway, a worn standing figure in a niche between the middle two, and directly below it a second carved front with a low gable and six half columns marking a banquet hall
the tomb called the Lonely Castle at Hegra, cut into one side of a single great rounded outcrop of golden sandstone standing alone on flat sand, four flat pillars with horned capitals, a plain doorway under a small gable and two giant staircases of five steps each carved facing one another across the top
a row of tombs at Hegra cut side by side along a golden sandstone outcrop, each a smooth flat front with a framed doorway, a band of moldings and a skyline of two rows of small stepped battlements, chisel lines slanting across every surface
