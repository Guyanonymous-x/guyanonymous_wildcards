Half past five, the first person down lights the grate, which is a housemaid aged fifteen and is the first of her twenty working hours
Seven, the servants' breakfast, fifteen minutes, standing room only at the lower end
Nine, the room empty and used as a passage
Eleven, beer and bread for those who can get down to it
One o'clock, the servants' dinner, which is the room's main event and the only time all twenty-four are in one place, taken in strict order with the upper servants leaving after the meat and the pudding eaten by the rest without them
The afternoon, two or three maids with sewing and a footman reading a paper, which is the nearest thing to leisure the house provides
Six, the servants' tea in shifts because dinner upstairs is being got
Nine, supper
Ten
Half past eleven, the fire raked out, the last candle taken
