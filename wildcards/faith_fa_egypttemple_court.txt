through the pylon doorway the open court, paved with stone and ringed by a covered walk of thick columns, an altar heaped with bread, geese, onions, lotus flowers and jars of beer in the middle, and townspeople in their twenties to sixties in white linen kneeling at its edge on a feast day
beyond the court the hall of columns, a forest of great stone shafts carved and painted from floor to roof with figures of the king and the gods, their tops shaped as open papyrus flowers, thin shafts of sunlight falling from stone grilles high above into the brown dusk between them
the great doors of the pylon standing half open, two leaves of cedar taller than four men sheathed in bronze and inlaid with gold figures, a doorkeeper in his forties with a staff beside them and behind them the bright court and the dark opening of the hall
beside the court the sacred lake, a rectangular pool with stone steps down each side where the priests wash at dawn, white geese on the water and a row of mud brick storehouses and bakeries along the precinct wall behind
