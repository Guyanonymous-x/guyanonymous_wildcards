men of the village in their twenties to forties climb long bamboo ladders lashed to the rock, and they haul a new wooden coffin up the cliff face on thick ropes toward an open chamber
two relatives in their forties and fifties stand on a narrow rock balcony beside the other figures, and they set a newly carved tau-tau of an adult man in its place, straightening his shirt and headcloth
at ma'nene, the rite of caring for the dead, relatives in their thirties to sixties open an old coffin at the foot of the cliff and lift out the dried body of an adult elder, its skin dark brown, hard and leathery over the bones, and they brush the dust away and dress it in a fresh shirt and sarong
relatives in their thirties to seventies climb a bamboo ladder to an old grave chamber, and they lay betel nut, cigarettes and small woven baskets of cooked rice on the ledge before its carved wooden door
