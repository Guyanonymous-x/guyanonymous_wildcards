a membership book on the end of the bar with a ballpoint on a string
signed by every guest a member brings, which is the single legal fiction the whole premises rests on and is therefore the most important object in the room
a clock set fifteen minutes fast, which is a real and widespread practice and exists so that last orders can be called honestly
a laminated notice of the rules and the licensed hours screwed to the wall, which is there for the inspector rather than the members
a framed license and a committee photograph beside it, which together say that this is a constituted club with officers and minutes and not simply a late bar
a bar shutter that comes down and padlocks, so that at the stated hour the room is open and the bar provably is not
a propped fire door with an ashtray stand outside it, which is where the room actually spends half its evening
a lock-up register with a key held by whoever is behind the bar
bottle crates stacked by the cellar hatch waiting to go down
a pool cloth worn through at the baulk end
cigarette smoke drifting back through the fire door, and bleach
