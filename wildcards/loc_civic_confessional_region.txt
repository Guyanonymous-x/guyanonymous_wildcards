the Roman Catholic confessional as the type, universal in Catholic churches worldwide since the Counter-Reformation and most numerous in Britain and Ireland in the Victorian and Edwardian churches of the industrial cities
the Irish version, built in greater numbers and in heavier joinery
the Italian, Spanish and Portuguese versions, which are frequently monumental, carved and integrated into the architecture rather than standing as furniture, and in the great basilicas run to a row of eight or ten with the language of the confessor painted above each
the Latin American versions, likewise
the Polish version, which remains in heavy use
the French version, often lighter and more open
the Anglican arrangement, where the practice is optional and the fitting is usually a pair of chairs in a side chapel or a curtained recess rather than a box, so the type barely exists
the Orthodox practice, which places priest and penitent side by side before an icon with no enclosure at all, which is a materially different arrangement and a different record; and the reconciliation room of 1975 onward, which is the type's living successor
