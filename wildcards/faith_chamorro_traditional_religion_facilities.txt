latte stone house foundations, the latte houses of the Chamorro, the Indigenous people of Guam and the Mariana Islands in the western Pacific, built from about a thousand years ago until the late 1600s, each house raised high above the ground on two facing rows of stone pillars, every pillar an upright squared shaft called a haligi crowned by a great half-round capstone called a tasa, set with its curved side down and its flat face up, the pairs of pillars carrying a long narrow house of timber and thatch with a steep roof, the house of the heads of a clan, who kept the skulls of their ancestors in baskets inside it and laid their dead to rest in the ground beside the pillars so that the ancestors stayed with the house, __faith_fa_lattehouse_scene__
