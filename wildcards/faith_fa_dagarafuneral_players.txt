two gyil set side by side on the earth before the stand, wide xylophones of fourteen flat reddish wooden bars tied over a low frame with a row of round gourds hanging under the bars, each played by a seated man in his forties with two rubber-tipped sticks
the lead xylophone player, Oscar, a broad-shouldered man in his fifties in a striped smock, bent low over the bars, his helper Leo, in his twenties, seated at the second xylophone and a third man tapping a rhythm on the end bar with a stick
a drummer in his thirties seated beside the xylophones with a kuor, a large round calabash with a skin stretched over its opening, held between his knees and played with the fingers of both hands
the xylophones played in turn by guests, Hugo, an old master in his seventies with a white beard, at one and Bruno, a thin man in his twenties, at the other, cowrie shells and coins lying scattered on the bars where mourners have thrown them
