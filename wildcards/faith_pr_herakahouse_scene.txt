and at sunrise they stand in rows on the open ground before the kelumki facing east, their palms pressed together at their chests, the pale gold first light of the sun rising over the forested ridges of the Barail hills onto their faces
and inside the kelumki, a long hall with walls of wooden planks and woven bamboo under a steep roof of corrugated tin, they sit on mats on the bare floor, men on one side and women on the other, facing a plain wooden altar at the east end hung with a framed photograph of Rani Gaidinliu, soft morning light falling through the small windows
and on a full moon night they gather in a hall of brick and plaster painted white with a small porch, on the very top of the hill above the village, the round moon bright over the dark hills and oil lamps lit along the altar inside
and at the new year feast they crowd into a kelumki with a thatched roof of grass on wooden posts, the altar decorated with green leaves and bunches of ripe rice stalks, warm midday light coming in through the open doorway
