Khasi women dancers in their twenties to their forties at the Shad Suk Mynsiem, in long dresses of yellow and gold silk pinned at the shoulders, a gold or silver crown around the head with a thin plume rising behind it, heavy necklaces of red coral and gold beads, their eyes cast down as they move slowly in a circle
Khasi men dancers in their twenties to their fifties in sleeveless jackets of colored silk, white dhotis wrapped around the legs and turbans of yellow silk with a plume, each holding a sword in the right hand and a white whisk of yak hair in the left
musicians in their forties to their sixties seated by the stand, drums of different sizes hung from their necks, one blowing a long thin pipe of wood with a flared end
spectators in their twenties to their sixties, Mina, a Garo woman in a wrapped skirt of patterned cloth, Sourav, a Bengali man in a white shirt, and Lina, a Khasi woman in a checked shawl holding up a phone
