In the style of Zsolnay of Pecs in Hungary, Art Nouveau iridescent ceramics. The signature is an eosin glaze, a shimmering metallic lustre which shifts between gold, green, magenta, purple, cobalt and copper-red as the light changes, like the surface of a beetle wing or an oil slick. Vases, bowls, jugs and figures have sinuous sculpted forms, swelling bellies, flared necks, whiplash curves and applied handles shaped like tendrils and lizards. Decoration is relief-modelled flowers, leaves, dragonflies, peacock feathers, seaweed, faces and Oriental motifs, with matte backgrounds and glossy iridescence on selected areas. Architectural pieces are frost-proof pyrogranite roof tiles and facade ornaments, in glazed warm colours of orange, green, turquoise, ochre and cobalt blue, often arranged in colourful tile roof patterns of scales and chevrons. The overall mood is lavish, jewel-like, organic and fin-de-siecle.
