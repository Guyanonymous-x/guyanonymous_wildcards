the old city of Quanzhou, red brick houses with swallowtail roof ridges, ends curving up into split points, low courtyard houses of warm orange-red brick with white stone bases and carved stone window grilles, their red tiled roofs sweeping up to sharp forked tips at each end
the twin stone pagodas of the Kaiyuan temple, two tall eight-sided towers of weathered gray granite, each with five stories of carved balconies and upturned stone eaves and a slim iron spire, rising above old banyan trees and red temple roofs
a harbor with fishing boats, a muddy brown river mouth crowded with wooden boats painted blue, green and red, with nets and floats heaped on their decks and red flags on short poles, moored side by side along a stone quay
the Quanzhou Maritime Museum, a large white modern building whose curved roofline swells upward in tall sweeping curves, built to recall the hull and sails of an old Chinese junk, a wooden sailing ship, with a broad paved plaza and palm trees in front
