the English Benedictine and Augustinian cloister as the type, with Gloucester, Canterbury, Norwich, Westminster and Salisbury the great survivals and several hundred ruined examples
the Cistercian cloister, plainer by rule, larger, and sited for water rather than for shelter, which changes the whole plan
the Carthusian cloister, which is an entirely different thing, a very large walk round a garth with individual cells and gardens opening off it, since the order's life is solitary, and that version deserves its own record
the Welsh and Scottish versions
the Irish version
the French cloître, more numerous and often with a carved program on the capitals that is the finest work in the building
the Italian chiostro, frequently arcaded on columns rather than traceried and in the Renaissance examples turned into a formal architectural set piece with a garden
the Spanish and Portuguese claustro, often two-storied and spectacularly carved
the German Kreuzgang
the cathedral close cloister, which is a secular canons' version with no monastic function and is what most English survivors actually are; and the collegiate and almshouse quadrangle, which is the cloister secularized and is the direct ancestor of every college quad and almshouse court in the country
