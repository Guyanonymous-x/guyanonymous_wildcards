at Romowe stands an ancient oak of vast girth, green in every season, and in three hollows of its trunk sit the images of the gods, Perkunas, a fiery-faced man in his forties with a curly black beard, Patrimpas, a smiling man in his twenties crowned with ears of grain, and Patollo, a pale old man in his seventies with a long gray beard
before the great oak burns the eternal fire of oak logs on a stone hearth, a curtain of cloth hung across the tree's front to hide the images, opened only for the high priest
beside the oak stand a pot holding a living snake for Patrimpas, sheaves of grain for him, and a horse's skull and a human skull hung high for Patollo, the god of the dead
