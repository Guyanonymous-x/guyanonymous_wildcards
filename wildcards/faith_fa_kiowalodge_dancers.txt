the dancers, men in their twenties to forties, their bodies painted white with clay, in long skirts of soft white buckskin to the ankles with a blue breechcloth hanging over the front, chests bare, wreaths of gray-green sage on their heads and at wrists and ankles, each with an eagle-bone whistle between his lips
the dancers standing in a row facing the Taime, men in their twenties to forties rising on their toes and sinking in place, arms stretched toward the image and eyes fixed on it, their white-painted faces hollow from days of fasting and thirst
the drummers at the south side of the door, men in their fifties and sixties seated around a large rawhide drum, in cloth shirts, leggings and blankets, their long hair in fur-wrapped braids, striking together with padded sticks, women in their forties in buckskin dresses seated behind them
the buffalo impersonators entering the lodge, men and women in their thirties bent under whole buffalo robes with the hair outside, driven in through the door by a rider and gathered into a heap before the center pole as a herd brought to the people
