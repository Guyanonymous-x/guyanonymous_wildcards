the abi, a deep round pit lined with stones, used to call up the spirits of the underworld, its walls dark and its floor deep, a shaft as wide as a small room sunk straight down into the mound, ringed with courses of rough gray limestone blocks that lean inward as they rise, sunlight catching only the upper stones while the lower rings fade into brown shadow, the bottom a floor of dark packed earth and ash
the stone rim of the pit, steps going down into the shaft, a ring of large pale gray blocks set level with the ground at the mouth of the pit, and from a gap in the ring a narrow flight of steep stone steps squeezed between two rough walls, descending out of the bright sun into the cool brown dimness of the round chamber below
offerings found in the pit, bones of piglets and puppies and pots, small pale bones and tiny jaws lying half bared in the dark ashy soil where the excavators have brushed them clean, beside them plain clay jars and little cups of buff and reddish pottery, some whole and some in curved broken pieces, and a few small figures of animals shaped in clay
