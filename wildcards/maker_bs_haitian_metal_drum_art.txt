In the style of the metalworkers of Croix-des-Bouquets in Haiti, flat decorative sculptures cut from recycled oil drums. Panels are made by flattening used steel drums, drawing a design with chalk and then chiselling and punching it with hammer and nail so the line work appears as a pattern of cut-through openings and raised ridges. Subjects are the tree of life, mermaids, birds, fish, sun faces, spirits with elaborate headdresses and crosses, drawn with symmetrical swirling leaves, scales, dotted outlines, hearts and curling vines. The metal is left in dark grey and rust-brown, sometimes polished to a rough silver with burn marks, or hand-painted in bright enamel colours like red, yellow and turquoise. Pieces are wall hangings in oval, round or irregular shapes with ragged cut edges, sometimes bent into three-dimensional animals and candle holders. The look is raw, intricate, spiritual and richly patterned, with a rugged industrial material turned into delicate lace.
