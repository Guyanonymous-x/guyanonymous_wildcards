Viktor, a man in his forties, seated on the lowest step drawing the point of a black obsidian blade across his earlobe and letting the drops of blood fall onto strips of white bark paper in a clay bowl, which a second man, Rami, in his thirties carries up to the shrine
women in their thirties to sixties climbing the stair one behind another with offerings, flat clay dishes of corn cakes, a jar of foaming white agave drink, bunches of yellow marigolds and a painted clay figurine held in both hands
a priest in his thirties holding a quail by the wings over the altar and cutting off its head with one stroke of an obsidian knife, then sprinkling its blood toward the four sides of the court
the bloodied tips of dark agave thorns, each as long as a finger, pushed one by one into a round ball of plaited grass laid on the altar by a man in his forties, a drop of blood still on his pricked earlobe
