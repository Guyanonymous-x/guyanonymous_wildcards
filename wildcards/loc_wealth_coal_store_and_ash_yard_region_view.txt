the English service court with coal plates and vaulted bays as the type, which is a nineteenth-century arrangement made possible by cheap railed coal
the Scottish version, identical and with a larger annual tonnage
the Irish version, with turf as well as or instead of coal, which changes the whole storage problem because turf must be kept dry and takes four times the volume for the same heat
the Welsh version, closest to the pits and best supplied
the French and German equivalents, with wood doing more of the work for longer and a wood yard rather than a coal bay
the Scandinavian and Russian versions, which store a winter's firewood in stacked cords and need a yard of 200-600 square meters rather than four vaults
the Alpine version, with the woodstack as part of the building's elevation
the American version, with a coal cellar, a chute and a furnace from 1870, which is the arrangement that produced central heating; and the colonial household, where charcoal and wood are bought daily in small quantities and no store of this kind exists
