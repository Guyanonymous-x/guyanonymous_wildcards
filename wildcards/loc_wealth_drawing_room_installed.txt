with a dado below; a plaster cornice of middling enrichment and no coffering; a chimneypiece on the inner wall with a register grate for coal rather than a basket for logs, which is the single most important difference from the saloon because it means the room can be warm in forty minutes instead of two hours; windows to the floor with inner muslins as well as curtains, so that the light can be softened rather than only admitted or excluded; shutters; a floor of oak board under a fitted carpet, which is the modern arrangement and is what distinguishes this room from the single-carpet state rooms; two bell pulls; a door to the saloon that is part of the enfilade and is kept closed in winter against the draft, which breaks the enfilade and is the standing argument of the house; and no concealed service door, because this room is entered by the family and by a maid at the ordinary door
