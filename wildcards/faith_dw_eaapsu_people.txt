the apkallu, the seven sages of Ea, men in their forties with long curled beards, wear cloaks made of whole fish skins, the fish heads over their own heads and the tails falling down their backs, each holding a pine cone and a small bucket
priests in their thirties to their sixties with shaved heads, in white linen kilts, bare-chested, pour water from tall jars into the temple apsu at Eridu, a broad stone tank of holy water in the temple court
beside the reed marsh at Eridu stands the temple of Ea on its raised platform of mudbrick, a stair climbing to the shrine at its top, and fishermen in their twenties to their fifties in reed boats bring fish as offerings
