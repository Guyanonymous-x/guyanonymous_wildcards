In the style of San Ildefonso Pueblo black-on-black pottery from New Mexico. Hand-coiled, perfectly symmetrical jars, bowls and plates with a rounded, full body, a marked shoulder angle, a short narrow neck and a slightly flattened rim. The whole surface is burnished to a glass-smooth gloss and fired in smothering dung smoke to a deep jet black. Decoration is made by painting designs in a clay slip which stays matte after polishing, so the pattern shows as flat dull black against a shining black field, visible only as light shifts across it. The designs are a wide band around the shoulder with a coiled avanyu water serpent with horns, feathers, rain clouds, kiva steps, lightning zigzags, mountains and bird tracks. Some pieces are carved or etched through the surface, others are red or sienna with matte-and-polish contrasts. The look is sleek, dark, formal and sculptural, timeless, with subtle reflective effect and perfect balance.
