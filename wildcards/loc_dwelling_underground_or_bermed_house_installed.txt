a dwelling that uses the ground as its insulation and its envelope
built in numbers during and after the 1970s energy crises and periodically revived, a structure strong enough to carry a meter of saturated earth, which means reinforced concrete or heavy masonry and a real engineer
a continuous waterproof membrane and a protection and drainage layer that are the single point of failure for the whole building
land drains and usually a sump and pump
earth bermed against three sides and over the roof with a soil depth chosen for planting and insulation
one facade fully glazed and oriented to the sun, which forces a linear plan one room deep because there is only one source of light
light wells
sun pipes or clerestories to reach anything behind
mechanical ventilation with heat recovery because natural cross-ventilation is impossible
an interior temperature that barely moves through the year, and a resale and mortgage conversation that is always harder than the owner expected
