across the water the gubida come back from Seiri in long dugout canoes cut from single tree trunks, paddled by men in their forties to their seventies in white shirts, the canoes heading in toward the beach
fishermen in their thirties to their sixties in dugout canoes with patched sails bring back fish from the cays for the dugu feast, and beside them, painted pale and shining on the water, the ancestors ride in with them
the soul of a woman in her seventies, newly dead, in a white dress and a white head wrap, walks out along a path of light over the calm sea toward Seiri, looking back once at the village on the shore
