Half past five, the first climb of the day, and it is a housemaid carrying a box and a candle
Six to eight, the stair's busiest hour of the whole day, four or five women climbing and descending continuously with boxes, pails, cans and coal
Eight, the boots going up and the slops coming down, passing each other on a half-landing
Mid-morning
One, the servants' dinner and the stair empty
Half past five, the dressing hour and eleven cans of hot water going up
Half past seven, the dinner service, which is the one time of day the stair is used by men in livery rather than women in aprons and is also the one time it is used at a run
Eleven, the family going up the principal stair and the staff going up this one at the same moment on either side of one wall, which is the clearest single image of the whole arrangement
Midnight, the last climb, to the attic, by candle; and every day of the year the same
