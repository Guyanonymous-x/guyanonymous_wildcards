In the style of Aldine Press, small portable books with a clean, balanced page, set in a crisp roman type with open, round letters and a slanted, narrow italic with joined strokes. Pages are uncluttered, with generous margins, justified text, simple initial capitals and almost no illustration. Title pages carry a small printer mark of a dolphin coiled around an anchor in black ink on cream paper. Bindings are soft vellum or brown leather with blind tooling, gilt edges and ties. The look is elegant, humanist, scholarly and compact, with an even, quiet, evenly inked colour on laid paper.
In the style of Plantin Press, dense, sturdy books of the Renaissance with well-spaced roman typefaces, italic accents and elaborate woodcut or copper-engraved title pages. Title pages are architectural, with columns, pediments, allegorical figures, garlands, cartouches and a printer emblem of a hand holding a compass. Pages show black ink on warm cream laid paper, with decorative headpieces, ornamented initials and neat marginal notes. Multi-language Bible volumes use columns in several scripts, symmetrical and orderly. Bindings are in tooled calf or vellum. The look is rich, rational, ornate and humanist.
In the style of Kelmscott Press, dense Gothic-revival books printed in heavy black blackletter type on thick handmade cream paper, with red ink for headings and marginal notes. Each page is framed by luxuriant woodcut borders of winding acanthus, vines, flowers, leaves and fruit in white on black, interlaced thickly. Large decorative initials in foliage set against dark ground open each chapter. Text blocks are tightly justified, with minimal leading and wide margins, giving a dark and ornate texture. Bindings use limp vellum with silk ties or half holland with boards. The mood is medieval, rich and romantic.
In the style of Doves Press, austere and elegant books with no illustration and no border. Pages are printed in a single classic roman type in rich black ink on thick cream handmade paper, with even word spacing, generous margins and a perfectly balanced column. Only coloured initials in red or blue, drawn with flowing calligraphic flourishes, add ornament, and chapter openings use large letters with simple lines. Title pages are plain with tidy letter spacing. Bindings are tooled with a simple gilt line pattern on limp vellum or morocco. The mood is pure, calm, disciplined and typographic.
In the style of Elzevir Press, tiny pocket-sized duodecimo books with neat, crisp roman and italic text set in narrow columns on small pages. The title page has a copper-engraved frame or a small printer device of a tree wound with a vine and an old man beside it. Typography is compact and clear, with compact page numbers, ornamental headpieces of printers flowers and small decorated initials. Paper is white to cream, and bindings are plain vellum or brown calf with gilt tooling and small marbled endpapers. The mood is compact, practical, tidy and scholarly.
In the style of Penguin Books paperbacks, a rigorous three-band cover layout with a central white panel framed by two coloured stripes, orange for fiction, green for crime, blue for non-fiction, with the title and author set in a clean sans-serif or classic serif inside the white band, a small penguin logo at the bottom, later swiss-style covers with bold illustration and grid, mid-century uncoated paper with matte printing and crisp rules.
In the style of Taschen art books, oversized heavy hardcover coffee-table volumes with glossy photo or painting covers bleeding edge to edge, a plain contrasting colour bar or foil-stamped title, rich saturated reproduction, large crisp images on thick paper, stylish clean sans-serif titles, hefty slipcase boxes with a satin finish and a bold, sensual, curious art and pop-culture look.
