six in the morning, the building unlocked, the sanctum curtain drawn back, the image woken, bathed in water, milk, honey and sandalwood with the used liquid running out through the channel, dried, dressed in the day's silks, garlanded with fresh flowers and offered the first lamps
half past seven, the first service of six
mid-morning, a steady few
midday, an offering of food
the early afternoon, the curtain drawn and the rest period
four, the curtain back
the evening service at about seven, the best attended of the weekdays, bell, conch, drum and cymbals and the recitation on the loudspeakers
half past eight, the last lamps, the image undressed of its heavy ornament, offered rest
a festival, when the building holds ten times its weekday number, the silks and the silver come out of the safe
a Sunday, the busiest ordinary day in Britain
