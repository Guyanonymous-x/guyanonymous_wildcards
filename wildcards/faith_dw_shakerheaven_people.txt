in the meetinghouse the Shakers worship in a dance, the sisters on one side and the brothers on the other moving in straight rows, women in their twenties to their sixties in long plain dresses, white caps and white kerchiefs, men in their twenties to their sixties in dark vests and white shirts
Polly, a sister in her fifties in a white cap and long gray-blue dress, sits at a table drawing the gift she received, a sheet of paper before her, while a soft white spirit figure of a woman in her forties stands beside her
the spirits of the dead walk among the living Shakers in the orchard, pale shining figures of men and women in their thirties to their seventies in white robes, the living brothers in broad-brimmed straw hats picking apples beside them
Mother Ann Lee, the founder, appears in the sky above the village as the Shakers told of her in their visions, a woman in her forties in a plain white cap and a long dark dress, light around her, her hands raised over the people below
