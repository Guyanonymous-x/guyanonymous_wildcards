at a famadihana, the turning of the bones, the dead are carried out of the tomb as long bundles wrapped in red-brown silk lamba mena shrouds, held up on the shoulders of their kin
a dead body newly unwrapped lies on a mat by the tomb, dry and dark, the bones showing through the old cloth, as women in their thirties to sixties wrap it in a fresh white silk shroud
an old woman ancestor in her eighties appears in a dream above the tomb, a thin woman in a white lamba with gray hair pulled back, her hand raised in blessing over the house
