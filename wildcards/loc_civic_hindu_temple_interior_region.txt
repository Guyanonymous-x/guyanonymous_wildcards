the South Indian Dravidian temple, with a towered gateway on the enclosure wall taller than the sanctum's own tower, concentric enclosures and a tank, which is a complete temple town rather than a building and deserves its own records
the North Indian Nagara temple, with a curved spire over the sanctum and a sequence of halls on one axis, which is the plan most British purpose-built temples follow
the Gujarati and Rajasthani traditions, with intricately carved white marble and the highest level of stone craft now practiced anywhere, and which supply much of the carved work for British temples
the Bengali, Odishan and Keralan regional forms, each with distinct roof and tower logic
the Nepali pagoda-roofed temple
the Balinese open courtyard temple, which has no enclosed sanctum hall at all
the Tamil diaspora temple in Britain, Malaysia, Singapore and South Africa
the domestic shrine, a cabinet or a dedicated alcove in a house serving one family with the same daily routine at a tenth of the scale, which is far more numerous than every temple put together and is a separate record; and the converted British building of 1965-1990, which is a distinct type in its own right and deserves recording as one rather than as a deficient version of something else
