the women of each family, Wendat women in their thirties to sixties, kneeling by an opened bark coffin and scraping the last dried flesh and skin from the bones of their dead with small knives and shells, the scraps heaped on a mat to be burned
the bones cleaned and bare, skulls, long leg and arm bones and ribs laid in neat bundles on new beaver robes, each bundle rolled up and tied with cord and hung with strings of white and purple shell beads
the bodies of the recently dead still whole, wrapped in old furs gone dark and stiff and laid out on new mats, the dried brown skin drawn tight over the faces, their kin in their twenties to sixties crouched beside them
