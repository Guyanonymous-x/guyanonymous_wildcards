In the style of Los Castillo, mid-century Mexican modernist silver and silverplate from a Taxco workshop. Heavy hand-hammered hollowware such as pitchers, creamers, sugar bowls, trays and lidded bowls, with sculptural animal forms like long-necked birds, seahorses, pelicans and fish whose bodies swell into smooth rounded volumes. Surfaces are bright polished metal with a warm slightly rippled hammered texture, set off by chunky cabochon inlays of deep green banded malachite, blue lapis and azurite pressed flush into the metal. Jewellery uses chunky link bracelets, cuffs and brooches in sterling silver married with copper and brass in interlocking geometric panels, stepped-fret and feathered-serpent motifs abstracted into bold simple shapes with strong negative space. Handles and feet are cast as stylised beaks, tails and fins. The mood is festive, organic and luxurious, like a 1960s cocktail party table, with a slightly imperfect handmade finish and visible tool marks rather than machined perfection.
