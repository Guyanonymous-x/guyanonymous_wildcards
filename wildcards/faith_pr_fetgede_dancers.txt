the possessed dancers men and women in their twenties to their fifties, dark-skinned and sweating, in black and purple clothes, the men in battered black top hats and black tailcoats, the women in purple dresses with black lace veils, all in dark sunglasses with one lens pushed out
one dancer a lean man in his forties ridden by a Gede, his face powdered chalk white, small plugs of white cotton pushed into his nostrils as a corpse is laid out, in a black top hat, a purple shirt and dark glasses
the dancers women in their thirties to their sixties in long purple and white skirts and black blouses, white headscarves knotted at the brow, faces dusted with white powder, each waving a purple silk scarf above her head
an oungan, a Vodou priest in his fifties with a gray goatee, in a white shirt, a purple sash across his chest and a black derby hat, leads the dancers, and he holds high an asson, a dried gourd rattle covered in a net of beads
