The mood is revolutionary, industrial, energetic and abstract.
In the style of De Stijl Workshops, everything built from straight horizontal and vertical lines and rectangles. The palette is limited to pure red, yellow and blue, with white, black and grey, set in flat colour blocks with thick black dividing lines. Objects are assembled from slim rails and flat panels that overlap and project at their ends, painted in contrasting colours with exposed ends. Spaces show asymmetrical balance and open planes. Lettering is blocky and constructed from squares. The mood is strict, rational, abstract and playful in its colour.
In the style of Omega Workshops, hand-painted surfaces with bold, loose, post-impressionist decoration. Brush marks are visible, with abstract blobs, stripes, spirals and stylised flowers or figures in strong colours, mustard, magenta, turquoise, orange, lilac, green and black. Compositions are irregular and asymmetrical with an unfinished hand-drawn quality. Underlying forms are plain and painted all over in dense patterns. Glazes are thick and opaque, with spots and swirls. The mood is cheerful, avant-garde, amateurish by design and bohemian.
In the style of Guild of Handicraft, Arts and Crafts silver, enamel and jewellery in a hand-wrought manner. Hammered metal shows visible tool marks, curved lines, wire twists and loose, flowing Art Nouveau lines. Peacocks, ships, tendrils, stylised flowers and hearts are set with cabochon moonstones, opals, turquoise or blister pearls and translucent coloured enamel in green, blue and rose. Objects look soft and slightly irregular, with a matte, pale silver surface and delicate suspended drops. The mood is romantic, medieval-influenced, simple and craftsmanlike.
In the style of Shaker Mount Lebanon, plain and sturdy construction in pine, maple and cherry with no ornament. Slender turned posts, arching slats, woven tape or rush texture and tapered legs give light, upright forms. Flat panels, simple pegs, finger joints, small wooden knobs and a smooth surface define the details. Colours are muted milk-paint tones, barn red, mustard yellow, olive green, blue and natural honey wood. Thin bent wood bands with swallowtail joints and copper tacks hold shapes together. The mood is serene, orderly, utilitarian and pure.
In the style of Amish Quilt Workshops, large simple geometric blocks of solid-colour wool or cotton in deep, saturated tones. The palette is black, plum purple, royal blue, forest green, wine red, brown and dusty rose, with no printed patterns. Designs use a centre diamond, bars, stepped squares and nine-patch grids, framed by wide borders and bound in contrasting colour. Fine, dense stitching creates feathers, wreaths, cables and grids in the dark areas, giving a subtle relief. The mood is bold, austere, modern-looking and graphic.
In the style of Gzhel Porcelain, bright glossy white ground painted only in cobalt blue brushwork, in graduated tones from pale sky blue to deep navy. Forms are rounded and plump, with soft outlines. The blue appears washed and flowing against the clean white. The mood is folk-like, cheerful, fresh and quintessentially Russian.
In the style of Lomonosov Imperial Porcelain Factory, bright white porcelain in two manners. The imperial manner shows gilded edges, cobalt blue nets of dark blue lattice with gold dots and rich floral reserves in naturalistic colour, on fine, thin, delicate forms. The revolutionary manner of the 1920s shows suprematist and propaganda designs with sharp red, black and white geometry, stars, workers and slogans in stencilled lettering, filling flat surfaces with boldly angular designs. Surfaces are lively and glossy.
In the style of Palekh Lacquer Miniatures, deep lustrous black glossy ground painted in egg tempera with fairy tale scenes. Elongated, elegant figures with small heads and graceful curved poses move through stylised hills, towers, trees and onion domes. The palette is bright red, gold, emerald green, white and orange against black, with fine gold highlights outlining draperies and ornamental borders of tiny patterns. Red lacquer provides contrast. Details are miniature and precise, as in an icon, and the mood is mythical, jewel-like and storybook.
In the style of Talavera Uriarte, thick glossy milk-white tin glaze over rounded, weighty, hand-made forms, painted freely by hand in cobalt blue with accents of yellow, green, orange, black and sometimes turquoise. Decoration is loose and generous, with flowers, vines, leaves, birds, rosettes and geometric borders in slightly raised shiny paint. Pinholes, pooled colour and small unevenness in the surface are kept as part of the charm. Repeated panels join into large blue and white patterns. Rustic, festive, colonial and clearly handmade.
In the style of Hereke Imperial Carpet Factory, exceptionally fine surfaces with very dense, minute detail and a glossy silk sheen. Composition is courtly and symmetrical, built around a central medallion with pointed arch shapes, tulips, carnations, curling serrated leaves and rosettes, plus touches of gold and silver shimmer. Colours are ivory, turquoise, rose, deep sapphire, emerald and ruby. Borders are narrow, multiple and perfectly even, with soft elegant curves. Imperial, lustrous, exquisite and delicate.
In the style of Batik Danar Hadi, hand-drawn wax-resist patterning in an earthy palette of soga brown, indigo blue, cream and black, with occasional red and gold. Motifs are classical and rhythmic, such as diagonal bands of interlocking S shapes, circles in a quatrefoil grid, scattered tiny flowers and winged cartouches, repeated across the whole surface. Fine dotted lines and slightly wavering white outlines show the hand of the maker, and thin crackle veins and tonal depth appear where the wax cracked. Courtly, refined and heritage-rich.
In the style of Bat Trang Ceramic Kilns, soft crackled ivory and cream-white glaze with freely brushed cobalt blue painting, and sometimes brown, green and iron-brown tones. Decoration is flowing and relaxed, with lotus, chrysanthemum, bamboo, birds, dragons, wave scrolls and key-fret borders, either brushed in blue or incised into the material. Forms have thick walls, swelling shoulders and a slightly uneven hand-thrown quality. Gentle, rustic, subtle and unpretentious.
In the style of New Zealand Maori Arts and Crafts Institute, dense ancestral carving where every surface is covered in deep curved relief. Spirals, interlocked fern-frond curves and notched ridges flow continuously over bold stylised figures with large heads, wide inlaid shell eyes and protruding tongues. Dark polished brown wood is accented with red ochre, white and black, and woven lattice in diagonal patterns fills the spaces between carved elements. Powerful, sculptural, rhythmic and full of flowing curves.
