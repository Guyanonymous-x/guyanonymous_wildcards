a table everybody can reach
writing surfaces on more than one wall, and no front; an oval or rectangular table arrangement with everyone able to see everyone, which is the room's only real requirement and is why the table is pushed together from four rather than being one fixed piece; whiteboards on two adjacent walls so that the group can turn and work on a wall, which is the feature that distinguishes a good seminar room from a small classroom; a short-throw projector on a wall bracket rather than a ceiling mount, so nobody casts a shadow; chairs on castors, which is a small thing that transforms a two-hour discussion; a window with a blind, because the room is occupied for two hours at a stretch and a view matters more here than in a lecture theater; a door with a vision panel and a bookable room system outside it; acoustic treatment that is usually absent and should not be
