In the style of Guna women of the San Blas islands of Panama, reverse-appliqued mola panels. Each mola is a rectangular blouse panel made of two to seven layers of thin cotton cloth in contrasting solid colours, the top layer cut away in curving shapes so the layers beneath peek through, with edges turned under and hemmed with tiny nearly invisible stitches. The design fills the whole panel with an energetic horror vacui of tightly packed shapes in parallel concentric outlines, like a thick-lined labyrinth, depicting birds, fish, sea turtles, parrots, flowers, masks, abstract geometry and, in modern ones, cartoon characters or everyday objects. Colours are bright and fully saturated and placed in bold contrast, red against orange, black against yellow, green against hot pink, blue against white. Extra details include small slits, dots, zigzag borders, lattice patterns and embroidered eyes. The look is graphic, rhythmic, vibrant and strongly handmade, flat and symmetrical like stained glass.
