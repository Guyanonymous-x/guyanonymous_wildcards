Seven, the turret bell at record 46 across the park, and eight men in the yard
Half past seven, the smithy's fire blown up, which takes twenty minutes and is the first fire lit here
The morning, the yard's real work, shoeing in the smithy with a horse brought over from record 46, a gate being framed in the carpenter's shop, a cartwheel being tired outside on the tyring platform with the whole yard watching because a tire has to be shrunk on hot and water thrown and it is the one piece of theater in the working week, logs on the deck and the saw running
Midday, dinner, the men eating in the shops
The afternoon, the same
Three, a cart weighed on the weighbridge and chalked
Five, the belts thrown off, the fire banked, the tools put up, the shops swept, which is a rule
Six, the bell
The yard empty
A wet week in February, when the yard is the busiest place on the estate because nothing can be done outdoors and everything gets mended
A hay or harvest week in July, when the yard is empty because every man is in a field
