Borobudur in Java, a vast temple mountain of gray volcanic stone built as nine stacked terraces, the lower ones square and lined with carved stone panels of the Buddha's life, the upper ones round and crowned by rows of bell-shaped stupas of stone latticework, each holding a seated Buddha, around one great central stupa
the plain of Bagan in Myanmar, thousands of red brick temples and pagodas, some bell-shaped and some stepped pyramids with spires, scattered across a dusty plain of palms and fields beside the Irrawaddy river
the Shwedagon Pagoda in Yangon, a great bell-shaped stupa covered in gold plate, its spire topped by a crown studded with diamonds and rubies, rising from a marble terrace crowded with smaller gilded shrines
the Temple of the Tooth in Kandy, Sri Lanka, a white temple with a red tile roof and a gold-roofed shrine within, beside a lake, holding a tooth of the Buddha in a golden casket in the form of a little stupa, a bell-shaped dome topped by a spire
the Potala Palace in Lhasa, Tibet, a huge white and dark red fortress-palace climbing a rocky hill in thirteen stories, with gold roofs on top, and nearby the Jokhang temple, its gold roofs and golden wheel between two deer
the old temples of Nara and Kyoto in Japan, great wooden halls with sweeping tiled roofs, the Todai-ji hall with its giant bronze Buddha, and five-story wooden pagodas among maples and moss gardens
