the entrance, a row of eight tall square pillars of pale limestone standing side by side, each cut from one stone and as high as five men, plain and sharp-edged, with a square notch at the top to hold the roof beams
the pillared hall behind the entrance, a court ringed by thirty-two square pillars carrying a flat roof of stone slabs over the walkway, the middle of the court open to the sky
the inner walls of the hall set with rows of false windows, tall narrow panels of stone carved as wooden lattices, and a frieze of ibex heads with ridged curving horns running above them
one pillar seen close, its face cut from top to bottom with neat rows of straight-lined angular marks, the South Arabian script, each mark as tall as a finger and filled with red paint
