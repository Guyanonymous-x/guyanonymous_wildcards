the dancers drink from bottles of piman, raw white rum steeped with a mass of red and green hot chili peppers, and rub it over their faces, eyes and lips and between their legs, a test that the spirit has truly come
the dancers do the banda, the bawdy dance of the Gede, rolling and thrusting their hips in quick jerks with hands on their knees, grinning and miming lewd jokes at the crowd
a priestess in her fifties in a purple dress holds a black rooster up by its legs over the candles at the foot of the cross, then cuts its throat with a short knife and lets the blood drip onto the stepped base
devotees kneel at the base of the cross, light new black and purple candles from the old ones and press them upright into the soft heap of melted wax, smoke curling up into the hot air
