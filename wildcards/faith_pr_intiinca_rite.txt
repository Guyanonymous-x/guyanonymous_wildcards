a black llama is laid on its side facing the east, its throat cut with a knife of bronze by a priest in his fifties, and the blood caught in a gold bowl is daubed on the faces of the nobles
the high priest kindles the new fire of the year by catching the sunlight in a polished concave mirror of gold and focusing it onto a tuft of cotton, and the flame is carried to the Temple of the Sun
rows of priests in their thirties to sixties throw handfuls of coca leaves and corn into a great fire, and chicha is poured from gold jars into a stone basin that runs to the Temple of the Sun
the chosen women, the aqllakuna, women in their twenties to forties in white dresses and shawls pinned with silver pins, bring out bread of corn and jars of chicha for the feast
