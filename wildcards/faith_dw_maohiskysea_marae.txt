on the shore stands the marae of Taputapuatea on Raiatea, a wide stone platform of black volcanic rock and pale coral slabs, upright stones standing in rows and a carved wooden altar heaped with fruit and fish
on the marae stands a tall wooden scaffold for offerings beside a raised stone step platform, and on it the to'o of Oro, a wooden figure wrapped in tightly braided brown coconut fiber and bound with bright red feathers
by a stone marae under great banyan trees, a priest in his sixties lays bananas, coconuts, pigs and fish on a high wooden altar, upright stones standing behind him for the gods
