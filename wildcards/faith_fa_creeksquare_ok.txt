women in their twenties to sixties dancing the Ribbon Dance in single file around the fire, in long full cotton dresses of red, turquoise and yellow, a fan of long bright ribbons hanging from a comb at the back of each head down to the hem, turtle-shell rattles strapped around their lower legs
men in their twenties to seventies dancing the Feather Dance, in jeans, ribbon shirts and straw hats, each carrying a long cane pole with white egret feathers tied along its upper end, moving in a column from arbor to arbor
the medicine man, a man in his sixties with brown skin and a gray braid, kneeling over a washtub of dark herb brew and blowing into it through a long cane tube so that it foams, as men in their twenties to sixties line up with tin cups to drink, then walk to the edge of the ground and vomit it up
the scratching, a man in his fifties drawing a small comb set with four steel needles down the upper arms and calves of men in their twenties to forties who stand in a row with sleeves and trouser legs rolled up, leaving thin parallel red lines beaded with blood
a firekeeper in his sixties in a dark vest and a black hat with a white feather, kneeling at the center of the square to push the four logs closer together, an ear of new green corn laid at the outer tip of each, as men in their thirties to seventies in ribbon shirts watch from the benches
