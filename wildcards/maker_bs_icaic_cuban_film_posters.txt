In the style of the Cuban film institute poster studio, hand-silkscreened posters from the 1960s and 1970s. Each poster is printed with a few flat, saturated colours in layers, with no gradients, bright red, yellow, cobalt blue, orange, green, pink and black, laid down one screen at a time on cheap rough paper with slightly uneven edges and visible misregistration. Imagery is bold, simplified and symbolic, such as a giant eye, a cut-out head, a hand holding a camera, a skeleton, a face split into colour planes, a single iconic object enlarged to fill the sheet, often with playful surreal collage-like juxtapositions and sharp graphic cut-out edges. Lettering is hand-drawn, chunky, irregular, sometimes stencil-like or distorted to match the theme, and sits in blocks of solid colour. The look blends pop art, psychedelia, Constructivist geometry and Latin satire, vibrant, witty, politically charged and strongly handmade.
