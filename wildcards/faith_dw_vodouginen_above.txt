above the water stands a Vodou peristyle, an open-sided temple with a tin roof on painted posts, its poto mitan painted in bright stripes, worshippers in white clothes dancing around it
on the riverbank a mambo in her fifties in a white dress and headwrap holds a rattle, calling a soul up out of the water into a clay jar, the govi, drummers in their thirties beside her
in the temple yard a houngan in his forties in white cuts the throat of a white chicken and lets its blood drip onto a vèvè, a sacred design drawn in white cornmeal on the dirt floor
