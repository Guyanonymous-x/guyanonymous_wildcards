a structure popularized by Buckminster Fuller and taken up enthusiastically by owner-builders from the late 1960s
in which a sphere is approximated by triangles so that the shell carries itself without internal structure, struts and panel edges meeting at hubs that are the critical and most-engineered component
a shell of plywood
timber
steel or concrete

a volume that is very efficient in material and surface area and very inefficient in usable floor area because the curve meets the floor at the edges
a mezzanine to recover some of the volume
no internal load-bearing walls so the plan is free and also hard to subdivide
superb strength against wind and snow
severe and well-documented difficulty in weatherproofing the joints
heat that stratifies to the apex and needs a fan or a vent, and a resale market that is thin because most buyers want rectangles
