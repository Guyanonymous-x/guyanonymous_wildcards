the arrangement of 1800 with a green, a line, a prop and nothing else, which is the normal condition before drying closets and is why the four-times-a-year wash made sense
the same department in 1870 with a drying closet installed, which is a dated and traceable improvement in many houses and transforms the laundry's reliability
the department in 1900 with a steam-heated closet, a rotary ironer and a gas iron, which is the peak
the department in 1930 with an electric iron and the closet's pipes drained
the department in 1960 with a tumble drier where the closet's horses were, which is the same idea in a box
the green surviving as a lawn with four post sockets in it and nobody knowing why they are there, which is extremely common
the closet as a boiler room, which is the commonest reuse because the pipes and the flue are there
the ironing room as a flat's bedroom
the department mothballed, pipes drained, horses racked, lines down, posts left
the department open to the weather, where the closet's brick chamber and its four ranks of pipe survive and are frequently mistaken for something else
