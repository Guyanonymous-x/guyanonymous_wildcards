and in the paved courtyard of Sheikh Adi's shrine at night, the sheikhs and pirs in white walk slowly in a circle around a tall flame on an iron stand, their arms raised and lowered in turn, the qewals seated beside them with their drums, the white cones of the shrines faintly lit against the dark sky
and in front of the shrine of Sheikh Shams in clear gold light of an October morning, men in their twenties to forties throw a brown bull to the ground by its horns, and a sheikh in his fifties cuts its throat with a long knife, the blood running down a channel cut in the stone floor
and inside the dim stone hall of Sheikh Adi's shrine, lit only by the daylight from the doorway, a woman in her thirties ties a knot in one of the many silk cloths of red, green and yellow hung from a pillar, then kisses the cloth
and in the crowded lower courtyard under hard white noon sun and a deep blue sky, pilgrims in their twenties to sixties stoop to kiss the carved stone door frame of the shrine, beside a carved black snake on the wall, stepping high over the sill so as to keep their bare feet off it
and in warm orange light of a fall sunset in the narrow valley of olive and mulberry trees, men in their thirties to fifties set small cotton wicks in olive oil burning in the niches of the shrine walls, rows of tiny yellow flames climbing up the stone
