the rainbow bridge springs from a high mountain ridge and arches over a deep gorge with a rushing white river far below, its bands of red, orange, yellow, green, blue and violet broad and solid enough to walk on
at the near end of the rainbow waits a guardian spirit, an old man in his seventies in a red-striped woven robe, who looks at the faces of the dead for their tattoos and at their hands for the marks of hunting and weaving, to judge who may cross
the rainbow rises from mist on a ridge of tall cypress and bamboo, and one soul who failed the Gaga falls from its edge into the gorge, where large dark crabs crowd the rocks at the water
at the near end of the rainbow crouches Utux Kalan, the crab spirit who guards the bridge, a dark red-brown crab as big as a crouching man with two huge raised claws, its stalked eyes turned on each soul that comes, letting the tattooed pass
the rainbow shakes hard under one soul who failed the Gaga, a man in his thirties with a bare untattooed face, and throws him from its edge toward the gorge, where hungry snakes and wild beasts wait on the rocks by the water
