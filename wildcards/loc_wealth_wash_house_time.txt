Half past four on a Monday, the laundry maid down before anyone in the house to light the copper, which is two hours to boiling
Six, the first tub, and the sequence is sorting, steeping, soaping, dollying, boiling, rinsing three times, bluing, starching, wringing, mangling, and only then drying, which for 340 pieces is two full days of two people's labor
Eight, the room saturated
Ten, the first load out to the drying green at record 34 if it is fine and onto the pulley rack if it is not, and the whole week's plan turns on that decision made at ten o'clock
One, dinner eaten standing
The afternoon, the mangle, which is the job that breaks people's backs and which no account of these houses mentions
Six, the floor washed, the gullies flushed, the copper's fire raked
Tuesday, the same again
Wednesday and Thursday, the drying closet and the ironing room at record 34
Friday, the linen counted back against the book and carried up the back stair to the press in record 23; and in a four-times-a-year house, a three-day event that takes over the whole service court
