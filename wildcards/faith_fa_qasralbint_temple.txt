Qasr al-Bint seen from its own walled court, a broad flight of white marble steps climbing to a porch of four great columns between the ends of the side walls, the walls coated in molded plaster painted in panels of red, yellow and cream, a band of carved rosettes and busts high up, and in the paved court in front a square altar platform as tall as two men, faced with marble and climbed by its own steps
the Temple of the Winged Lions on the slope across the stream from Qasr al-Bint at Petra, a porch of columns leading into a square hall crowded with columns whose capitals are carved with crouching winged lions, a raised altar platform in the middle, the walls bright with painted plaster and niches holding flat stone slabs carved with two square eyes and a long straight nose
the temple of Khirbet et-Tannur, alone on a bare mountaintop above a deep canyon in central Jordan, a small square shrine within a paved court ringed by colonnades, its front carved with vines, leaves and fruit around the bust of a goddess with leaves on her cheeks and grain in her hair, and a carved ring of the twelve star figures around another goddess crowned with a tower
the temple of Allat at Wadi Rum in the far south of Jordan, a low square building on the sand directly below towering walls of red and tan sandstone, its ring of stout columns plastered and painted in bands of red, blue and yellow, a spring-fed channel running down from the cliff to a stone basin beside it
