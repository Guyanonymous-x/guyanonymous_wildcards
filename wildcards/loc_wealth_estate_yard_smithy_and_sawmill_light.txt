Three distinct conditions in one yard, and the smithy's is the most dramatic working light in the document, the smithy deliberately dim, with one small window and a dark interior, because a smith judges temperature by color and cannot do it in bright light, which lights the hood, the anvil and the man from below and is the reason every painting of a smithy looks the same
The carpenter's shop the opposite, exactly as at record 51
The sawmill lit by open-sided bays and full of airborne sawdust, so that any beam of sun through it is solid and visible and the whole interior is hazy
The yard in flat daylight; and at night, one lantern, which is all
