Temple A at Pyrgi, the harbor sanctuary of the city of Caere on the flat seashore north of Rome, ships drawn up on the beach behind it, its rear gable filled with a painted clay relief of struggling warriors, one biting into the head of a fallen enemy while a bearded god raises a thunderbolt above, three thin sheets of gold nailed beside the door of the smaller temple next to it, each covered with rows of fine scratched marks, and along the south precinct wall a row of some twenty small identical one-door rooms, each about as wide as a man is tall and half again as deep, where women of the sanctuary in their twenties, in thin sleeveless tunics and coral necklaces, sit on the thresholds and receive visiting sailors
